# Wifecraft

An Android scene simulation. Choose a location, pick or create NPCs, and select the objects available in the room.

1. Open **Choose Scene** and pick the Stormwind Embassy.
2. Select up to six NPCs. **Create NPC** saves a character to your roster. NPCs from older projects also appear in the roster.
3. Enable interactions for objects already in the scene. Stormwind Embassy uses its existing study table and books.
4. Begin the scene. NPCs wander until you give them an order (with **Partner** on, two may also start a partner animation together: Sims 4 animations, below). The scene fills the screen with its HUD over it, in the menus' look: a top bar (the place, the clock, and the gear for the scene menu: Resume, Options where offered, **End scene**), the cast as party frames (down the left on a wide screen, a strip under the top bar on the cover screen standing; the NPC you control lit gold, their partner in rose), a line of help, and an action bar. Tap an NPC (in the room or their frame) to control them; tap another NPC to make them the partner (the **Partner: name ×** chip clears it); tap the frame again, **Release** or empty floor to let go. The action bar shows six orders at a time (two rows of three on the cover screen standing), with a page button at its end for the rest: **Walk** (the camera follows the NPC; tap the floor to send them there, drag to orbit, pinch or use the zoom buttons), **Play animation**, **Partner animation** (with a partner), **Say…**, **Chat with…**, **Stop repeat** and **Clear queue** while there is one, **Release**, then the scene's objects (**Sit in chair**, **Read a book**...) and **Free will**. Tapping an object in the room still uses it.

The Stormwind Embassy uses the existing wago.tools location pack. The title and scene setup use the original Wifecraft menu backdrop. The title emblem and first-launch download art also come from the earlier Wifecraft builds. Warcraft controls and selector icons load from wago.tools. The character, furniture, and book models load through the app's existing wago.tools pipeline.

## Places: the Embassy and the bedrooms of Azeroth

**Choose Scene** lists eleven places. Each is one of the game's own buildings, loaded from its files as the game stands it in the world: the building, the props it is furnished with, the map's props and ground around it, and its music and ambience. None is made up or modelled for the app. Each is fetched when first chosen (10 to 30 MB a place), and the preview shows it once fetched.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stormwind Embassy | human | `7hu_garrison_barracks_v2_embassy.wmo`, Stormwind | real |

| Thunderbrew Distillery, Kharanos | dwarf | `snow_inn.wmo`, the guest beds in the cellar | real |
| Dolanaar Inn, Teldrassil | night elf | `nightelfinn.wmo`, the loft's beds | real |
| Razor Hill Inn, Durotar | orc | `orcinn.wmo`, its beds among the sleeping mats | real |

| Fairbreeze Village Inn, Eversong | blood elf | `bloodelf_twostory01.wmo`, the upstairs bed and a chair | real |
| A House in Brill, Tirisfal | Forsaken | `nd_forsaken_house.wmo`, a bed among the coffins | real |

Two are composed, where no troll or draenei building the game stands has a bed: the Sen'jin hut stands where the game stands it but is furnished with the same building's other furnishing set from the game (its bunk-hammocks and sleeping mats) instead of the one Sen'jin uses; the Telaar hut is the game's own, as it stands and is furnished in Telaar, with one bed added: a draenei bed of Draenor (`6dr_draenei_bed01`, the hut's own ancient draenei style) on its rug. Nothing else in either was added or moved.

Each bedroom's **Rest on bed** lies on its bed (on the mattress, along its length), walked to from the floor beside it; the Lion's Pride also has **Sit in chair** and **Read a book** at its desk, the Fairbreeze inn a chair. Only these are interactive: the rest of each room is scenery. The places are worked out by `tools/locations.py` (the building's placement on the map, its furniture by index, the camera and spots measured against its floors and walls) into `assets/locations.json`: file ids, positions and sizes only, never the game's files. Every place was drawn on the desktop (`engine-test`, inside and outside views) and its camera picked by looking; nothing has been seen on a phone, and the lying pose on each bed (which end is the head) is worked out from the bed's model, not watched with an NPC on it.

## Transmog and offline downloads

In the character creator, **Transmog** opens the game's own Transmogrify window (Midnight's, laid out from Blizzard_Transmog's XML and drawn with its atlas art, fetched from wago.tools by id the first time it opens, about 9 MB; `assets/transmog_art.json`, made by `tools/transmog_art.py`). It fills the screen as the game fits it, its middle column over the character, who still turns with a drag and comes nearer with a pinch there. Left: **Clear Current Transmogrifications** (every slot empty), the NPC's outfit card and **Save Outfit**. Middle: her slot buttons, Head, Shoulders, Back, Chest, Tabard, Shirt and Wrist down the left, Hands, Waist, Legs and Feet down the right (tap one to choose it), **Hide Ignored Slots**, and the undo arrow while something is unsaved. Right: **Items** (the chosen slot's appearances, 20 to a page, with **Ignore Slot** to leave the slot empty and **Show Equipped Gear** to put back what she wore), **Sets** (the game's item sets, 9 to a page) and **Custom Sets** (the named characters' outfits: Chromie's also sets her look when she is a gnome woman), with search, a Filter by expansion and page arrows. Hold a card or slot for its tooltip. A pick shows on her at once and glows purple until **Save Outfit**; closing the window (its X or Back) puts back what was saved; with a mouse or stylus, resting on an item card for a moment shows it on her until the pointer leaves. Each Items card shows her (her race, sex, face, hair and skin) wearing only that piece, framed for its slot as the game frames them (head and shoulders close, chest, shirt and tabard at the torso, hands and wrists with the arms, the belt at the waist, legs and feet low, the back from behind), and each Sets card shows all of her in the set: drawn off-screen by the 3D view, one at a time between frames, for the page on show only (a new page, slot or tab drops what was still waiting), the piece's icon until its picture is ready; the pictures are kept in memory and as PNGs in `files/thumbs/transmog/` (48 MB at most, the least recently used let go). Belt buckles and the cloth of hooded helms are drawn on her, and the Back slot lists cloaks (it listed bows before). New NPCs start with Warcraft recruit clothing; the creator's Gear switch still turns all clothing off. Clothing and empty slots are saved with the NPC and appear when the NPC joins a scene. Not as in the game: the cards are still pictures (the game's turn), they don't show Wrath++'s body options, there are no weapon, illusion or second-shoulder slots, no Situations tab, no outfit prices or new outfit slots, no New Custom Set, and the window uses the app's interface sounds. The layout and its rules are unit-tested (`TransmogWindowTest`), the cards' framing, cache names and queue too (`TransmogCardsTest`); the first pages of eleven slots and the sets were drawn on the desktop for a human woman, a human man and an orc woman (`TransmogCardsRealTest` writes them, engine-test's `card` command draws them, about 70 ms a card in software); the window is compiled by CI only and has not run on a phone. The preview uses Warcraft's race-selection backdrop models from wago.tools, framed by the camera stored in each model.

In **Options**, choose **Download all** or download scenes, clothes, and Warcraft character animations individually. The download bar shows pack progress, bytes downloaded, and failures. Pause keeps completed files for the next attempt. Sims 4 rigs and clips are imported from your own files.

## Partly undressed named characters (model parts)

Some named characters are drawn by their own game model with the armour built into it (Sylvanas in Shadowlands, Calia, Jaina, Alexstrasza's visage, Alleria, Xal'atath, Valeera), so nothing can be taken off. Their partly undressed look is put together from two models: the character's bare stand-in body (the playable race's model, as in the creator's bare look) and the pieces kept from her own model: her head with its hair, helm or hood, horns and face markings, her pauldrons and, for Calia, the halo behind her. The stand-in's own head and hair are cut away, and its skin is tinted to the colour of her face. Each piece follows its bone (head, shoulders, chest) in every animation, as she is posed standing in the game.

`assets/model_parts.json` lists which character, look and model give which pieces to which stand-in. The pieces are cut from the downloaded model into `files/bodyparts/<set>/` (`Native.buildModelParts`; on the desktop `engine-test/build/modelparts MANIFEST FILES OUT`, drawn by the harness's `modelset`, `skinmatch` commands). Checked in desktop renders of every set: the head, hair and pauldrons sit where the original model has them (her model and the stand-in share the same skeleton), with small defects: a hard line at the neck where the face's colour and the tinted body still differ (most on Calia), the odd sliver of collar or strap at the neck, and floating effect shards that also float on the original (Alleria in Midnight). Alleria's Midnight face glows blue and is not matched. Alexstrasza's Dragonflight model (4569783) has a skeleton of its own (none of the player rig's bones) and is left out; her visage model (4218362) is used. Not yet offered in the character list (the roster's hooks come next), and not run on a phone.

## Sims 4 animations

For WickedWhims, open **Choose objects → Animation library → Import WickedWhims**. Tap **Install** to download the original archive from the author’s itch.io page and add its compatible clips to the **WickedWhims** category.

From **Choose objects**, open **Animation library** and select **Import rig and clips**. Choose a Sims 4 animation package or its ZIP or 7z archive. The importer can use a humanoid skeleton included in the package. For packs without one, choose an adult Sims 4 skeleton from your game installation. The importer reports which clips it can retarget and lets you review them before saving.

Use **Animation library → Browse animations** to preview imported clips before a scene. Once the scene starts, NPCs wander. Select an NPC and choose **Play animation**: a window with category tabs (All, each group, and **Partner**, first while the NPC has a partner), a search and large rows. Pick a row, then **Preview** plays it once at once with the window kept open, and the main button plays, queues or repeats it as the mode row says (**Play now**, **Queue**, **Repeat**, **Queue stages**). For a partner animation, pick another NPC and the app starts the two roles together after they meet. **Repeat** loops the selected animation until you stop it or queue another clip. **Queue** adds a clip after the current order, and **Queue stages** follows the stages linked by the author. Reinstall WickedWhims to refresh the grouping, stage links, and authored cycle counts for clips already in your library.

The imported Sims rig supplies the source bone hierarchy, bind transforms, and animation curves. The retargeter converts mapped tracks to Warcraft's coordinates and bind pose so Warcraft mesh skinning stays intact. Source clip timing is retained. Ordinary wandering and furniture commands use Warcraft animations.

**Partner animations in the library.** Two kinds of two-person animation are offered, one list for both: an author's performances (WickedWhims and similar packs: the roles, offsets, stages and cycle counts from the pack's own tuning, kept on its clips) and partner animations the importer pairs itself (category **Partner**, saved as one entry with both actors' clips). The importer pairs a package's clips that no author's performance already covers (packages of up to 64 clips; larger ones are converted one clip at a time), in this order: from the package's animation tuning (an entry that lists `animation_actors_list` with each actor's clip name, offset and angle), from the clip names (the same name ending `_x`/`_y`, `_a`/`_b`, `_0`/`_1`, `actor0`/`actor1`, `_xx`/`_yy` or `_male`/`_female`), or from two clips with different actor names, the same length and roots that start apart. Such a pair keeps where each actor stands and faces from the pair's shared origin (one horizontal scale for both, so they stay where the clips put them), whether it loops, the furniture it was made for, and the sound names its clips' events give, with their times. Clips that look like half of a pair are listed on the review under **Clips without a partner**: tap one for actor A and one for actor B, then **Pair these two**. **Animation library → Manage library** has a **Partner** tab to rename, recategorize, turn off for scenes or delete them. Limits: only two-actor animations (three or more are reported); clips whose lengths differ by more than a frame are not paired; prop and object clips, hand IK targets and face curves are not converted; sounds are names only (the audio is not in these packages). Real two-actor packages have not been tested: the pairing follows the format's tools and documentation and synthetic test files.

**Partner animations in the scene.** Every two-person animation plays the same way, whether you chose it with **Play animation**, sent an NPC with **Send to partner**, or two NPCs began one by themselves: both walk to their places around a shared spot, the one waiting turns to face the one coming, and both clips start on the same simulation step and play on one clock, cycle after cycle; the animation's sound events play at their times (Partner sounds, below). By themselves: an idle NPC (not on furniture, not carrying out your order) whose cooldown has passed may go to another idle NPC within six yards (the nearest; a random one of equally near) and play a partner animation picked at random from the enabled ones whose place the scene has (the floor, or the couch, bed or chair it was made for); with none that fits, both play one Warcraft emote their models share (wave, cheer, dance, laugh, bow, kiss, applaud or salute) facing each other. A looping one repeats 1 to 4 times or for 6 to 25 seconds; then both step apart and wait 20 to 60 seconds before doing it again. In **Choose objects**, **Partner** sets how often: Off, Sometimes (the default) or Often, and the row under it counts the partner animations available. **Send to partner** (select an NPC, press it, tap another NPC) sends the selected NPC to start one with that NPC. The cast bar shows "With <name>" for both, and the line under the orders names the animation. Your orders to either NPC and **End scene** interrupt it; the other NPC goes back to idle at once. In the live scene (and the placement ghost) every NPC stands as tall as a human of their sex, whatever the race, so the Sims clips and the pairs line up; dwarves (Dark Iron and Earthen too) and gnomes (Mechagnomes too) keep their own size, as the creator shows them, and a gnome stands nearer its partner by its size (pair slots, its steps, the distance it talks from) with its speech bubble at its own head: its hands meet a human partner's across the floor but stay about 0.8 yards lower, since lifting it would leave it in the air (measured on the game's models by `engine-test/humanscale_test`, not seen on a phone). The simulation is unit-tested on the desktop with fake animations (`simulation/PartnerActivityTest`, `PerformanceTest`); the screens are compiled by CI only, and none of it has run on a phone.

## Music, sounds and the menus

The title, Gathering the Files, the scene's setup, the live scene's HUD (top bar, party frames, action bar, speech bubbles, the Play animation window and its lists), the animation library's page, Options and Updates use the current game's look: dark slate panels with a thin gold trim, rounded buttons that glow when pressed (or hovered with a mouse or stylus), a backdrop that breathes slowly with motes rising over it (still when the phone's animations are off). The character creator keeps its own look.

The menus play the title music of The War Within (MUS_110_MAIN_TITLE); a live scene plays its place's own music and ambience from the game's tables (the Stormwind Embassy: its room's ZoneMusic 1929, six tracks one after another with a short gap, and SoundAmbience 569's day or night loop), crossfading between them. The music dips while an emote's voice or a partner sound plays. Buttons, tabs, switches and windows play the game's interface sounds (SoundKitConstants: IG_MAINMENU_OPEN/CLOSE/OPTION, GS_TITLE_OPTION_OK, IG_CHARACTER_INFO_TAB...). Options > Sound has Enable Sound, Master, Music, Ambience, Sound effects and Dialog, as the game's Sound page; everything is silent while Wifecraft is in the background. The files are the game's, by id (`assets/sounds.json`, made by `tools/sounds.py`), fetched from wago.tools as they are first played once the files have been gathered; a file that can't be fetched leaves silence and is tried again later. The decisions (mixer, ducking, crossfade, which music where, the sound ids) are unit-tested (`audio/Soundscape.kt`); the playback is compiled by CI only and nobody has listened to it on a phone.

## Partner sounds

Options > Sound > Partner sounds plays sounds with partner animations from the user's own files. A clip's sound event carries a sound name; put audio files (ogg, wav, mp3, m4a, aac, flac), single or in a zip, through Add sounds, named after those names (the exact name, the name without a `vo_` or `sfx_` prefix, numbered variants `name_01`, `name_02` of which one is picked at random, a file named by the name's 32- or 64-bit FNV-1a hash in hex, or a file called `default` for the rest). A sound with no file is simply silent. The files are kept in `files/partner-sounds`; there is an On box, a volume and Remove all. The game's own EA audio (SNR/SNS) is not decoded yet. The folder (`audio/PartnerSounds`, `SoundNames`) is unit-tested; the playback (`SceneAudio.partnerSound`) and the Options rows are compiled by CI only and have not run on a phone.

## Building

Run `cd android && bash gradlew :app:assembleDebug` with Android SDK 35, NDK 29.0.14206865, and CMake installed. Run the platform-independent tests with `bash android/gradlew -p unit-tests test` from this directory.

## Smoothness

Menus, windows and buttons share one motion kit (`ui/MenuMotion.kt`, `ui/MenuMotionUi.kt`): windows fade and grow in over 160 ms and out over 120 ms, dropdowns take 90 ms, a press shrinks to 0.96 over 100 ms, and all of it is off when the phone's "remove animations" setting is on. Every tap target ignores a second tap within 300 ms. Controls take their size from the layout, never from a picture or font that has not arrived yet. Download progress reaches the screen at most four times a second and only to the parts that show it. Pictures decode on two background threads into a size-limited cache, newest request first, and a request is cancelled when its cell leaves the screen. The live scene runs on a fixed 1/120 s step (same speed at 60 and 120 Hz, no jump after a pause), draws people interpolated between steps, and writes the camera and positions straight to the renderer; the menus see a snapshot at most ten times a second. The camera uses critically damped springs with inertia and soft limits, eases between the room and NPC cameras over 350 ms, and an 8 dp touch slop separates a tap from a drag. A loading cover hides the scene until its first frames are drawn. Skins are composed at most one per frame. Desktop measurements only (Mesa llvmpipe): the first frame after dressing six NPCs went from one 427 ms frame to a worst frame of 94 ms; steady walking runs at p95 20.6 ms. Not run on a phone.
