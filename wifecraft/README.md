# Wifecraft

An Android scene simulation. Choose a location, pick or create NPCs, and select the objects available in the room.

1. Open **Choose Scene** and pick the Stormwind Embassy.
2. Select up to six NPCs. **Create NPC** saves a character to your roster. NPCs from older projects also appear in the roster.
3. Enable interactions for objects already in the scene. Stormwind Embassy uses its existing study table and books.
4. Begin the scene. NPCs go about their day (Life simulation, below; with it off they wander) until you give them an order (with **Partner** on, two may also start a partner animation together: Sims 4 animations, below). The scene fills the screen with its HUD over it, in the menus' look: a top bar (the place, the clock, and the gear for the scene menu: Resume, Options where offered, **End scene**), the cast as party frames (down the left on a wide screen, a strip under the top bar on the cover screen standing; the NPC you control lit gold, their partner in rose), a line of help, and an action bar. Tap an NPC (in the room or their frame) to control them; tap another NPC to make them the partner (the **Partner: name ×** chip clears it); tap the frame again, **Release** or empty floor to let go. The action bar shows six orders at a time (two rows of three on the cover screen standing), with a page button at its end for the rest: **Walk** (the camera follows the NPC; tap the floor to send them there, drag to orbit, pinch or use the zoom buttons), **Play animation**, **Partner animation** (with a partner), **Say…**, **Chat with…**, **Stop repeat** and **Clear queue** while there is one, **Release**, then the scene's objects (**Sit in chair**, **Read a book**...) and **Free will**. Tapping an object in the room opens its **Play animation** window with only the animations that fit on it (Furniture, below); when none does, it uses the object as before.

The Stormwind Embassy uses the existing wago.tools location pack. The title and scene setup use the original Wifecraft menu backdrop. The title emblem and first-launch download art also come from the earlier Wifecraft builds. Warcraft controls and selector icons load from wago.tools. The character, furniture, and book models load through the app's existing wago.tools pipeline.

**Choose Scene** lists eleven places. Each is one of the game's own buildings, loaded from its files as the game stands it in the world: the building, the props it is furnished with, the map's props and ground around it, and its music and ambience. None is made up or modelled for the app. Each is fetched when first chosen (10 to 30 MB a place), and the preview shows it once fetched.

## Transmog and offline downloads

In the character creator, **Transmog** opens the game's own Transmogrify window (Midnight's, laid out from Blizzard_Transmog's XML and drawn with its atlas art, fetched from wago.tools by id the first time it opens, about 9 MB; `assets/transmog_art.json`, made by `tools/transmog_art.py`). It fills the screen as the game fits it, its middle column over the character, who still turns with a drag and comes nearer with a pinch there. Left: **Clear Current Transmogrifications** (every slot empty), the NPC's outfit card and **Save Outfit**. Middle: her slot buttons, Head, Shoulders, Back, Chest, Tabard, Shirt and Wrist down the left, Hands, Waist, Legs and Feet down the right (tap one to choose it), **Hide Ignored Slots**, and the undo arrow while something is unsaved. Right: **Items** (the chosen slot's appearances, 20 to a page, with **Ignore Slot** to leave the slot empty and **Show Equipped Gear** to put back what she wore), **Sets** (the game's item sets, 9 to a page) and **Custom Sets** (the named characters' outfits: Chromie's also sets her look when she is a gnome woman), with search, a Filter by expansion and page arrows. Hold a card or slot for its tooltip. A pick shows on her at once and glows purple until **Save Outfit**; closing the window (its X or Back) puts back what was saved; with a mouse or stylus, resting on an item card for a moment shows it on her until the pointer leaves. Each Items card shows her (her race, sex, face, hair and skin) wearing only that piece, framed for its slot as the game frames them (head and shoulders close, chest, shirt and tabard at the torso, hands and wrists with the arms, the belt at the waist, legs and feet low, the back from behind), and each Sets card shows all of her in the set: drawn off-screen by the 3D view, one at a time between frames, for the page on show only (a new page, slot or tab drops what was still waiting), the piece's icon until its picture is ready; the pictures are kept in memory and as PNGs in `files/thumbs/transmog/` (48 MB at most, the least recently used let go). Belt buckles and the cloth of hooded helms are drawn on her, and the Back slot lists cloaks (it listed bows before). New NPCs start with Warcraft recruit clothing; the creator's Gear switch still turns all clothing off. Clothing and empty slots are saved with the NPC and appear when the NPC joins a scene. Not as in the game: the cards are still pictures (the game's turn), they don't show Wrath++'s body options, there are no weapon, illusion or second-shoulder slots, no Situations tab, no outfit prices or new outfit slots, no New Custom Set, and the window uses the app's interface sounds. The layout and its rules are unit-tested (`TransmogWindowTest`), the cards' framing, cache names and queue too (`TransmogCardsTest`); the first pages of eleven slots and the sets were drawn on the desktop for a human woman, a human man and an orc woman (`TransmogCardsRealTest` writes them, engine-test's `card` command draws them, about 70 ms a card in software); the window is compiled by CI only and has not run on a phone. The preview uses Warcraft's race-selection backdrop models from wago.tools, framed by the camera stored in each model.

In **Options**, choose **Download all** or download scenes, clothes, and Warcraft character animations individually. The download bar shows pack progress, bytes downloaded, and failures. Pause keeps completed files for the next attempt. Sims 4 rigs and clips are imported from your own files.

## Partly undressed named characters (model parts)

Some named characters are drawn by their own game model with the armour built into it (Sylvanas in Shadowlands, Calia, Jaina, Alexstrasza's visage, Alleria, Xal'atath, Valeera), so nothing can be taken off. Their partly undressed look is put together from two models: the character's bare stand-in body (the playable race's model, as in the creator's bare look) and the pieces kept from her own model: her head with its hair, helm or hood, horns and face markings, her pauldrons and, for Calia, the halo behind her. The stand-in's own head and hair are cut away, and its skin is tinted to the colour of her face. Each piece follows its bone (head, shoulders, chest) in every animation, as she is posed standing in the game.

`assets/model_parts.json` lists which character, look and model give which pieces to which stand-in. The pieces are cut from the downloaded model into `files/bodyparts/<set>/` (`Native.buildModelParts`; on the desktop `engine-test/build/modelparts MANIFEST FILES OUT`, drawn by the harness's `modelset`, `skinmatch` commands). Checked in desktop renders of every set: the head, hair and pauldrons sit where the original model has them (her model and the stand-in share the same skeleton), with small defects: a hard line at the neck where the face's colour and the tinted body still differ (most on Calia), the odd sliver of collar or strap at the neck, and floating effect shards that also float on the original (Alleria in Midnight). Alleria's Midnight face glows blue and is not matched. Alexstrasza's Dragonflight model (4569783) has a skeleton of its own (none of the player rig's bones) and is left out; her visage model (4218362) is used. Offered in the NPC picker as each character's "<look>, partial" (Named characters, below); not run on a phone.

## Named characters and your NPCs

The NPC picker lists, after your own NPCs and the two starters (Mira and Rowan), ten named characters: Sylvanas Windrunner, Calia Menethil, Lilian Voss, Jaina Proudmoore, Alexstrasza, Chromie, Valeera Sanguinar, Alleria Windrunner, Sally Whitemane and Xal'atath (`assets/characters.json`, made by `tools/characters.py`). They join a scene like any other NPC. The small round button on a card opens the NPC's menu:

- **Look** (named characters): each of her outfits (the game's own NPC display, or her playable race's body wearing her items), **underwear** (no gear), **bare** (the skin colour's underwear layers left off too), **<outfit>, nude** (her outfit without its shirt, chest, belt, tabard, legs and feet) where she has a body outfit, and **<outfit>, partial** for the eleven looks `assets/model_parts.json` cuts (her stand-in's bare body wearing her own head, pauldrons and back-piece, the stand-in's skin tinted to her face; cut on the phone from her downloaded model the first time she is staged, "Preparing a named character's partial look…"; if it can't be cut she shows her bare look and the scene says why). Looks that can't be drawn are not listed: Alexstrasza has no Dragonflight partial, Chromie has her outfit only. The choice is kept (`files/npc-looks.json`) and saved with a scene's cast (the appearance's `named`). Options' downloads have a **Named characters** pack (every display they are drawn as or cut from).
- **Body parts** (men): Size (Small, Medium, Large), Cut (Uncut, Cut, Retracted) and Scrotum (High, Low); Medium, Uncut, High by default; saved with the NPC (the appearance's `parts`; older saves load as the defaults).
- **Delete NPC** (only NPCs you made: npc-roster.json's): asks first, then takes the NPC out of `files/npc-roster.json` (written whole or not at all), out of the cast being chosen, and its portraits out of `files/thumbs`. The starters, the named characters and the NPCs of older projects have no Delete.

Cards of a named character in one of her own displays show that display's picture; her body looks show her body (the bare ones as in underwear: the portrait doesn't leave the underwear layers off).

**Body parts add-on.** Options → Mods → **Body parts**: **Choose file** (a `.package` or a `.zip` of them) or **Choose folder** imports your own Sims 4 body-part mod, never part of the app: its male parts are read and written as part files (`bodyparts/PartPackage`) into `files/bodyparts/<set>/` on a background thread, the row saying how far it is; **Remove** deletes them. A man with nothing worn below the waist (No Gear, or clothing without chest and legs) then wears the DEFAULT part for his settings, its texture tinted to his skin (sampled from his skin texture where the engine says the part meets the body); the scene's On / Off switch shows the ON part. Which part: the one whose variant is his settings' ("Medium Uncut High"); where the set names two, the one whose mesh isn't another variant's under the wrong name (the user's set has a second DEFAULT "Medium Uncut High", A45EFB6003C7A1EB, with exactly Medium Cut High's 986 vertices and 1722 triangles: E853F196527D7CF4 and, for ON, BC041B5233235A43 are used); a variant the set lacks takes the nearest (size first). Tested on the desktop: the variant lists from the real assets, the roster file's add, change and delete, the cast and thumbnail clean-up, the part choice with the user's set's labels and the switch (`NamedCastTest`, `NpcRosterFileTest`, `PartChoiceTest`); the menus, the import, the drawing of the looks, the cut on the phone and the skin tints are compiled by CI only and have not run on a phone.

## Sims 4 animations

For WickedWhims, open **Choose objects → Animation library → Import WickedWhims**. Tap **Install** to download the original archive from the author’s itch.io page and add its compatible clips to the **WickedWhims** category.

From **Choose objects**, open **Animation library** and select **Import rig and clips**. Choose a Sims 4 animation package or its ZIP or 7z archive. The importer can use a humanoid skeleton included in the package. For packs without one, choose an adult Sims 4 skeleton from your game installation. The importer reports which clips it can retarget and lets you review them before saving.

Use **Animation library → Browse animations** to preview imported clips before a scene. Once the scene starts, NPCs wander. Select an NPC and choose **Play animation**: a window with category tabs (All, each group, and **Partner**, first while the NPC has a partner), a search and large rows. Pick a row, then **Preview** plays it once at once with the window kept open, and the main button plays, queues or repeats it as the mode row says (**Play now**, **Queue**, **Repeat**, **Queue stages**). For a partner animation, pick another NPC and the app starts the two roles together after they meet. **Repeat** loops the selected animation until you stop it or queue another clip. **Queue** adds a clip after the current order, and **Queue stages** follows the stages linked by the author. Reinstall WickedWhims to refresh the grouping, stage links, and authored cycle counts for clips already in your library.

The imported Sims rig supplies the source bone hierarchy, bind transforms, and animation curves. The retargeter converts mapped tracks to Warcraft's coordinates and bind pose so Warcraft mesh skinning stays intact. Source clip timing is retained. Ordinary wandering and furniture commands use Warcraft animations.

**Partner animations in the library.** Two kinds of two-person animation are offered, one list for both: an author's performances (WickedWhims and similar packs: the roles, offsets, stages and cycle counts from the pack's own tuning, kept on its clips) and partner animations the importer pairs itself (category **Partner**, saved as one entry with both actors' clips). The importer pairs a package's clips that no author's performance already covers (packages of up to 64 clips; larger ones are converted one clip at a time), in this order: from the package's animation tuning (an entry that lists `animation_actors_list` with each actor's clip name, offset and angle), from the clip names (the same name ending `_x`/`_y`, `_a`/`_b`, `_0`/`_1`, `actor0`/`actor1`, `_xx`/`_yy` or `_male`/`_female`), or from two clips with different actor names, the same length and roots that start apart. Such a pair keeps where each actor stands and faces from the pair's shared origin (one horizontal scale for both, so they stay where the clips put them), whether it loops, the furniture it was made for, and the sound names its clips' events give, with their times. Clips that look like half of a pair are listed on the review under **Clips without a partner**: tap one for actor A and one for actor B, then **Pair these two**. **Animation library → Manage library** has a **Partner** tab to rename, recategorize, turn off for scenes or delete them. Limits: only two-actor animations (three or more are reported); clips whose lengths differ by more than a frame are not paired; prop and object clips, hand IK targets and face curves are not converted; sounds are names only (the audio is not in these packages). Real two-actor packages have not been tested: the pairing follows the format's tools and documentation and synthetic test files.

**Partner animations in the scene.** Every two-person animation plays the same way, whether you chose it with **Play animation**, sent an NPC with **Send to partner**, or two NPCs began one by themselves: both walk to their places around a shared spot, the one waiting turns to face the one coming, and both clips start on the same simulation step and play on one clock, cycle after cycle; the animation's sound events play at their times (Partner sounds, below). By themselves: an idle NPC (not on furniture, not carrying out your order) whose cooldown has passed may go to another idle NPC within six yards (the nearest; a random one of equally near) and play a partner animation picked at random from the enabled ones that fit somewhere near them (on the floor, or on a free bed, couch, chair or table within ten yards where both bodies land: Furniture, below); with none that fits, both play one Warcraft emote their models share (wave, cheer, dance, laugh, bow, kiss, applaud or salute) facing each other. A looping one repeats 1 to 4 times or for 6 to 25 seconds; then both step apart and wait 20 to 60 seconds before doing it again. In **Choose objects**, **Partner** sets how often: Off, Sometimes (the default) or Often, and the row under it counts the partner animations available. **Send to partner** (select an NPC, press it, tap another NPC) sends the selected NPC to start one with that NPC. The cast bar shows "With <name>" for both, and the line under the orders names the animation. Your orders to either NPC and **End scene** interrupt it; the other NPC goes back to idle at once. In the live scene (and the placement ghost) every NPC stands as tall as a human of their sex, whatever the race, so the Sims clips and the pairs line up; dwarves (Dark Iron and Earthen too) and gnomes (Mechagnomes too) keep their own size, as the creator shows them, and a gnome stands nearer its partner by its size (pair slots, its steps, the distance it talks from) with its speech bubble at its own head: its hands meet a human partner's across the floor but stay about 0.8 yards lower, since lifting it would leave it in the air (measured on the game's models by `engine-test/humanscale_test`, not seen on a phone). The simulation is unit-tested on the desktop with fake animations (`simulation/PartnerActivityTest`, `PerformanceTest`); the screens are compiled by CI only, and none of it has run on a phone.

**Furniture.** Each animation is read for what it needs (`anim/Requirements`): from its bodies over the clip (the pelvis's height, the torso's tilt, which side faces up, the thighs and knees, where the feet and hands are) and its names and tuning (bed, sofa, chair, counter; lie, sit, kneel), whether each actor stands, sits, lies, kneels, leans or is on all fours, on the floor or on a bed, a seat or a top, and at what height. The result is kept beside the library (`files/anims/requires.cache`, made again when an animation is converted again; the importer reads it with the Sims clip's own names and slot targets). The scene's objects are furniture with places to sit, lie, kneel and lean (`simulation/Furniture`): each bedroom's bed, the Lion's Pride's chair and desk, the Fairbreeze chair and the Embassy's study table, measured in `assets/locations.json` ("furniture": where each stands, its front, its bed's, seat's or top's height). An animation plays on a piece only where every actor lands on something that takes their posture at their height (`FurnitureFit`): a lying one on a bed, a sitting one on a chair, a couch or a bed's edge, a leaning or sitting-up-on-a-top one at a table, a floor one on the floor or lifted onto a bed; one that needs a surface the scene doesn't have nearby is never offered or picked. NPCs picking by themselves choose among the ways to play that fit (the floor, any free furniture in reach), and on furniture each takes the role whose place is nearer. Tapping a piece with an NPC controlled opens **Play animation** with only what fits on it, in tabs by posture (Lying, Sitting, Kneeling...); a two-person one plays with the partner, or the nearest NPC who can join. While the controlled NPC is on furniture the camera button goes on past Room, Orbit and Follow to the furniture's views (a bed: Foot, Head, Left side, Right side, From above; a chair or couch: Front, the sides, Over the shoulder, Three-quarter; a table: Front, the sides, Three-quarter), framed on the people on it and kept inside the room; a drag orbits from the view. A body's size is a human's, at human scale (a dwarf's or gnome's own: a gnome's feet hang off a chair made for a human). Animations read from their names only (no body to read) play as before, by the furniture their names give. Limits, said plainly: the reading is a heuristic checked on synthetic bodies and one sitting pack, never on real WickedWhims packs; a head-to-feet pair at human scale is longer than a bed and plays on the floor; every bedroom bed is taken as a double; chairs are taken to face their model's +x and their seats as 0.47 m, and the tables' tops as high as their books stand (about 1.1 yards); the views' walls are checked by a walk along the floor and are offered anyway when none passes. Tested on the desktop with synthetic bodies (`FurnitureTest`, `FurnitureSceneTest`, `FurnitureCamerasTest`, `RequirementsTest`); the window, the camera button and the drawing are compiled by CI only; nothing of it has run on a phone.

## Music, sounds and the menus

The title, Gathering the Files, the scene's setup, the live scene's HUD (top bar, party frames, action bar, speech bubbles, the Play animation window and its lists), the animation library's page, Options and Updates use the current game's look: dark slate panels with a thin gold trim, rounded buttons that glow when pressed (or hovered with a mouse or stylus), a backdrop that breathes slowly with motes rising over it (still when the phone's animations are off). The character creator keeps its own look.

The menus play the title music of The War Within (MUS_110_MAIN_TITLE); a live scene plays its place's own music and ambience from the game's tables (the Stormwind Embassy: its room's ZoneMusic 1929, six tracks one after another with a short gap, and SoundAmbience 569's day or night loop), crossfading between them. The music dips while an emote's voice or a partner sound plays. Buttons, tabs, switches and windows play the game's interface sounds (SoundKitConstants: IG_MAINMENU_OPEN/CLOSE/OPTION, GS_TITLE_OPTION_OK, IG_CHARACTER_INFO_TAB...). Options > Sound has Enable Sound, Master, Music, Ambience, Sound effects and Dialog, as the game's Sound page; everything is silent while Wifecraft is in the background. The files are the game's, by id (`assets/sounds.json`, made by `tools/sounds.py`), fetched from wago.tools as they are first played once the files have been gathered; a file that can't be fetched leaves silence and is tried again later. The decisions (mixer, ducking, crossfade, which music where, the sound ids) are unit-tested (`audio/Soundscape.kt`); the playback is compiled by CI only and nobody has listened to it on a phone.

## Time of day

A live scene has a world clock (`simulation/WorldClock.kt`): a real second is a game minute at x1, so a day takes 24 real minutes (8 at x3, 4 at x6; Paused stands still), counted in days from Day 1, its hours named Midnight, Night, Dawn, Morning, Noon, Afternoon, Dusk and Evening. A scene starts in the morning (8:00) unless another start hour is chosen; the clock is saved and put back as JSON, and the life simulation follows it (`LifeTime`). Its settings (speed, start hour, and **Day/night cycle**: off keeps the start hour's light and sound while the clock still runs) are `ClockSettings`, on Options' **Time** page (`Settings.clock`): the speed there is where each scene starts, the start hour is where a new cast's first day begins (a cast played before goes on from its own saved time), and the cycle applies at once. The live screen drives the clock every frame on the GL thread, hands the hour to the light (`LightFeed`, `SceneView.setTimeOfDay`) and, four times a second, to the sound (`Audio.timeOfDay`); the life simulation follows it (`LifeControl.setTimeSource`), and its speed buttons go through life to it (`LifeSim.onSpeed`: Pause, x1, x3, x6 are `LifeSpeed` PAUSE, NORMAL, FAST, FASTEST, the same multipliers: `LifeHud.lifeSpeed`/`clockSpeed`).

The light follows the hour (`SceneView.setTimeOfDay`, cpp `engine/daylight.cpp`) with the game's own day for each place: its map light's LightData keyframes (`dayCycle` in `assets/locations.json`, from `tools/locations.py CACHE --day-cycle`), sampled between keyframes and round midnight with no jump: an orange dawn at 6, the day's light, a golden and then violet dusk from 17, a blue moonlit night from 20:30. The sun crosses from the east at 6 through the south at noon to the west at 18; by night the light comes from the moon in the north. At noon the colours are those the scene had before (only the sun's direction is the hour's). Inside, a room keeps its own baked light (the game bakes lamps and windows into its vertex colours): by night it is scaled to about two thirds and warmed, which reads as lamplight but can't light a lamp or darken a window by itself; what is seen through its doors and windows (the outside's light, sky and fog) changes with the hour. Lanterns' and windows' glowing textures stay lit at night. Checked in desktop renders of the Embassy outside and inside and the Lion's Pride bedroom at 6, 9, 12, 17, 19, 22 and 2 o'clock (`engine-test`: `time HOURS`; `build/daylight_test` checks the cycles); not seen on a phone.

The sound follows it too (`audio/TimeOfDaySound.kt`, played by `Audio.timeOfDay`): from 20:00 to 5:30 the Embassy's ambience crosses over eight seconds to its night loop (SoundAmbience 569's night kit; its day kit by day) while its music plays on, a little quieter by night (70 %, eased over dusk and dawn); now and then (every 25 to 70 seconds) one of the place's ambience flavour sounds plays (SoundAmbienceFlavor: for the Embassy the high winds of kit 130139), and Stormwind's bell (kit 6594, belltollalliance.ogg) tolls the hour at 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 and 21 (as many strokes as fit before the next). The bedrooms have no sound entry yet, so no day and night sound either; nothing is made up where the tables have none (there are no rooster or cricket kits for these places). Not heard on a phone.

## Life simulation

With **Life simulation** on (Options > Time; on by default: `Settings.lifeSimulation`; turned off in a running scene, needs stand still and NPCs choose as before), the live scene's NPCs live as in The Sims, in Warcraft's words (`simulation/LifeSim.kt` and its `Life*.kt` files; the screen's side is pinned in `simulation/LifeApi.kt`). It drives the same runtime as everything else (walking, sitting, resting on the bed, reading, talking, partner animations, emotes); it adds no second one.

- **Needs.** Seven bars from empty to full: Rested (energy), Well Fed (hunger), Refreshed (thirst), Company (social), Amused (fun), Clean (hygiene) and At Ease (comfort). They fall with game time (`LifeClock`, or the world clock when it drives it: a game minute a real second at x1, so a day is 24 real minutes; x3, x6 and Pause): about 19 waking hours to tire out, 12 to go hungry, 9 to thirst, so a day holds a night's sleep, about three meals and some company. What an NPC does fills them: sleeping in the bed (or a night on the couch when the bed is taken), napping on the couch, sitting, eating or drinking at the table (an inn meal: the game's Eat emote, bite after bite) or from the pack standing, reading at the study table, dancing, freshening up, stretching, chatting. A fireplace, a basin or a bookshelf is used when a location names one (none does yet: no washing here, only freshening up).
- **Traits.** Three each, the same every time for the same NPC (from their id, race and sex): Early Bird, Night Owl, Social, Loner, Hearty Appetite, Ascetic, Romantic, Playful, Bookish, Brave, Mischievous, Calm. Some lean lightly by race (dwarves love their food and ale, night elves keep the night, tauren and draenei are calm, gnomes read, goblins scheme, trolls play); sex never decides one. They change how fast needs fall, what an NPC likes doing and talking about, when they sleep (22 to 6; an early bird 21 to 5, a night owl 1 to 9) and who they get on with.
- **Moods and thoughts.** A mood from the needs and from what happened lately (Well Rested, Inspired by a Book, Shared a Laugh, Had an Argument...), a mood score, a thought bubble now and then ("I could use a drink.", "Is it nap time?", "Not a free bed in sight.") and a wish ("wants a hot meal", "wants a friend", "wants to hear a story").
- **Choosing.** When an NPC is free (not under your order), everything that can happen now is scored: how urgent the need is, what the thing gives, the traits, the distance, the hour, a little chance, and less of what they did lately; the best wins. What it's for is cut short when the need is full or another becomes urgent (never your orders). What can't be had (a bed when it's taken) is wanted aloud in a thought, not faked.
- **Relationships.** Each pair has a friendship in the game's reputation standings (Hated, Hostile, Unfriendly, Neutral, Friendly, Honored, Revered, Exalted) and a romance (Spurned, Stranger, Acquaintance, Flirting, Sweethearts, Romantic, Soulbound). Chats (their topic suiting both or not, and both moods), jokes, compliments, flirting, gifts, hugs, dances and time sitting together raise them; arguments and refusals lower them; apart, they drift back slowly. By themselves NPCs hug and dance from Friendly, kiss from Sweethearts and play the library's other (intimate) pairs from Romantic; the one asked may refuse (a line in a bubble and a small cost). **Play animation** and **Send to partner** are never held back. The Chatter and Partner settings still apply: Off means NPCs don't start those by themselves.
- **The feed.** What happened ("Jaina went to bed.", "Thrall and Valeera became friends.") in a list of the last forty.
- **Your interactions.** From an NPC: Rest, Eat, Drink, Read, Dance, Wash, Wander; to another: Chat, Tell a joke, Compliment, Hug, Flirt, Kiss, Argue, Invite to sit, Dance together, Give a gift. They are your orders; the other accepts or refuses and the answer is shown. Each NPC's needs can be frozen.
- **Saving.** The whole life (clock, needs, traits, moods, relationships, the feed) saves as JSON per cast (`files/life/` + `LifeSim.fileName`, the world clock beside it as `clock_<same>.json`, each written whole or not at all) every half minute and when the scene ends, and comes back with the scene.
- **On screen** (`ui/LifeHud.kt`, pure and unit-tested; `ui/LifeOverlay.kt`, `ui/LifePanels.kt`). Over each NPC's head a mood gem, WoW's quest-marker diamond in the mood's colour (green happy, yellow content, orange a need, red irritable, lonely or sad, blue bored or inspired), glowing and bobbing gently (still with the phone's animations off), hidden while they speak. Now and then a thought cloud (pale, with puffs trailing to the head: never mistaken for the dark speech bubbles) with the game's own icon for it (a mug, bread, sleep, a book, a drum, a valentine, the sun and moon, water...: `ui/LifeIcons.kt`, item and spell icons by their file ids from the listfile, a drawn glyph until they're fetched or if they can't be): at most three at once, one per NPC, each up a few seconds, a rest after it, never the same words twice running, the controlled NPC's first. Each party frame rings the NPC's plate with their seven needs (red to green) and carries their gem (dimmer while they're off screen) and, beside the controlled NPC, a heart (Flirting or more), linked rings (Friendly or more) or crossed blades (below Neutral); its line reads "Happy · Reading". A tap on the plate opens their **Life** window (Needs: mood, wish, what coloured it lately, seven reputation-style bars, a long press on one says what fills it, Freeze needs; Relationships: each friendship as the game's reputation bar in its standing's colour and the romance on a rose bar, closest first; Traits). **Hold** an NPC (or its frame) for the radial menu: your own Rest, Eat, Drink, Read, Dance, Wash, Wander, or, with someone controlled, Chat, Tell a joke, Compliment, Hug, Flirt, Kiss, Argue, Invite to sit, Dance together, Give a gift to the one held; hold a piece of furniture for what it gives (and its Play animation window). The action bar's **Actions** opens it too (with the partner when there is one). What can't be had is greyed and says why when tapped; a request that may well be refused is marked. The answer comes as a short toast (green tick or red cross). The top bar shows the world clock (sun or moon, the time, "Day 2 · Morning"); a tap gives Pause, x1, x3, x6 for this scene. The book beside the gear opens the last twenty events, a gold dot when something new happened. The scene menu has the controlled NPC's life and Freeze. Taps that controlled an NPC or chose the partner do exactly as before; the numbers refresh four times a second.

Tested on the desktop only (`unit-tests/.../simulation/LifeSimTest`): each need sends an NPC to what fills it, traits change the rates, friends are made by chatting and gate what pairs are picked, refusals cost, saving round-trips, the same seed lives the same life, and three game days of six NPCs in a room with one bed, one couch, a chair and a table (at x3) pass with nobody in one waking state over about 90 real seconds and everyone sleeping every night (in turns: some end the night tired), about 6 microseconds a simulation step for all six and at most one decision per NPC every 1.25 seconds. The screen's logic (gem colours, bar colours and words, reputation bars, the relation marks, the thought throttle, the radial menu's angles, spacing and hit test, the clock's face and speeds, every icon key having an icon) is unit-tested (`unit-tests/.../ui/LifeHudTest`) and the screens were type-checked against Compose Desktop; nothing of it has been compiled for Android here (CI builds it), the tuning has not been played by a person, and none of it has run on a phone.

## Partner sounds

Options > Sound > Partner sounds plays sounds with partner animations from the user's own files. A clip's sound event carries a sound name; put audio files (ogg, wav, mp3, m4a, aac, flac), single or in a zip, through Add sounds, named after those names (the exact name, the name without a `vo_` or `sfx_` prefix, numbered variants `name_01`, `name_02` of which one is picked at random, a file named by the name's 32- or 64-bit FNV-1a hash in hex, or a file called `default` for the rest). A sound with no file is simply silent. The files are kept in `files/partner-sounds`; there is an On box, a volume and Remove all. The game's own EA audio (SNR/SNS) is not decoded yet. The folder (`audio/PartnerSounds`, `SoundNames`) is unit-tested; the playback (`SceneAudio.partnerSound`) and the Options rows are compiled by CI only and have not run on a phone.

## Building

Run `cd android && bash gradlew :app:assembleDebug` with Android SDK 35, NDK 29.0.14206865, and CMake installed. Run the platform-independent tests with `bash android/gradlew -p unit-tests test` from this directory.

## Smoothness

Menus, windows and buttons share one motion kit (`ui/MenuMotion.kt`, `ui/MenuMotionUi.kt`): windows fade and grow in over 160 ms and out over 120 ms, dropdowns take 90 ms, a press shrinks to 0.96 over 100 ms, and all of it is off when the phone's "remove animations" setting is on. Every tap target ignores a second tap within 300 ms. Controls take their size from the layout, never from a picture or font that has not arrived yet. Download progress reaches the screen at most four times a second and only to the parts that show it. Pictures decode on two background threads into a size-limited cache, newest request first, and a request is cancelled when its cell leaves the screen. The live scene runs on a fixed 1/120 s step (same speed at 60 and 120 Hz, no jump after a pause), draws people interpolated between steps, and writes the camera and positions straight to the renderer; the menus see a snapshot at most ten times a second. The camera uses critically damped springs with inertia and soft limits, eases between the room and NPC cameras over 350 ms, and an 8 dp touch slop separates a tap from a drag. A loading cover hides the scene until its first frames are drawn. Skins are composed at most one per frame. Desktop measurements only (Mesa llvmpipe): the first frame after dressing six NPCs went from one 427 ms frame to a worst frame of 94 ms; steady walking runs at p95 20.6 ms. Not run on a phone.
