# Wifecraft

An Android scene simulation. Choose a location, pick or create NPCs, and select the objects available in the room.

1. Open **Choose Scene** and pick the Stormwind Embassy.
2. Select up to six NPCs. **Create NPC** saves a character to your roster. NPCs from older projects also appear in the roster.
3. Enable interactions for objects already in the scene. Stormwind Embassy uses its existing study table and books.
4. Begin the scene. NPCs go about their day (Life simulation, below; with it off they wander) until you give them an order (with **Partner** on, two may also start a partner animation together: Sims 4 animations, below). The scene fills the screen with its HUD over it, in the menus' look: a top bar (the place, the clock, and the gear for the scene menu: Resume, Options where offered, **End scene**), the cast as party frames (down the left on a wide screen, a strip under the top bar on the cover screen standing; the NPC you control lit gold, their partner in rose), a line of help, and an action bar. Tap an NPC (in the room or their frame) to control them; tap another NPC to make them the partner (the **Partner: name ×** chip clears it); tap the frame again, **Release** or empty floor to let go. The action bar shows six orders at a time (two rows of three on the cover screen standing), with a page button at its end for the rest: **Walk** (the camera follows the NPC; tap the floor to send them there, drag to orbit, pinch or use the zoom buttons), **Play animation**, **Partner animation** (with a partner), **Say…**, **Chat with…**, **Stop** while an animation plays, **Stop repeat**, **Skip** and **Clear queue** while there is one, **Release**, then the scene's objects (**Sit in chair**, **Read a book**...) and **Free will**. Tapping an object in the room opens its **Play animation** window with only the animations that fit on it (Furniture, below); when none does, it uses the object as before. With an NPC controlled, the **Move** chip puts them on a tapped item and holds them there to be nudged and reported (Move mode and item reports, below).

The Stormwind Embassy uses the existing wago.tools location pack. The title and scene setup use the original Wifecraft menu backdrop. The title emblem and first-launch download art also come from the earlier Wifecraft builds. Warcraft controls and selector icons load from wago.tools. The character, furniture, and book models load through the app's existing wago.tools pipeline.

## Places: the Embassy and the bedrooms of Azeroth

**Choose Scene** lists eleven places. Each is one of the game's own buildings, loaded from its files as the game stands it in the world: the building, the props it is furnished with, the map's props and ground around it, and its music and ambience. None is made up or modelled for the app. Each is fetched when first chosen (10 to 30 MB a place), and the preview shows it once fetched.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stormwind Embassy | human | `7hu_garrison_barracks_v2_embassy.wmo`, Stormwind | real |

| Thunderbrew Distillery, Kharanos | dwarf | `snow_inn.wmo`, the guest beds in the cellar | real |
| Dolanaar Inn, Teldrassil | night elf | `nightelfinn.wmo`, the loft's beds | real |
| Razor Hill Inn, Durotar | orc | `orcinn.wmo`, its beds among the sleeping mats | real |

Two are composed, where no troll or draenei building the game stands has a bed: the Sen'jin hut stands where the game stands it but is furnished with the same building's other furnishing set from the game (its bunk-hammocks and sleeping mats) instead of the one Sen'jin uses; the Telaar hut is the game's own, as it stands and is furnished in Telaar, with one bed added: a draenei bed of Draenor (`6dr_draenei_bed01`, the hut's own ancient draenei style) on its rug. Nothing else in either was added or moved.

Each bedroom's **Rest on bed** lies on its bed (on the mattress, along its length), walked to from the floor beside it; the Lion's Pride also has **Sit in chair** and **Read a book** at its desk, the Fairbreeze inn a chair (each chair's sitter gets up onto the floor beside it, not into the seat). In a room whose view stands far from its people (the Embassy's, across the hall from the doorway), the room camera moves in along its own line of sight until the cast fits the picture, no nearer than 3.2 yards and no farther than the floor is clear, and glides after them as they move about (`CastFraming`). Only these are interactive: the rest of each room is scenery. The places are worked out by `tools/locations.py` (the building's placement on the map, its furniture by index, the camera and spots measured against its floors and walls) into `assets/locations.json`: file ids, positions and sizes only, never the game's files. Every place was drawn on the desktop (`engine-test`, inside and outside views) and its camera picked by looking; nothing has been seen on a phone, and the lying pose on each bed (which end is the head) is worked out from the bed's model, not watched with an NPC on it.

## The character creator (Create a Character)

**Create NPC** opens the creator as a Create-a-Sim style screen in the Warcraft look (slate panels with a gold trim, the game's own creator pictures cut on the phone, Friz Quadrata). The tabs run down the left side (across under her on a tall screen): **Race & Sex**, **Body**, **Face**, **Hair**, **Makeup & Details**, **Outfit**, **Accessories** and **Personality**. She stands in the middle of the space the panels leave; a drag turns her, a pinch brings her nearer, and the camera buttons go to **Full** body, **Upper** body, **Face** or **Legs**, with **Turn** (a slow turntable) and **Light** (a warm evening light). **Tap a part of her** to open its tab: the engine finds the triangle under your finger on her body as posed (`engine/bodypick.h`) and the bones it moves with (head, hair, neck, chest, waist, hips, legs, arms, hands, feet), the camera goes to that part and a soft gold ring shows where you touched; the hips, chest, waist or legs open the Body tab's sculpt sliders with that part's sliders first and lit. The top bar has **Back**, her **Name** (with a dice for a random one), **Undo** and **Redo** (every change, the look, the sculpt and the personality, back and forth), **Presets**, **Randomize** and **Done**. **Randomize** gives every unlocked tab new choices from the race's real option lists, kept right with each other as the old dice did (a face for the skin drawn), the body options and a moderate sculpt on Body, three traits that go together on Personality, a wardrobe outfit on Outfit; **long-press a tab or tap its small padlock** to lock it, and Randomize leaves it as it is.

Every option Shadowlands' creator has for the race and sex is still there, on the tab its name belongs to: Face (face, eyes, eyebrows, features, markings), Hair (style, colour, facial hair), Makeup & Details (skin colour, makeup, overlays, scars, paint, tattoos, the Undead switch), Accessories (ears, horns, jewellery, earrings), Body (the skin colour again, Upright, and a woman's Shape, Style and Density). Each is a line with the creator's own arrows and box; a tap on the box opens all the choices under it (colours as swatches, the others as numbered tiles), and a switch is a switch. **Outfit** has the **Gear** switch and the **Class** buttons (see Gear on and off below). The old two-step Wrath++ layout is kept whole in the code (`ClassicCreatorScreen`).

**Body: Sculpt.** A full-body editor like Create-a-Sim's, on every body: the game's own default body of every race, women and men, and the Eve body alike. Thirty-six sliders from -100 to +100, grouped by part (the part strip: Overall, Neck, Shoulders, Chest, Arms, Waist, Hips, Legs): the eleven it always had, Height, Shoulders, Arms, Bust Size, Bust Lift, Waist, Belly, Hips, Butt, Thighs and Leg Length (same names, same ranges, same places in a save), and eleven more, **Neck** (thickness), **Chest** (the pectorals forward, a man's or a woman's), **Bust Width**, **Bust Spacing**, **Shoulder Slope**, **Upper Arms**, **Forearms**, **Calves**, **Butt Lift**, **Body Fat** (overall softness: shares of the waist, belly, hips, butt, thighs, arms, calves, bust and neck) and **Muscle** (shares of the shoulders, arms, chest, thighs, calves and neck, the waist and belly a little less); and the Bust & Butt designer's fourteen shapes (below), saved under their own keys after those (`bustProjection`, `bustDroop`, `bustUpper`, `bustRound`, `bustSide`, `underbust`, `buttWidth`, `buttProjection`, `buttRound`, `cheekGap`, `hipDip`, `underbutt`, `pecs`, `abs`: a look saved before reads them as 0 and is otherwise as it was). Every slider is 0 by default and 0 is the body exactly as made; a long press on a name puts it back to 0, **Reset** *part* puts that part's back and **Reset all** the lot. Height is her size (the actor's scale, 6% either way). **Leg Length** edits the actor's own skeleton (`engine/bodyrig.h`), each thigh and shin stretched along its bone from 0.85 times as long at -100 to 1.2 times at +100 (girths, feet and everything above the hips as they were), the whole body raised or lowered every frame so the feet meet the ground where the animation puts them on the model's own skeleton (standing, and the planted foot while walking), so her hips stand higher or lower; everything skinned on her follows with no refit (the body, Eve, a tabard's cloth, what she holds), and the flesh measure the pair poses read is taken on the edited skeleton. A walk's stride grows and shrinks with the legs while the walking speed doesn't, so the feet slide a little at the ends of the range. **Drag handles:** on the Body tab, hold a finger still on her for about 0.4 s (a tick) and the part under it is picked (the tap-to-edit's pick, `engine/bodypick.h`): the drag that follows sculpts it on her body, up for bigger and down for smaller, left and right for its width (the bust's size and width, a man's chest and shoulders, the belly and waist, the butt and hips, the thighs and calves, the upper arms and forearms, the neck and shoulders), and with **Lift** on (or a second finger put down while dragging) up and down lifts it and left and right spaces it (the bust's lift and spacing, the butt's lift, the legs' length, the shoulders' slope). Half the stage's height sweeps a slider end to end; the slider on the panel moves with it, the name and value show by the finger ("Bust Size +35"), the phone ticks as a value crosses 0 or reaches an end, and each drag is one step of Undo. A hold on the head, hair, hands or feet turns her as ever. **Symmetry** (on by default): off, a drag changes only the side of her body you hold (her left or right: the pick tells which), and the panel shows each side's value with **Even** to make them alike again (saved as `"sides"` in the sculpt; Height, Leg Length, Neck, Belly, Bust Spacing, Body Fat and Muscle are always both sides). **How it is drawn** (`engine/sculpt.h`, `engine/scene.cpp` refreshBody): the model's vertices are moved in its bind pose in smooth regions made from their own bone weights (each bone's region by its WoW-side name: breasts where the model has breast bones, every playable woman measured here has them, else the chest's front by place and weight; on the game's own women the bust is a soft mound by place round each breast bone, on the chest's front, as far as the skin there is the chest's, because their breast bones' weights are light and patchy: most of a breast's skin is weighted under a tenth to them, which made Bust Size move 1.5 cm at +100 where it now moves about 5), scaled about the limbs' axes, across the body for the hips and waist, pushed forward or back for the belly, chest and butt, up for the lifts, and then skinned as before, so every animation bends the sculpted body exactly as it bent the old one. The spine is followed along its own curve (an orc's or a Forsaken's hunched chest), the limbs' moves fade out before the wrists, ankles and knees, the head, hands and feet never move, copies of a vertex at one place (two geosets meeting) move as one so no seam opens, a vertex at the body's middle takes both sides' mean so the skin stays mirror-symmetric, any triangle that would turn over or shrink below a tenth is eased back, and no vertex moves more than 12% of the body's own hip height (each race's caps scale with its size). The normals of what moved are made again (shared by the copies of a place that faced alike before, so an overlapping hidden geoset's never darkens the skin). The texture coordinates are not changed: the skin and underwear stretch with the surface, by as much as it grows (at the ends: the bust about 40%, the limbs about 30%, the waist and hips about 20%, within the 12% cap), which reads as the same skin on a larger shape. **Live while you drag** (`SculptMorph` in `engine/sculpt.h`, `engine/bodyrig.cpp` sculptSync, the vertex shader's `morphed` in `engine/scene.cpp`): every move above is a slider times a fixed field (its masks come from the body before the sculpt, not from the values), so the first time a slider moves on a body each move slider's field for a value of 1 is made once (45 to 62 ms on this machine) and uploaded as two float textures (per vertex its first entry, how many and its side; per entry the slider and the move; 2048 texels wide, 7.6 to 9.4 MB per body on the five measured, the CPU copy then dropped); from then on a change sets two uniform arrays (the change since the body was last sculpted, both sides and the balance) and the vertex shader adds the fields in the bind pose before the skinning: no CPU sculpt and no upload while you drag, so it follows the finger every frame (the creator sends every 16 ms). The CPU sculpt (each time from the body as it was before the sculpt, not the shape again) is made once the sliders have rested 150 ms, or after a second of moving: it puts back what the fields leave out (the 12% cap, the triangles it eases, Body Fat's and Muscle's 1.5 clamps, the normals), and the body is then exactly the CPU's again; the result is kept until the look changes; the flesh profile the pair poses read, the soft body, the tabard's cloth and the tap-to-edit pick all use the sculpted body. **Gear:** the clothing geosets the body model carries itself (robes, skirts, trousers, sleeves, boots, cloaks, tabards: they are in its own vertex list) take the same moves and follow the body (a robe's vertices near the skin moved within about 15% of the skin under them on the five bodies measured); armour textures are painted on the skin and follow with it. What is a separate model does not follow: shoulder pads, helmets, belt buckles and held things are rigid on their bones, and a piece drawn as its own skinned model (some newer chest pieces and capes) keeps its shape, so at the ends (bust, chest, shoulders or hips past about +60) the body can show through or poke out of those. **Measured on the desktop** (`engine-test/sculpt_test FILES_DIR 1000764 1011653 949470 921844 959310`: the human woman and man, the orc woman, the night elf woman, the Forsaken man; their default models have 226,000 to 266,000 vertices with every geoset variant and 48,000 to 57,000 triangles): every slider at both ends bounded, no triangle turned over, the skin mirror-symmetric, the head, hands and feet unmoved, no seam opening between geosets drawn together, the same input giving the same body, and a sculpt costing 20 to 29 ms each on this machine (a phone will be slower); the live sculpt's fields against the CPU sculpt (`morphCheck`): a drag's 0.2 step from a mixed sculpt within 0.001 cm on four bodies and 0.07 cm on the Forsaken man, a mixed sculpt from the body as made within 0.001 cm (0.11 on the Forsaken man), one slider at ±50 from the body as made within 0.001 cm on the night elf and up to 0.6 to 2.4 cm on the others at a few hundred vertices (0.002% of all vertex results: the shoulders' and the orc's small bust where the CPU sculpt eases a triangle that would collapse; the CPU sculpt 150 ms after the drag puts them back), the fields the same twice and none on the head, hands, feet or ankle rings, their weights 0.2 µs to work out; drawn by the harness (`sculptlive on`), every bust and butt slider at ±100 on the human woman by the fields alone matches the CPU's silhouettes exactly, only the light differs (the normals are the old ones until the CPU sculpt lands); a frame drawn while a slider moves cost 4.5 ms (4.0 standing still) against 27 ms when the CPU sculpted each change (`sculpttime`); and in desktop renders of those five, every slider group at -100 and +100 from the front, three-quarters and side, and with a robe and a plate set at bust and hips +100. Not run on a phone: its frame rate while dragging and the fields' memory (7.6 to 9.4 MB of float textures per body sculpted, the Eve body none) are measured on the desktop only; the drag handles, the haptics and the panels are compiled by CI only. The Eve body takes the same sliders as before (its fit sculpted after fitting, as it always was) and the new ones too (but Neck: its neck ring meets the Warcraft head). The Body tab's **Skin & Shape** part holds the Eve body's own switch, lower body, skin tone, overlay and its strength once the host binds them to the look (`CasEveBinding`; until then a note says to import a set). The sculpt is saved with the NPC (`"sculpt":{...}` in its look, left out at all zeros); the Eve body's shape follows her into every scene, her Height shows in the creator only so far.

**Bust & Butt** (a man's **Chest & Glutes**): the Body tab's third part, a designer for the bust and the butt rather than a list. Its groups, **Bust** (Size, Width, Spacing, Projection, Lift, Droop, Upper Fullness, Roundness, Side Fullness, Underbust) and **Butt** (Butt, Width, Lift, Projection, Roundness, Cheek Gap, Hip Dip, Underbutt, with Hips, Waist and Thighs as they are), on a man **Chest** (Chest, Pec Definition, Abs, Shoulders) and **Glutes** (his Butt, its width, lift, projection and roundness, Hips, Waist, Thighs). Opening it turns the camera to the group's view (the bust from the side, the butt from behind) and **Front**, **3/4**, **Side** and **Back** turn and frame her there. Big sliders with their value, drawn live as they move (above). The drag on her body: hold the bust or the butt, then drag, in the mode chosen, **Size** (up and down its size, left and right its width), **Lift** (its lift and spacing; a second finger down while dragging does the same) or **Projection** (the bust's projection and roundness, a man's pectorals; the butt's projection and roundness). **Mirror** (on by default; the same switch as Sculpt's Symmetry): off, each slider of the group shows its sides' balance (her left above the value, her right below, L and R shown) and a drag changes the side you hold; turned back on, the group's sides are made alike. **Shapes**: Slim, Natural, Athletic, Curvy and Full set the group's sliders (the rest of the group to 0) and the sliders glide there over a third of a second, drawn live; **Randomize** a plausible shape for the group (its size leading, the others leaning its way, within ±70), **Reset** the group to 0, and **Undo** and **Redo** (each slider drag, body drag, shape or Randomize one step, as the top bar's). On the game's women the designer's shapes are drawn about the bust's mound (Projection forward from the chest, the tip leading; Droop turning each breast down about its root, the underside rolled out over the fold; Upper Fullness the slope above; Roundness fuller round the tip; Side Fullness the outer side toward the arm; Underbust the underside fuller) and about each cheek's centre under the hip joints (Width the back half wider, Projection each cheek's crown back, Roundness fuller and mostly down, Cheek Gap the cleft deeper, Hip Dip the side under the hip bone drawn in by how far round to the side of the spine it lies, Underbutt the fold where the cheek meets the thigh fuller); the Eve body takes them as its own sculpt (her fit, as before: on the CPU, on the worker). Checked on the desktop in renders of the human, orc and night elf women (every bust and butt slider at -100, 0 and +100 from the front, three-quarters and side, the butt from behind, three-quarters behind and side) and the human and Forsaken men (Chest, Pec Definition, Abs and the glutes): bounded, none turned over, symmetric. Limits: the texture is stretched with the surface (a large bust or butt shows the skin's detail larger); armour drawn as its own model doesn't follow (as above); at the ends Droop folds the underside against the chest in a crease, Lift at -100 likewise, and Cheek Gap and Underbutt are small changes on these models' meshes.

**Personality.** Choose up to three of the twelve life traits (opposites, such as Social and Loner, don't go together; none chosen: she gets three of her own as before; **Choose for me** picks three from her look), her **Preferences** (when someone else starts something: Yes, Ask me or No; Pace: Slow, Either or Intense; which stages she allows: Kiss, Embrace, Cuddle, Dance, Play, Intimate), a short **About her** and a favourite colour. The life simulation reads them (`simulation/LifePreferences`): her chosen traits replace the assigned ones; NPCs never start a stage with her that she doesn't allow or when she said No, ask her (she says yes less often) when she said Ask me, and the most two NPCs do together by themselves is the least of what their standing and both of their boundaries allow; a slow pace makes her want kisses and cuddles less, an intense one more. What you order yourself is yours to choose. Saved with the NPC as `"personality":{...}`.

**Presets** keeps whole looks under a name (race, every choice, body options, sculpt, personality, outfit) in `files/cas-presets/`: **Save look**, **Load**, **Delete**, **Export** (a `.json` file you can share) and **Import a file**. The named characters' looks are built in (Chromie's gnome look with her own golden hair).

Looks saved before read back exactly as they were. The tabs' sorting, the history, Randomize with locks, the sculpt and personality models, the presets' files and the old saves are unit-tested (`CasTest`, `SculptModelTest`, `BustButtDesignerTest`: the designer's groups, views, shapes and their glide, Randomize, Reset, Mirror, the new keys in saves and the Projection drag; `LifePreferencesTest`, `CasLayoutTest`); the sculpt's maths and the body pick on a made-up body (`engine-test/sculpt_test`), and the sliders were drawn on the desktop on the human woman's Eve body; the tap pick was checked on her there too (head, hair, chest, waist, arms, hands, legs, feet). The screen itself is Compose, compiled by CI only: it has not been run, on a desktop or a phone.

## Gear on and off

There is no wardrobe: every NPC has one switch, **Gear**. **Gear on** wears the NPC's class's default clothing: the game's own starting outfit for that race, sex and class (the worn pieces of `CharStartOutfit`: shirt, chest or robe, belt, legs, feet, wrists, hands, and for a death knight the whole set from helm to cloak). **Gear off** is nude: every armour slot empty and the skin colour's underwear left off too (the named characters' bare look; the game's and an imported set's underwear alike), while the face, hair, makeup, markings, horns and the rest stay as they are; a woman with the Eve body on wears Eve, a man with an imported body-parts set wears his parts, as with any unclothed look.

- **Where the switch is:** the creator's **Outfit** tab (the **Gear** switch, and the **Class** buttons: Civilian and the classes the race may be, a class tap also turning Gear on); the classic two-step creator has a **Class** button at its foot beside the Gear row of every category; every NPC's menu in the NPC picker (the round button on its card) has a **Gear** row; in a live scene the bar over the actions has **Gear on / Gear off** for the selected NPC and **All gear on / All gear off** for everyone in the scene (they are dressed again at once; the switches are kept for the NPCs when the scene ends). Randomize keeps the switch and the class while Outfit is locked.
- **Classes:** warrior, paladin, hunter, rogue, priest, death knight, shaman, mage, warlock, monk and druid, as each race may be them (`CharBaseInfo`); **Civilian** (no class, the default for every NPC made before) wears the recruit's shirt, pants and boots, the clothing new NPCs always started in. Demon hunters and evokers are not offered: the game has no starting outfit for them in any client that still has the table.
- **The outfits** (`assets/class_outfits.json`, ids only, made by `tools/class_outfits.py`): `CharStartOutfit` from Mists of Pandaria Classic (5.5.4: the newest client with the table, retail dropped it; races 1 to 11, the worgen 22 and the pandaren 24), each item turned into its look at the app's build (12.1.7: `ItemModifiedAppearance`, `ItemAppearance`, `Item`'s slot). Where the table has no row: an allied or newer race takes the race it was made from (a void elf a blood elf's, a Kul Tiran a human's, a Mag'har an orc's...), a pair the table never had (an orc priest, a dwarf druid...) a human's or another race's of that class and sex, and anything else the civilian clothes. The pieces' models and textures are fetched from wago.tools as an NPC first wears them (no download pack lists them).
- **The named characters:** Gear on is her signature outfit (her first outfit, or another outfit of hers if one is chosen), Gear off her nude look (her **bare** look; else her outfit's nude one; else her underwear one); her **Look** picker stays as it was, and choosing a look there sets the switch with it (an outfit, tabard or partial look is on; underwear, bare and nude are off). Chromie has her outfit only, so her switch stays on.
- **Saves:** the appearance keeps `"gear"` and, for a class, `"class"`; nothing of the clothing itself is saved. NPCs, scenes and presets saved with the Transmog window's outfits load as Gear on (any piece worn, or the recruit clothes) or Gear off (`"gear":false`, or every slot cleared), their pieces not kept; a field the app doesn't know is ignored. The switch set on the menu or in a scene is kept in `files/npc-looks.json` (`"gear"`, over the NPC's own; a named character's as her chosen look).
- **What was removed:** the Transmogrify window (slot buttons, item and set lists, Custom Sets, search, filter, the cards and their pictures in `files/thumbs/transmog/`, which are no longer made), its art (`assets/transmog_art.json`), the starter-clothing catalog's tables loaded at the creator, and the **Transmog items (large)** download pack. The named characters' underwear, nude, tabard-only and partial looks never depended on it and are all kept.

- **Bare toes in boots:** a death knight's Acherus Knight's Greaves show the toes, as in the game: the boots' foot texture is see-through over the toes and the item's display (140716) asks for the toes' geoset (`GeosetGroup_1` 1: geoset 2001; 0, the shod foot, is 2002). The classes with no boots at all in their starting outfit (the monks, the pandaren, the tauren and draenei of several classes, the robed orc, troll and night elf casters) go barefoot, as they start in the game.

Tested on the JVM (`GearTest`: the classes per race, each class's outfit per race and sex and the fallbacks, Gear on and off, the Eve decision both ways and the men's parts, older saves, the creator's class and switch, npc-looks.json, everyone's switch, the named characters; `ImportedLooksTest`: Gear off draws no underwear; `GearRealTest` on the game's tables: every outfit with boots, every race, sex and class, sets the boots' and the feet's geosets as its item says and places the boots' lower leg and foot layers). Desktop renders of a human woman and man in four classes, on and off, were drawn by engine-test; the switch's screens are compiled by CI only and have not run on a phone.

**Download everything** (Options → Downloads) fetches every file the app can otherwise fetch from wago.tools as it is first wanted, so the game can be played offline afterwards. Its packs, in order, each with its files, size and state (Not downloaded, Partial 62%, Complete, Needs update) and its own Download button: **Game basics** (the first launch's gathering: the interface, the tables, Dalaran, every character creator option for every race and sex, about 6.1 GB), **Places** (the eleven locations and every race's backdrop, 0.21 GB), **Menu and creator art** (the creator's and the menus' atlas sheets, kept in their own caches), **Music, ambience and voices** (sounds.json, the time of day's flavour and bells, fire, rain and cloth, every emote's voice for every race and sex: 4,364 files, about 0.15 GB), **Character animations** (every animation file of every character model, about 1.6 GB), **Named characters** (every display they are drawn as or cut from, about 0.26 GB), and three big packs ticked by default that can be left out: **Library creatures (large)** (the placement library's creature models and the game's named creatures: 16,063 displays, about 7.6 GB), **Library props (very large)** (51,111 props, about 3.8 GB) and **Library buildings (huge)** (12,584 buildings with their rooms, about 22 GB): about 42 GB in all. The sizes are estimates (`assets/files.json`'s `estimates`, made by `tools/pack_sizes.py` from the game's tables, the asset lists and the averages of files measured while making the app: wago.tools gives no sizes ahead) until a pack has run, then its real count. Before it starts the free space is checked (1 GB always kept free): with too little room it says how much is needed and offers **Download what fits** (the required packs and the optional ones that fit, smallest first). It runs as a foreground service (`data/DownloadService.kt`, type dataSync) with a notification (the pack, the progress, Pause and Cancel), so it goes on with the app in the background or the screen off, the CPU kept awake; the notification says how it ended. **Wi-Fi only** waits for Wi-Fi; **Also on mobile data** lets this download go on without it. Pause, Resume and Cancel keep every file already here. A file that fails is tried again by itself after 30 s, 1 and 2 minutes (each file also has the limiter's five tries with 429/5xx backoff, the download speed's 24 to 96 at once); after that, or when wago.tools has no such file, it is listed under **Failed files** for **Retry failed**. The state is kept (`files/cache/download-state.txt`): a download the app's closing or Android stopped carries on when the app is opened again (Android 15 lets a background download run six hours a day; at that limit it pauses until the app is opened). At the end **Verify** checks every file of every pack (each pack's list in `files/cache/packs/<pack>.ids`: there, and the length it came with or the length the asset lists give; the lists carry no checksums) and fetches the damaged ones again: "Everything is here (N files, X GB)". **Pre-build caches** (off by default) also prepares the scene sounds' index after it. Still fetched as wanted: what an imported or user-made look needs that no pack lists, the game's sounds for every creature and object placed in a scene (the whole game's sound library is too large to fetch blindly), and the community animation packs (installed from the Animation library, not here). The packs' logic is unit-tested (`DownloadPlanTest`: the packs, sizes, free-space refusal, states, failed queue, kept state, verify's check); the service, the notification and the page are compiled by CI only and have not run on a phone, and how Android 14 and 15 treat the service has not been seen on a device. Sims 4 rigs and clips are imported from your own files.

## The Eve body

Your own Eve body (the Sims 4 body replacer, never part of the app) can take the place of a woman's body: her head, neck, hair and feet stay the game's, the body from the neck to the ankles is Eve's, fitted to her race's skeleton and tinted to her own skin, with an optional breast overlay. Only unclothed looks: a woman wearing nothing on her chest, shirt, tabard, waist, legs, wrists or hands (`bodyparts/EveOptions.kt` EveRule; boots and a helm are fine); any other look keeps her own body, and the swap follows every change of look.

**The conversion** (`bodyparts/EveConvert.kt`, on a background thread on the phone): from the packages you pick (`.package` files, folders or `.zip` archives of them) it finds by their contents Eve's upper body (a CAS top with a mesh), each lower body (plain, animated, fairy, ...: one mesh each), the skins (CAS parts without a mesh whose texture covers Eve's body: a nearly grey one is the neutral skin, tinted per NPC; the others are skin tones, their mean colours the tone table) and the overlays (textures covering part of the body, from packages without full skins: each package a slot named by its last word, "...overlay-acne" is `acne`, each part a shade with the swatch it was made for). It writes `files/bodyparts/eve/`: `eve_<variant>.weve` (the merged mesh in Warcraft axes, Sims metres, both texture sets, four weights by Warcraft bone name through `assets/sims_profile.json` plus Eve's twist and helper bones, the open rings at the neck and ankles, the Sims woman's joints; the layout, its version and CRC are documented in `bodyparts/EveFormat.kt`), `skin_neutral.png`, `tone_NN.png`, `ov_<slot>_NN.png` (cropped to what each paints) and `set.json`. Every texture is cropped to the rows of the 2048 x 4096 atlas Eve uses and stored 1024 wide; textures are decoded as a stream of 4x4 tiles straight into that crop (`SimsTexture.decodeTiles`), so the peak stays under 100 MB. The user's set (Eve 10.2 top, three bottoms, three skin packages, the four "juicyboobs" overlay packages): 31 s on the desktop, 72 MB written (3 meshes of 4,695 vertices and 8,206 triangles, 34 tones, 4 overlay slots of 24 to 33 shades), run with a 100 MB heap. The Sims woman's joints are estimates made from Eve's own skin weights (`EveProfile.JOINTS`), not a rig.

**The fit** (`engine/evebody.cpp`, `evescene.cpp`): when the look is dressed or changes, the engine fits Eve to that model as shaped: the torso by height between the hip, shoulder and neck joints, its width by the skin's own hip width and the shoulders' spacing (times the race's `girth`), each limb segment along its bone with its girth, the hands and fingers by their knuckles; the neck and ankles drawn onto the model's own neck tube and foot tops, then the rings snapped onto the model's cut edge with its bone weights and normals (the neck ring tucked inside the neck, the ankle rings over the foot's edge), so the seams stay shut in every pose. The model's skin under the body bones is cut (a triangle goes when any corner is weighted mostly to the pelvis, spine, chest, clavicles, breasts, arms, hands, thighs or calves; hair, ears and other non-skin sections never). Eve is drawn in the body pass, skinned on the actor's own bones (animations, imported clips, human scale), with the soft body re-measured on her (the breasts' regions from the vertices weighted to the breast bones) and the flesh measure taken on the body as shown, so the flesh lift and pair spacing see Eve. The skin: the neutral skin's baked low-frequency shading flattened once (80% of each 6 cm neighbourhood's difference from the body's mean taken out), then tinted per channel in linear light so its colour near the neck ring is the actor's own skin at the neck's cut edge (her base skin layer sampled there); or a chosen tone as it is. The seams are feathered in texture space: within 1.2 cm of a ring the texture fades to the Warcraft skin's colour there. The overlay's shade is the one whose swatch is nearest her skin by Lab distance, tinted in brightness only (fully, per channel, for a skin no shade was made for: green, blue, grey), blended at 60% by default.

**Her own rig (two skeletons).** Eve works in the Sims because she is skinned to the Sims rig, her weights authored for its joints. Squeezed onto the Warcraft skeleton (the old fit, still `rig` 0) her ribcage hung on a spine bone 10 to 25 cm below it, her belly on the pelvis, her thighs pulled skin above the hips, and her joints sat off her flesh. Now each actor has two skeletons: her **regular** one (the model's own, untouched, whenever Eve is off: the same matrices as before) and, while she wears Eve, the **Eve rig** (`rig` 1 in `eve_fit.json`'s defaults; `EveBody::rigPivots`). Her body is used as she was made, scaled to the model's neck height (so she stands as tall as the body she replaces), and every body bone's pivot is put at the joint the Sims adult rig has for it (`kEveSimsRig` in `engine/evebody.cpp`: read from the game's own rig resource, the one she was skinned for: pelvis, the three spine joints, clavicles, shoulders, elbows, wrists, fingers, hips, knees, breasts and the twist helpers); her weights stay as authored (the .weve already carries them, one Warcraft name per Sims bone, so nobody re-imports). Her arms and legs are first turned to point as the model's rest pose does, skinned by her own weights about her own joints as a Sims body is posed, and her legs are set to end on the model's own ankles; the model's animations are then run about her joints (`engine/bodyrig.h` `bodyEditFrom`: each bone's turn as the model's, about her pivot, so every limb points as the model's does in every pose, checked to 0 degrees), the feet kept on the ground. Pictures don't carry the sculpt sliders.

**In the game's poses.** Her shoulders' weights are smoothed over her mesh within 13 cm of the shoulder joint (four passes, most at the joint): the Sims rig's shoulder helpers fell onto the model's upper arm and clavicle in a narrow band, which pinched a ridge over the shoulder and a crease into the armpit with an arm raised (cheering, asleep with an arm under the head). `eve_test` with the real set and the game's files poses the human, night elf and blood elf women in their own sequences (SitChairMed, KneelLoop, Death, Sleep, EmoteCheer, EmoteBow; each sampled at five moments) and skins her and the model on the CPU as the renderer does (her proportions' skeleton): no seam opens (each stitch vertex stays on its model vertex), no edge across the seam strip grows more than 3 cm or half again the model's own cut edge, her triangles' edges grow at most 1.5 times the model's own body's in the same pose and 1 cm more (99.9th percentile; measured: 1.7 to 8.7 cm against the model's 3.1 to 22 cm), and under 0.2 % of her triangles collapse. Smoothing took the night elf's and blood elf's asleep and cheering figures down by about a tenth (7.8 to 6.7, 6.7 to 5.6, 5.5 to 5.0 cm). Desktop stills of the three races in the six poses beside the standard body were looked at. What is left: the ankle seam steps out where the model's cut edge runs round the top of the foot (worst on the night elf, a 1 cm cuff seen from a few centimetres; at full length a faint line), the blood elf's own cheer clip doesn't raise the arms high, and the human's high-arm shoulders still peak a little more than the model's.

**Off the GL thread.** A look change sends a job (`EveJob`: the body as built before Eve's cut, its geosets, the look's options, sculpt, skin file and tint, taken on the GL thread) to the scene's worker thread (`EveWorker`), which does the fit, the sculpt, the cut, the measure mesh, the skin's composition and its mip levels (`eveCompute`). Until it comes what was drawn stays (her last Eve, or her own body the first time); a newer look replaces a job still queued and a finished fit for an older look is dropped. The result is then uploaded a slice a frame (its mesh, then about 640 KB of texture rows a frame) and swapped in whole in one frame, the body's own triangles changed by its index buffer alone. Pictures (`Scene::thumbnail`) and the placing ghost still fit at once. On the desktop (llvmpipe), a look change on a woman already in Eve: the GL thread's worst frame 2.4 to 4.2 ms (the snapshot and swap 0.2 to 1 ms, the soft body's re-measure about 1.6 ms of it) against about 70 ms when it all ran there; the fit is ready 15 to 22 frames later. The worker's result is byte for byte the one computed at once (`eve_test`, the real set on the human and draenei models); drawn, the two differ only by the mip levels' filter (a mean of 2/255 on Eve's skin).

## HD Models

Your own high-detail characters can **replace the game's models**: for a race and sex with an imported HD set, the HD figure is the character everywhere (the creator, the scene, partner animations, cinematic mode, portraits and pictures, Gear on and off): its own body, head, face, ears, eyes, brows, lashes, hair, jewellery and, where the set has them, its own outfits. The game's model of that race and sex is no longer drawn. The sets are made from your own downloaded model files (FBX zips such as Liard's Art's edited World of Warcraft models, or a Blender `.blend`); none of them is part of the app.

**Importing.** Options → Mods → **HD Models**: **Choose files** (the converted zip, `Wifecraft-HD-*.zip`, or its `.whd` files); each set lands in `files/bodyparts/hd/<race>_<sex>.whd`, one per race and sex (a new one replaces that race's alone). Each set's row says what it has (hair styles, outfits, eyes, marks, skin tints, jewellery, triangles, texture memory) and who made it, with its own **Remove**; **Remove all**. The files are copied and checked (the header, its index, every part inside the file) on a thread of their own (`bodyparts/HdModels.kt` HdImportRunner), and the folder is replaced only when every picked set was good: a damaged file keeps the old sets and says why. **Use HD Models** (on once sets are imported; off draws everyone on the game's models again, the sets kept) applies to looks dressed after it changes.

**Converting** (on a computer, `tools/hd/whd_convert.py`, inside Blender's Python): `python -I tools/hd/whd_convert.py RECIPE.json MODEL.fbx TEXTURE_DIR OUT_DIR --zip Wifecraft-HD-Name.zip`. A **recipe** (`tools/hd/recipes/*.json`, data, not code: the next races are recipes) names the model's body, its always-drawn pieces (lashes, brows), its hair styles, jewellery and outfits (each outfit with the classes it is for), each material's image (an FBX names none), the skin and hair base textures and the presets that replace them (Liard's Art's other skin tones and hair colours), the eye textures, the face and body marks, and its rig: `weights` (each rig bone to the game bone it does the work of; `{S}` for each side) and `joints` (each game bone's joint on the HD figure). A recipe may `extends` another (`liard_rig.json`: the joints every Liard's Art model shares). The converter imports the FBX (or opens the `.blend`), evaluates every named object at the rig's rest pose with its shape keys (armature, subdivision and mask modifiers off), decimates a piece over its budget, writes Warcraft axes in metres, four normalised weights per vertex, the textures as PNG (at most 1024 px, the body's skin 2048), each outfit's covered body triangles (the skin under it isn't drawn, so it can't poke through) and the presets. Recipes now: Liard's Art's **Human Female 4.0** (`liard_human_female.json`: 32 hair styles, 12 jewellery pieces, 2 skin tones and 2 tinted, 13 hair colours, 23 eye colours; she is the human woman's HD body, with human ears), **Nelf Female 4.0** (`liard_nightelf_female.json`: 18 hair styles, 15 pieces of jewellery and horns, 4 skin tints, the hair colours, 10 eyes), **Human Male 3.0** (`liard_human_male.json`: his hair and beard painted on his skin, 4 skin tints), and a night elf woman from a `.blend` (`nightelf_female.json`, with the Tyrande, Warden, druid, ranger and swimsuit outfits mapped to classes, and its **human variant**: the ears made human (the blend's EarsSmall key, then each ear vertex's distance along the ear folded to 3.4 cm, its reach cut by a third, its top rounded), the brows shortened, the skin turned toward a human tone in Lab).

**The set** (`.whd`, version 1): `WHD1`, u32 version, u32 JSON length, u32 blob count, the JSON (padded to 16), a table of (u64 offset, u64 size) per blob, the blobs at 16-byte boundaries. The JSON: race and sex (ChrRaces id; 0 male, 1 female), label, credit, the bone names the weights use, the joints, the textures (PNG blobs), the meshes (vertex and index blobs, parts by texture, opaque or alpha-tested), the always-drawn meshes, eye textures, hair, outfits (classes, covered body triangles), jewellery, overlays, skin and hair presets (a colour gain and/or a texture of their own), the base skin and hair textures, the texture memory and triangle counts. A vertex is 40 bytes: position f32 x3 (metres, x forward, y left, z up, feet at 0), normal f32 x3, UV f32 x2 (top-left origin, repeating), four bone indices and four u8 weights summing to 255; triangles u32.

**On the game's skeleton** (`engine/hdbody.cpp` fitHd, `hdscene.cpp`). The actor keeps its game model underneath (skeleton, animations, imported Sims clips, attachments, speech anchors, scale), only it isn't drawn. The HD figure is scaled so its neck stands as high as the model's, its pelvis over the model's; its arms, hands, fingers, legs and feet are turned to point as the model's rest pose does (skinned by its own weights about its own joints); every bone's pivot is put at the HD figure's joint (`bodyrig.h` bodyEditFrom, as for the Eve rig), so the game's animations bend it at its own joints. Its weights are the model's own authored ones mapped onto the game's bones by name: a nearest-vertex transfer from the game's body was measured against them and stretches the body by 6 to 60 cm in the game's poses where the authored ones stretch it 0.4 to 5 cm, about as the game's own body does; the transfer remains for a bone the model lacks. The soft body is found on the HD body (its breasts measured on its own vertices), the flesh measure (partner alignment) and the creator's sculpt sliders work on it (the sculpt about its own joints, outfits and hair following); Leg Length too. Fitting takes 1 to 5 ms; each texture is decoded once and shared by everyone drawn with it.

**Clothing.** Gear on wears the set's outfit for her class (the Class picker; Civilian the civilian outfit), or the first other outfit when the set has none for it (the Outfit tab says so); Gear off is the bare HD body. A set without outfits (the three Liard's Art models: their FBX carry no clothing) is drawn bare with Gear on too, and the creator says so. Each NPC can also pick an outfit of the set or bare whatever Gear says.

**In the creator.** For an HD race the game's own Face, Hair, Makeup and Accessories rows are replaced by the set's: eyes and face marks, hair style and colour, jewellery; the Body tab's Skin & Shape has the skin presets and body marks (the Eve body never goes on an HD figure); the sculpt sliders and Bust & Butt shape the HD figure. Saved in the look as `"hd":{...}`.

**Memory.** Liard's Art's woman: 66,000 triangles for the body and up to 3,000 for her hair (the game's body has 3,000 to 4,500), about 54 MB of textures when every preset is decoded (about 25 MB as usually drawn: a 2048 px skin, a hair and an eye texture, with mipmaps). Drawn on the GPU like the game's body.

Tested on the desktop: `HdModelsTest` (the header's checks, imports from zips and loose sets, one per race and sex, a failed import keeping the old sets, Remove per race and all, the options' save, the decision table: off, no set, Gear off bare, Gear on by class, Civilian, a class without an outfit, an NPC's own outfit, a set without clothing, the pictures' mark), the whole JVM suite; `engine-test/hdbody_test SET.whd FILES_DIR MODEL` with the three Liard's Art sets on the game's human woman, night elf woman and human man (the file written back and read again unchanged, a cut file refused, every weight summing to 255 on bones the model has, the limbs within 0.03 degrees of the model's rest directions, the neck's height and the pelvis within 2 cm, the edge stretch and collapsed triangles in standing, walking, a chair, kneeling, asleep, cheering and bowing against the model's own body); the harness (`hdset`, `hd`, `hdstatus`) drew them side by side with the game's models in those poses and twenty of the game's emotes, the outfits standing, walking and from behind, and the faces close up. What isn't done: the Liard's Art models carry no clothing; the human man's hair and beard choices (the zip's scalp and beard textures are laid onto his skin in the game's own layout, not mapped yet: his painted hair and beard are drawn); the night elf woman's nightborne hair and brows (their textures aren't in the zip) are left out; the jewellery's own texture isn't in the human woman's zip (the hair texture stands in); the shape keys the models carry (bigger or smaller breasts, hips, thighs) aren't offered yet; a named character drawn as the game's own display stays the game's model. The Android side (Options rows, the creator's rows, the Dresser, Thumbs and JNI calls) compiles in CI only; nothing of it has run on a phone.

## Sims 4 animations

For WickedWhims and animation packs made for it, open **Choose objects → Animation library → Import WickedWhims**: a list of packs (`assets/anim_packs.json`: each pack's name, author and page address only; no pack content is in the app). Nothing is downloaded until you tap **Install** on a pack. **WickedWhims** downloads the original archive from the author’s itch.io page and adds its compatible clips to the **WickedWhims** category, and when no Sims skeleton is loaded it keeps the skeleton it carries for the other packs. The others come from their authors' wicked.cc pages: Tibo131's Facial Climax Pose Pack (18 poses), Anarcis' Animations for WickedWhims (one 293 MB archive) and Azmodan22's WickedWhims Sex Animations (four archives: Gold or Platinum, Normal or Rough; you choose one). They carry no skeleton, so they need WickedWhims installed first or a skeleton chosen under Import Animations; they go to the review like picked files. The page's download links are read in order with their file names and sizes (only that page's own `/download/<id>` links); only https to the pack's own hosts (itch.io and its file hosts; wicked.cc and files.wicked.cc) is followed, a busy server's Retry-After is waited out, and the download shows its progress and can be cancelled. The importer reads both generations of these packs' tuning (an actor's clip as `animation_clip_name` or `animation_name`, its turn as `animation_angle_offset` or `animation_facing_offset`), names entries from the package's own string tables, groups an author's stages by their next-stage links ("morning1 → morning2…", "… Stage 1 → … Stage 2 → … Climax": one "… sequence" in play order), and leaves out, saying why, the furniture's and props' own clips and the clips only an animation for three or more people uses. Measured on the desktop with the packs themselves (numbers, nothing looked at, nothing on a phone): see the limits below.

**Storage.** The library keeps each animation as a small clip file (`files/anims/clips/<id>.wclip`, `anim/ClipStore`: rotations as smallest-three 15-bit numbers, positions as 16- or 24-bit steps of each track's own range, key times as whole ticks, deflated, with a version byte and checksums), with keys dropped where the keys either side reproduce them (within 0.1 degree on the body's chain from the pelvis to the wrists and ankles, 0.25 degree on hands, face and toes, 0.5 mm on positions; the first and last key always kept, so poses hold and loops close). Names, categories, the Use in scenes switch, the importer's furniture reading and each clip's pose/loop verdict are in one index (`files/anims/index.json`, its format in `anim/AnimLibrary`), which is all the library reads when it opens; a clip's keys are read only when it is played or previewed, and the last eight read are kept. Renaming, moving or deleting touches only the index. An import writes each clip to a staging folder as it is converted, so a pack never sits in memory; the review lists the staged clips, **Save** moves the kept ones in and **Discard** deletes the folder. The review can keep **Only two-person animations** or **The first N**, and says how much the kept ones take. A pack's file list and its download say about how much it will take on the phone; with too little free space an install is refused before it starts, and one that runs out of room stops, keeping what came before (200 MB is always left free). Animations saved in the old, larger JSON files keep loading: each is converted the first time it is played, or all at once with **Compact library** on the Animations page. Measured on the desktop with the packs themselves (numbers only): Anarcis 2,649 animations, 38 MB (was about 4.4 GB), its import's heap at most 68 MB after collection with a 200 MB limit (was about 900 MB); Azmodan22 Gold 927, 17 MB (was about 3 GB); Platinum 117, 2 MB; WickedWhims 160, 4.4 MB; Tibo131's 18 poses, 43 KB. The largest difference from the unreduced clips at the pelvis, head, hands and feet is 0.47 cm (mean 0.03 cm). The library opens in about 0.2 s with 4,000 entries on the desktop (a phone is slower). Not run on a phone.

From **Choose objects**, open **Animation library** and select **Import rig and clips**. Choose a Sims 4 animation package or its ZIP or 7z archive. The importer can use a humanoid skeleton included in the package. For packs without one, choose an adult Sims 4 skeleton from your game installation. The importer reports which clips it can retarget and lets you review them before saving.

Use **Animation library → Browse animations** to preview imported clips before a scene. Once the scene starts, NPCs wander. Select an NPC and choose **Play animation**: a window with category tabs (All, each group, and **Partner**, first while the NPC has a partner), a search and large rows. Pick a row, then **Preview** plays it once at once with the window kept open, and the main button plays or queues it as the mode row says (**Play now**, **Queue**, **Queue stages**). For a partner animation, pick another NPC and the app starts the two roles together after they meet. Under the modes, how it repeats: **Once** (the author's own loop count), **Repeat ×N** (- and + set N, 2 to 20 loops) or **Until stopped** (until you stop it or queue another clip); and the queue's own **Repeat queue** (when the queue ends it plays round again, each entry whole) and **Shuffle** (its next is picked at random). All of them are always shown (the count's buttons only work for Repeat ×N, so nothing moves), and they are kept per scene (its place and its cast). **Queue** adds a clip after the current order, and **Queue stages** follows the stages linked by the author. Reinstall WickedWhims to refresh the grouping, stage links, and authored cycle counts for clips already in your library.

**Start, loop and end as one animation.** A pack's clips named as one animation's start, loop and end (`..._start`, `_intro`, `_begin`; `_loop`, `_idle`, `_regular`, `_anim1`...; `_end`, `_stop`, `_outro`, with an author's `stage 1/2/3` after them or not: `anim/StageAnimation.kt`'s rules) are one row in the window and one entry in the queue, its parts hidden; anything that doesn't follow that pattern (a pose pack's `_set_1`/`_set_2` actors, an author's numbered stages) stays as it was. Played, the start plays once, then the loop (several loop clips in turn) for its count, then the end once; repeated (Until stopped, or a repeated queue's last entry) it is the start, then the loop on and on, and the end only when it is stopped, skipped or the queue moves on. **Stop** plays the end at once and then they stand (the queue cleared); **Stop repeat** lets this loop finish, then the end; **Skip** plays the end and goes on to the queue's next. While it plays, the line over the action bar says the part and the loop: "Stage 2 · Cowgirl · Loop 3/5", "… · Loop 4 · repeating", "… · Start", "… · End". In the user's WickedWhims package nine such animations were found (the make-it-rain, cramps and cramps seated, camera recording, ballerina statue, both floor dances, and the two three-stage solo ones). Every join (start to loop, loop to loop, loop to end, a stage to the next, onto furniture and back to standing) is smooth: the engine crossfades the body from the pose last drawn (250 ms), and where the person stands and faces is eased from the old place to the new one over the same time (0.4 s onto furniture and back to standing), so nobody teleports or snaps round between stages; a stage after a stage on the same furniture never walks or re-seats. Measured on the package's 24 two-person performances (`JoinEase`; numbers, not looked at): the author's stage chain 1 to 9 had its worst frame-to-frame jump of a joint at 2.3 yards (stage 8 to 9 turns the bodies 180°) and 1.8 (7 to 8), now 0.35 and 0.33, the bodies' own crossfade; loops of the same stage were already crossfaded (at most 0.09 yards a frame). The playback is unit-tested on the desktop (`StagePlaybackTest`, `StageRulesTest`, `PlayOptionsTest`); how smooth it looks has not been seen on a phone.

A two-person animation whose bodies need furniture (a bed, a couch, a chair, a table: the furniture it was read as needing) is played on the nearest free piece it fits, wherever it is ordered from: Play now, Queue and Queue stages place it exactly as the furniture's own Play window does, and the stages of a sequence stay on that one piece from the same spot, never re-seated between stages; a floor one plays where the NPCs stand, as before, and one with nothing free to fit on is refused with what it needs. Clips made with the body moved by the pelvis rather than the root (many pose packs: the Sims root left standing while the hips lie, kneel or sit) now keep that movement, so both bodies lie on the bed, kneel or sit apart instead of standing in one spot; an author's offsets are scaled as the clips themselves are (by the Sims skeleton's own size), so a raised or shower role stands where the author put it. Animations converted before this are counted in **Manage library** (“Re-import to update N animations”): **Import WickedWhims** again, or the same files, converts them anew and replaces them, names, categories and the Use in scenes switch kept. Tested on the desktop against the WickedWhims package's own clips and tuning (positions measured, not looked at); not run on a phone.

The imported Sims rig supplies the source bone hierarchy, bind transforms, and animation curves. The retargeter converts mapped tracks to Warcraft's coordinates and bind pose so Warcraft mesh skinning stays intact. Source clip timing is retained. Ordinary wandering and furniture commands use Warcraft animations.

**Partner animations in the library.** Two kinds of two-person animation are offered, one list for both: an author's performances (WickedWhims and similar packs: the roles, offsets, stages and cycle counts from the pack's own tuning, kept on its clips) and partner animations the importer pairs itself (category **Partner**, saved as one entry with both actors' clips). The importer pairs a package's clips that no author's performance already covers (packages of up to 64 clips; larger ones are converted one clip at a time), in this order: from the package's animation tuning (an entry that lists `animation_actors_list` with each actor's clip name, offset and angle), from the clip names (the same name ending `_x`/`_y`, `_a`/`_b`, `_0`/`_1`, `actor0`/`actor1`, `_xx`/`_yy` or `_male`/`_female`), or from two clips with different actor names, the same length and roots that start apart. Such a pair keeps where each actor stands and faces from the pair's shared origin (one horizontal scale for both, so they stay where the clips put them), whether it loops, the furniture it was made for, and the sound names its clips' events give, with their times. Clips that look like half of a pair are listed on the review under **Clips without a partner**: tap one for actor A and one for actor B, then **Pair these two**. **Animation library → Manage library** has a **Partner** tab to rename, recategorize, turn off for scenes or delete them. Limits: only two-actor animations (three or more are reported); clips whose lengths differ by more than a frame are not paired; prop and object clips, hand IK targets and face curves are not converted; sounds are names only (the audio is not in these packages). Real two-actor packages have not been tested: the pairing follows the format's tools and documentation and synthetic test files.

**Partner animations in the scene.** Every two-person animation plays the same way, whether you chose it with **Play animation**, sent an NPC with **Send to partner**, or two NPCs began one by themselves: both walk to their places around a shared spot, the one waiting turns to face the one coming, and both clips start on the same simulation step and play on one clock, cycle after cycle; the animation's sound events play at their times (Partner sounds, below). By themselves: an idle NPC (not on furniture, not carrying out your order) whose cooldown has passed may go to another idle NPC within six yards (the nearest; a random one of equally near) and play a partner animation picked at random from the enabled ones that fit somewhere near them (on the floor, or on a free bed, couch, chair or table within ten yards where both bodies land: Furniture, below); with none that fits, both play one Warcraft emote their models share (wave, cheer, dance, laugh, bow, kiss, applaud or salute) facing each other. A looping one repeats 1 to 4 times or for 6 to 25 seconds; then both step apart and wait 20 to 60 seconds before doing it again. In **Choose objects**, **Partner** sets how often: Off, Sometimes (the default) or Often, and the row under it counts the partner animations available. **Send to partner** (select an NPC, press it, tap another NPC) sends the selected NPC to start one with that NPC. The cast bar shows "With <name>" for both, and the line under the orders names the animation. Your orders to either NPC and **End scene** interrupt it; the other NPC goes back to idle at once. In the live scene (and the placement ghost) every NPC stands as tall as a human of their sex, whatever the race, so the Sims clips and the pairs line up; dwarves (Dark Iron and Earthen too) and gnomes (Mechagnomes too) keep their own size, as the creator shows them, and a gnome stands nearer its partner by its size (pair slots, its steps, the distance it talks from) with its speech bubble at its own head: its hands meet a human partner's across the floor but stay about 0.8 yards lower, since lifting it would leave it in the air (measured on the game's models by `engine-test/humanscale_test`, not seen on a phone). The simulation is unit-tested on the desktop with fake animations (`simulation/PartnerActivityTest`, `PerformanceTest`); the screens are compiled by CI only, and none of it has run on a phone.

**Switch positions and the preview before Play.** A two-person animation has role A (its first actor: the one who starts it) and role B (the partner), each with its own clip, place and facing, and the app can't tell which body each was made for. So whenever you start one with a partner (**Play animation** with a partner chosen, **Invite who?**, or a piece of furniture's **Play animation**), nothing plays at first: a **Ready to play** panel opens at the bottom of the screen, naming who plays **A** and who plays **B** (each with their sex), and the scene above holds still with the two drawn where they would start, in their roles' first frames, the camera framed on them (cinematic mode's director waits meanwhile). **Switch positions** gives each the other's role (clip, place and facing) and the scene shows it at once; **Play** starts it as shown (the walk to their places, then the clips, then the cinematic director as before) and **Cancel** (or Back) leaves everything as it was. The choice is kept per animation (saved on the phone), so the next time it opens the way you left it, and the browser's own preview, queues and the scene flow's stages play in the roles kept. While a two-person animation plays, a **Switch positions** button at the top right (left of the camera's buttons, on their row; the **Cinematic** button sits in the top bar, left of the feed and the gear, so neither covers another control) starts it again from its first part with the two turned round, on the same spot and furniture, each eased onto their new place. A solo animation has no switch. On furniture, the panel's roles are kept as set (the pick of the nearer place for each is not made). Tested on the desktop: the swap (clips, places, facings, people, double switch, sizes), the kept choice, the panel's states, the preview's places and the switch mid-play (`simulation/RoleSwitchTest`), and a still of the Lion's Pride bed pair before and after the switch drawn by `engine-test/film`; the panel, the held scene and the button are compiled by CI only, and none of it has run on a phone.

**Furniture.** Each animation is read for what it needs (`anim/Requirements`): from its bodies over the clip (the pelvis's height, the torso's tilt, which side faces up, the thighs and knees, where the feet and hands are) and its names and tuning (bed, sofa, chair, counter; lie, sit, kneel), whether each actor stands, sits, lies, kneels, leans or is on all fours, on the floor or on a bed, a seat or a top, and at what height. The result is kept beside the library (`files/anims/requires.cache`, made again when an animation is converted again; the importer reads it with the Sims clip's own names and slot targets). Every scene opens in this one live screen (there is no separate preset-scene screen), so both are there from the first moment the scene is shown. A one-line hint ("New: Build mode...", with **Got it**) shows under the cast until it is dismissed or build mode has been entered once (remembered on the phone). While it is on, the Build bar replaces the action bar at the bottom: that is the sign it is on. Pure logic: `BuildEntry`, tested in `BuildEntryTest`; the layout is compiled by CI only.

The HUD's **Build** chip (beside Gear on and On/Off; it replaces the old Move chip, and Move mode is part of it) turns on build mode, in any scene. The scene never stops for it: the NPCs go on with their day and their animations while you work. **No animation ends while it is on.** Whatever plays keeps looping until you leave Build mode: a pair's or a solo play (its natural end, the End part and the return to standing are held; a staged play loops the part it is on, on the same clock, so it never pops), a queued order, an NPC's own next pick (walks, chats, sitting down, partner animations, the life simulation's choices and invitations) and the cinematic auto-play (below) all wait. One flag does it, `holdAnimations`, fed by Build mode being on (`SceneSimulation.holdAnimations`, `CinematicAutoplay.hold`, `BuildEntry.holdAnimations`); a walk stands still, a conversation stops where it is. Your own orders (Stop, Play, Switch positions) still act. Leaving Build mode lets everything go on where it was.

**Build mode works in cinematic mode.** They are no longer exclusive: pressing Build keeps cinematic mode (the 4:3 film frame, the light, the grade), turning cinematic on keeps Build mode. While both are on the director's camera steps aside for Build's orbit round the selection, auto-play stands on the current shot (nothing is changed, Next scene does nothing, the dwell does not run), and the HUD and the Build bar stay up (the interface never hides while Build mode is on). Leaving Build mode returns to normal cinematic mode: the director's camera comes back and auto-play goes on with the rest of the dwell. The Back button ends cinematic mode only when Build mode is off. In immersive mode the HUD stays up while it is on.

**The camera orbits round what you selected.** One finger drags the orbit round the pivot, two fingers pan it along the view and pinch to zoom; there is no free flight. A tap on anything (a prop of the place, even one that was never interactive, a piece of furniture, an NPC, a marker, the floor) selects it and makes it the pivot: the camera glides to centre it. The selected item gets a blue-white box round it (the placing ghost's colour).

**The axis gizmo (no free move).** What can be moved shows three arrows (**X** red: its front, **Y** green: its left, **Z** blue: up) and a gold ring. Drag an arrow to move it along that axis only, drag the ring to turn it; a drag that starts on a handle (within 24 dp) moves the thing, any other drag orbits. The steps: **Snap** cycles **1 cm**, **5 cm**, **10 cm** and **Fine** (no snap, a quarter of the finger's move, 1° turns); the ring turns in 15° steps. The panel shows x, y, z (yards, the place's frame) and the facing, with fields for exact values and **Set**. Every gesture is one step of **Undo** and **Redo** on the bar.

**The Build bar** (at the bottom, in the action bar's place): **Select**, **Marker**, **Move**, **Interact**, **Send**, Undo, Redo and **Done**. The panel at the side (under the camera's buttons, never over the middle where the gizmo is) shows what is selected and what the tool does with it.

- **Markers.** With **Marker**, pick a type (**Stand**, **Sit**, **Lie**, **Kneel**, **Pair origin**: a pair's shared origin, **Camera spot**) and tap the floor, or **Add marker** at the selection (an item's anchor, a pair's origin, a marker) or at the middle of the view. A marker is a small pin with a ring on the floor and an arrow for its facing, coloured by type; it is named ("Sit 2"), can be renamed, retyped, deleted and moved with the gizmo, and belongs to the item that was selected when it was made. The list shows yours and the shipped ones (**Go** selects one). **Show markers** shows them small outside build mode too.
- **Interact (the classifying).** Select any item and choose its class (**Bed/couch**, **Chair/seat**, **Table top**, **Floor/rug**, **Wall/stand**, **Other**), tick the animation classes it takes (**Lying**, **Sitting**, **Kneeling**, **Standing**, **Leaning**) and set its surface's height (from its box; − and + by 2 cm, **Box** for its top), then **Make interactive**. It takes effect at once in this scene: the item's anchor (where a body goes; move it with the gizmo, the Z arrow sets the surface) becomes its place and its **Play animation** window offers what its ticks allow. A prop that was not one of the scene's objects takes the place of the scene's bed (lying ticked or Bed/couch), chair (sitting or Chair/seat) or table (leaning, Table top or Wall/stand), so any prop can be made a bed and the "no bed found" cases go. Your items are kept in `files/build/items.json` (every place's) and win over the shipped file (`assets/item_overrides.json`), which wins over the furniture's own measure. **Hold NPC here (item report)** on one of the scene's objects is Move mode (below).
- **Moving a playing animation live.** Select an NPC who is playing an animation: the gizmo stands on its pair's shared origin, and moving or turning it moves both of them while they keep playing on the same clock (their contact and lifts are the clip's, kept). **Re-place** puts the pair back on its computed place.
- **Remember for races** (a race's own placement, not a separate animation). Under **Remember**, choose the scope (**Only this race pair**, **This race in this role (any partner)**, **This size class**: gnome-size, dwarf-size, human-size or tall) and the roles (A, B), then **Remember for gnome female** (or whichever): the correction is kept as a delta from the computed placement in the pair's own frame (x its front, y its left, z up, the turn), for this animation, the role, the race and sex (and the other's race for a pair), the furniture class it was on (bed, couch, chair, table, floor) and, for one NPC on an item, the item. From then on, whenever that race plays that animation in that role (the live scene, partner animations, cinematic auto-play, the Ready-to-play preview, after **Switch positions**: by role), the delta is laid on the computed placement; the animation itself is not changed. The most specific one wins (the exact pair, then the race in the role, then the size class), the phone's own (`files/build/race_offsets.json`) before the shipped ones (`assets/race_offsets.json`). **Reset to default placement** forgets this animation's for these races. The **Race offsets** list (in Send, and in **Options → Mods**) shows each one with **Delete** and **Reset all**.
- **Send.** **Save and send** keeps the session's markers, items, moved pairs and race placements on the phone (`files/build/<place>.json`) and sends them as one GitHub issue, `Build report: <place>`, labelled `build-report`, with your notes; the first time it asks whether reports may be sent (the same question as Move mode's). It goes the way item reports go: the same token, the queue (`files/build/queue/`) when offline, sent again when build mode opens or the phone comes online.

**What is sent:** the place (its id and name), the app's version and build, your notes, and a fenced JSON block (schema 2): `markers[]` (id, name, type, x, y, z, yawDeg, item), `items[]` (key, name, model, class, supports, surface, position, yawDeg, scale, box, anchor, slot), `pairs[]` (animationId, animationName, the roles' race and sex, the origin as computed and as moved: x, y, z, yawDeg, item) and `raceOffsets[]` (animationId, animationName, role A or B, race, sex, otherRace, furnitureClass, item, scope, sizeClass, dx, dy, dz, dyawDeg). Positions are in the place's own frame (locations.json's, yards). Nothing else: no account, device, file path or name of yours.

**Reading them (the developer):** `python3 tools/item_reports.py` reads the `build-report` issues with the `item-report` ones and writes three files: their items join `assets/item_overrides.json` (the same merge as item reports, with the commonest set of ticks as `supports`), their markers go to `assets/place_markers.json` (by place; markers of the same type and name merged to the median place and facing; with three or more, those over 0.35 yards or 45° off are left out and listed as outliers), their race placements to `assets/race_offsets.json` (one per animation, role, race or size class, scope, furniture class and item: the median delta, outliers flagged the same way). `--markers-out` and `--race-out` choose other files. The app shows the shipped markers in build mode (dimmer than yours) and lays the shipped race placements on as it does yours. The secret **`WIFECRAFT_REPORT_TOKEN`** must be set for anything to be sent (below).

Pure and unit-tested on the desktop (`BuildModeTest`: the projection against the renderer's, the handles' hit, a drag's component along an axis, the snaps and the ring, the orbit round a pivot with its pitch and zoom limits, the data's JSON round trip, the store, the report and its request with a fake HTTP layer and the queue, the state machine from select through a drag to undo, redo and send, the ticks and the overrides' precedence, the race placements' scopes, precedence, frame maths and merge, a gnome and a human on the same animation with the roles switched, and a pair moved while it plays; `tools/test_item_reports.py`: the tool's parse and merge of hand-made build issues). The gizmo's and markers' drawing (engine/buildview.cpp), the gestures, the panels and the performance on a phone are compiled by CI only: none of it has run on a phone.

### Move mode and item reports (part of Build mode)

With an NPC controlled, in build mode select one of the scene's objects and press **Hold NPC here (item report)**: that is Move mode, the item mapper (its panel's **Done** lets go and the build panel comes back). Tap a piece of furniture in the room and a small window asks **What is this?**: **Chair/seat**, **Table top**, **Floor** or **Bed/couch** (the one the furniture's own measure gives is lit). The NPC is put there at once and held: on a seat sitting on its seat, on a bed lying on its lying place, at a table standing in front of it, on the floor standing in front; a tap on open floor holds them on that spot (Floor, no question). They stay until you tap another item, press **Done** (or **Done moving**, or Back), or give another order. The panel at the side nudges them in the item's own frame (up is the item's front): the arrows (held, they repeat), the drag pad in the middle, ⟲ ⟳ to turn, **Up** and **Down**, **Fine** (2 cm, 5°) or **Coarse** (10 cm, 15°), and **Pose** where the class has more than one (a seat: Sit or Stand; a top or the floor: Stand or Sit; a bed: Lie or Sit). The held place is shown under it (x, y, z yards from the item, the facing in degrees from its front).

**Report…** takes a screenshot of the scene (without the HUD), asks once whether reports may be sent (*This sends item data to the developer's repository*), then opens the report: an optional note and the checkboxes **Fits two people**, **Too small**, **Wrong facing**, **Floating**, **Clips**, **Other**, **Share screenshot** and **Send report**. The report becomes a GitHub issue on this repository, titled `Item report: <class> <item> <model> @ <place>` and labelled `item-report`; the panel then says **Report sent: #123**. Without a connection it waits in `files/reports/queue/` and goes by itself when the phone is online (while a scene is up), the next time Move mode opens, or with **Send waiting reports** in Options; a refusal says why, with **Retry**.

**What is sent:** the place (its id and name), the item (its object and model file id, its position and facing in the place's frame, its box in yards, its surfaces and their heights, the furniture fit's own measurements: its kind, surface and places, and the class that measure gives, to compare), the class you chose, the NPC's race, sex and size (and a name only for the game's named characters, never one of your own NPCs'), the held place in the item's frame (x, y, z yards, facing degrees), the pose and its game animation, your note and checkboxes, and the app's version and build number. Nothing else: no account, device, file path or name of yours. The issue's body carries a summary and the same as a fenced JSON block (schema 1). **The screenshot is never sent** (GitHub's API takes no images in an issue): it stays in `files/reports/shots/` (640 pixels wide, at most 200 KB), and **Share screenshot** hands it to Android's share sheet if you want to pass it on yourself.

**Your mappings take effect at once.** Each report is also kept on the phone (`files/reports/mapped.json`): the item is taken as the class you gave it (a table called a seat is a seat), your held place becomes its first place for that class at its height (the surface a sit or a lie rests on is moved to that height), and its **Play animation** window offers only what belongs on that class (a seat the seated ones, a top those at a table or counter, the floor the floor's, a bed those on a bed or couch and the lying ones). **Options → Mods → Mapped items** lists what you classified (the item, the place, the class and pose, Reported: #n, Waiting to send or Not sent), each with **Delete** (it stops changing the item on this phone; a sent issue stays). Items nobody mapped behave as before. Precedence: your own mapping, then the shipped file, then the furniture's own measure.

**Setting it up (the repository's owner):** the app opens issues with a token built into the release by CI. Add a repository secret named **`WIFECRAFT_REPORT_TOKEN`**: a fine-grained personal access token limited to this repository with **Issues: Read and write** (nothing else; the label is made on the first report). `.github/workflows/build-wifecraft.yml` passes it to Gradle (`-PwcReportToken`), which puts it in `BuildConfig.WC_REPORT_TOKEN`; the issues go to `WC_UPDATE_REPO` (this repository). Without the secret the build sends nothing: reports are kept on the phone (the panel says so) and go once a build with the token is installed.

**Reading the reports (the developer):** `python3 tools/item_reports.py` (standard library only; the token from `$GITHUB_TOKEN`, `$GH_TOKEN` or `gh auth token`; `--repo OWNER/NAME`, `--dry-run`, `--from-json FILE` for a saved list of issues, `--state all`) lists the open `item-report` issues, checks each JSON block (bad ones are named and skipped), and writes `android/app/src/main/assets/item_overrides.json`, keyed by `place/object/model`: the class most reports gave (a tie: the newest), and for each class reported the median held place and facing, the commonest pose, the surface's height, the notes and the issue numbers; with three reports or more of a class, those over 0.35 yards or 45° from the median are left out and listed as outliers (the same rules as the app's `ItemOverrides.merge`). It prints a summary (each item, where the measure differed, the outliers, the skipped issues). It never closes or edits an issue: close them once their item is merged, commit the file, and the next release ships it.

Pure and unit-tested on the desktop (`ItemMapperTest`: the report's JSON and its round trip through the issue body, the frame maths for turned and scaled items, the state machine from tap to reported or queued, the queue and its retry, the request with a fake HTTP layer, the answers' classification, the merge's medians and outliers, the lookup's precedence, the overrides' effect on the furniture, the picker's filter per class, the hold in the scene); the tool was run on a hand-made list of issues. The GitHub call itself can't be tried without the token, and the panel, the windows, the screenshot, the share sheet and the hold's drawing are compiled by CI only: nothing of it has run on a phone.

The character creator uses it too (Create a Character, above).

The menus play the title music of The War Within (MUS_110_MAIN_TITLE); a live scene plays its place's own music and ambience from the game's tables (the Stormwind Embassy: its room's ZoneMusic 1929, six tracks one after another with a short gap, and SoundAmbience 569's day or night loop), crossfading between them. ## Time of day

A live scene has a world clock (`simulation/WorldClock.kt`): a real second is a game minute at x1, so a day takes 24 real minutes (8 at x3, 4 at x6; Paused stands still), counted in days from Day 1, its hours named Midnight, Night, Dawn, Morning, Noon, Afternoon, Dusk and Evening. A scene starts in the morning (8:00) unless another start hour is chosen; the clock is saved and put back as JSON, and the life simulation follows it (`LifeTime`). Its settings (speed, start hour, and **Day/night cycle**: off keeps the start hour's light and sound while the clock still runs) are `ClockSettings`, on Options' **Time** page (`Settings.clock`): the speed there is where each scene starts, the start hour is where a new cast's first day begins (a cast played before goes on from its own saved time), and the cycle applies at once. The live screen drives the clock every frame on the GL thread, hands the hour to the light (`LightFeed`, `SceneView.setTimeOfDay`) and, four times a second, to the sound (`Audio.timeOfDay`); the life simulation follows it (`LifeControl.setTimeSource`), and its speed buttons go through life to it (`LifeSim.onSpeed`: Pause, x1, x3, x6 are `LifeSpeed` PAUSE, NORMAL, FAST, FASTEST, the same multipliers: `LifeHud.lifeSpeed`/`clockSpeed`).

The light follows the hour (`SceneView.setTimeOfDay`, cpp `engine/daylight.cpp`) with the game's own day for each place: its map light's LightData keyframes (`dayCycle` in `assets/locations.json`, from `tools/locations.py CACHE --day-cycle`), sampled between keyframes and round midnight with no jump: an orange dawn at 6, the day's light, a golden and then violet dusk from 17, a blue moonlit night from 20:30. The sun crosses from the east at 6 through the south at noon to the west at 18; by night the light comes from the moon in the north. At noon the colours are those the scene had before (only the sun's direction is the hour's). Inside, a room keeps its own baked light (the game bakes lamps and windows into its vertex colours): by night it is scaled to about two thirds and warmed, which reads as lamplight but can't light a lamp or darken a window by itself; what is seen through its doors and windows (the outside's light, sky and fog) changes with the hour. Lanterns' and windows' glowing textures stay lit at night. Checked in desktop renders of the Embassy outside and inside and the Lion's Pride bedroom at 6, 9, 12, 17, 19, 22 and 2 o'clock (`engine-test`: `time HOURS`; `build/daylight_test` checks the cycles); not seen on a phone.

**Cinematic light.** Cinematic mode's look is the place's own light made low-key, not one warm look laid over everything (cpp `engine/daylight.h`: `cineProfile`). Each frame it is worked out from the place's light at the hour (the map light's ambient, sun or moon, sky and fog; inside, the room's own ambient and baked light, with a little of the day through its windows) and from the practical lights standing near (`cinematic.practicals` in `assets/locations.json`: candles, torches, braziers, lanterns and lamps, which flicker, and crystals and arcane lamps, which don't; found by their model's file name by `tools/locations.py --cinematic=LISTFILE`). The profile gives the people a key from a low side angle in the place's colour (pulled toward the sun by day, the moon by night, or toward the practicals inside) and a rim from behind, deepens the shadows, and grades the picture toward the place's hue (lift, gamma, gain, a gentle S-curve, saturation, vignette). So a cold room stays cold: the Brill house is a moonlit blue-violet with its candles showing only as a warm rim and a flicker; the Lion's Pride stays warm, its key the colour of its flames; a daylit exterior gets a sun key and a soft grade, a night one a moon key and a deeper grade; a place without a flame (Bloodhoof's longhouse, Dolanaar) doesn't flicker. Limits keep it in hand: no colour cast past a chroma limit, black never washed out and the shadows never crushed, highlights never blown, a person's light kept between a floor and a ceiling, and a place that is already dark isn't made darker. The profile eases over about a second when the camera passes from a room to the outside or the hour moves on, and changes at once when the clock jumps. Options' Cinematic **Lighting** (0 to 100 %) scales every difference from neutral (0: the light as it is) and fades in and out with the mode. A place can set any part of it by hand (`cinematic.override.inside`/`outside` in its entry: `key`, `keyGain`, `rim`, `rimGain`, `lift`, `gamma`, `gain`, `contrast`, `saturation`, `vignette`, `flicker`, `hue` and the rest; kept when the tool runs again). Unit-tested on the desktop (`engine-test/daylight_test`: identity, limits, continuity over the day, the blend, the key's direction, the JSON, every place at every half hour; `mood_test`), and seen in desktop renders of all eleven places with two clothed people, off, the former warm look and the new one, inside at 8 and 22 and outside at 8, 18 and 22. Not seen on a phone; a room's baked light can still only be dimmed and tinted as a whole.

**Zone mood.** On top of that, cinematic mode takes the colour of the zone itself: the light the game draws at the place (`tools/zone_light.py CACHE_DIR` reads the game's `ZoneLight`, `ZoneLightPoint`, `Light`, `LightParams` and `LightData` tables from wago.tools at the app's build, picks each place's light as the game does: the zone light polygon it stands in, else a point light whose falloff reaches it, else the map's default, and writes every hour's ambient, sun, sky top and middle, fog and end-fog colours and fog distance, and the sun's glow, the clouds', the two sky bands' and the horizon and ground ambient colours, to `assets/zone_light.json` and to each entry's `cinematic.zone`). Brill takes Tirisfal's own zone light (a mauve ambient, a dim blue sun, a dark teal sky and a deep purple fog, the same all day); Goldshire and the Embassy Elwynn's and Stormwind's, Dolanaar Teldrassil's violet, Razor Hill and Sen'jin Durotar's dusty reds, Bloodhoof Mulgore's, Thunderbrew Dun Morogh's, Gadgetzan Tanaris', Telaar Nagrand's; Fairbreeze stands in no Eversong zone light at this build, so it takes the Outland map's default (a warm gold). The location's ambient, sun, sky and fog are pulled toward the zone's (at a brightness between the two, so night stays night), the fog comes nearer where the zone's is thick (Tirisfal's to about 50 yards; never past the area's edge), the grade splits the picture by brightness: a haze of the fog's colour in the blacks, the sky's middle and its clouds' colour in the shadows, the ambient in the mid-tones and the sun's glow in the highlights (Tirisfal: purple blacks, green-teal shadows, mauve mid-tones, pale green highlights; warm hues such as skin and candle-lit wood take little of the shadows' colour), and a room's own light is white-balanced part of the way toward the zone (its candles and hearths stay the warm part). People keep their skin: the light on them keeps its brightness with its colour held within a limit. Rooms stay moody but readable: a dark room's ambient is raised to a floor in its own colour (the zone's under a zone mood, by day a little of the sky's through its windows) and the frame's exposure goes up with a soft shoulder (black and white stay put), so faces and furniture read while the candles and hearths stay the warm part; a place's `cinematic.exposure` (a number, or `{"inside": n, "outside": n}`, 0.5 to 2) tweaks it by hand (Razor Hill's room 0.9, Brill's yard 1.15). Options' Cinematic **Zone mood** (0 to 100 %, default 70 %; 0 changes nothing) sets how far, and **Mood boost** exaggerates the zone's colours (more saturation and contrast) for a stylised look. Tested on the desktop (`engine-test/zonelight_test`: every place's zone read and the same as `zone_light.json`, continuous over the day, zone mood 0 exactly the look without it, the tone limits for every place, hour, side, strength and boost, skin hue and brightness against the look without it, Tirisfal's mauve, teal and purple, its green shadows and highlights; `daylight_test`: every room at every half hour, with and without the zone mood, a mid surface graded at least 0.12 and at most 0.50 and never under 0.6 of the look without it) and in desktop renders of Brill (house and outside), Goldshire, Dolanaar, Razor Hill, Telaar and Gadgetzan at 8, 19 and 22, the former look against the new one and the boost. Not seen on a phone.

The sound follows it too (`audio/TimeOfDaySound.kt`, played by `Audio.timeOfDay`): from 20:00 to 5:30 the Embassy's ambience crosses over eight seconds to its night loop (SoundAmbience 569's night kit; its day kit by day) while its music plays on, a little quieter by night (70 %, eased over dusk and dawn); now and then (every 25 to 70 seconds) one of the place's ambience flavour sounds plays (SoundAmbienceFlavor: for the Embassy the high winds of kit 130139), and Stormwind's bell (kit 6594, belltollalliance.ogg) tolls the hour at 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 and 21 (as many strokes as fit before the next). The bedrooms have no sound entry yet, so no day and night sound either; nothing is made up where the tables have none (there are no rooster or cricket kits for these places). Not heard on a phone.

## Immersive mode

A live scene opens immersive (Options > **Immersion**; `simulation/Immersion.kt`, `ImmersiveSettings` kept as `Settings.immersive`): no HUD at all until you tap the view. A tap brings back a quiet one for a few seconds (**Controls stay**, 2 to 10 s, 4 by default; held while a window or menu is open; every tap keeps it a little longer; the first tap only shows it, it picks nothing): the cast frames with their names only, the camera and Cinematic buttons, the action bar without Actions, and no counters or numbers anywhere (the "Stage 2 · Loop 3/5" line is gone; only what the scene answers is shown). The Sims layer (need bars and rings, mood gems, thought clouds, the clock, the feed, the radial and Actions menus) is behind one switch, **Show life HUD**, off by default; the life simulation keeps running underneath either way. Updating moves everyone to immersive mode once (the life HUD off); every other option is kept, and after that your own choice stays.

**A flow, not a menu.** With an NPC controlled and a partner chosen, the soft controls (a shallow arc of plain words at the right, quieter than the action bar) offer **Closer** and **Continue**. Continue starts the flow (`simulation/SceneFlow.kt`): the lead-in (they come together, face each other and share a slow kiss, the game's own emote), undressing (a piece at a time, about a second apart, alternating between them, in a natural order: cloak, helm, shoulders, gloves, bracers, boots, belt, legs, tabard, chest, shirt; each piece re-dresses the NPC through the same dresser without that slot and plays one of the game's cloth movement sounds; Options' **Keep on** chips say what stays on for everyone, `Settings.keepOn` holds a list per NPC; **Undress slowly** off skips it), the stages (the author's chain from its first stage when the library has one, queued as Queue stages does, else another two-person animation, else a partner animation; your repeat options apply), the afterglow (they stop, and with a bed in the room the lead lies down on it) and rest (the life simulation's Sleep, with it on). Each part moves on with **Continue**, or by itself with **Auto flow** (the lead-in 9 s, the stages when their queue has played, the afterglow 40 s). During the stages the controls are **Slower**, **Faster**, **Closer**, **Change position** (the queue's next stage, or the Play window with the partner animations first), **Continue** and **End** (which also dresses them again). Slower and Faster move the moment's intensity, not the clips' speed (the engine has no playback rate for imported clips yet). The long-press radial and Actions stay for the life menus, with Show life HUD on.

**Body language** (`cpp/engine/motionlayer.h`): every NPC in a live scene breathes, shifts their weight a little and tilts their head now and then, and looks at whoever they are with (their partner or the one they talk to; in the flow, each other): the neck and head turn within 60° either way, 20° up and 26° down, eased over about a third of a second, given up for someone behind them, and given up to the clip where a clip turns the head itself (its Head and Neck keys turning more than about 9° fade it out by 20°). It is laid over whatever pose the clip or emote made, as small turns of the posed bones about their own joints: nothing below the waist is turned, so the feet never move. The breathing's rate (12 to 30 a minute) and depth follow an intensity from 0 to 1 (`IntensityModel`): 0.2 standing about, 0.3 a solo animation, a two-person one from 0.4 at its first stage up 0.07 a stage to 0.85, a start a little under its loop, the loops climbing with each pass and with time, an end easing back; the lead-in 0.3, undressing 0.4, the afterglow 0.15; Slower and Faster move it 0.08 a step. It rises over about 2.5 s and settles over 5 s, never a jump; the idle motions quieten as it rises. Options: **Breathing** and **Small movements** (percent) and **Eye contact**. Measured on the desktop on the game's human woman and man standing, at intensity 1 with both at 100%: the most anything moves in 30 s is the head 1.1 to 1.2 cm, the neck about 1 cm, the shoulders 0.6 to 0.8 cm, a hand 1.9 cm, the feet 0 (for a 170 cm body); eye contact turned the head about 45 to 49° toward someone 45° aside and never past its limits for targets all round (`engine-test/motionlayer_test FILES_DIR`). On a rendered still the breathing is too small to see; the head turns are plain.

**Sound that carries the mood.** The scene's intensity (its most intense person's) sets the mix through the same mixer: the music swells from 55% to full as it rises, the room's ambience recedes to 70%, slowly (one music bed's levels, not stems or other tracks: the game has no quiet and full layers of these tracks); the partner sounds follow it by **Voice intensity** (0: always their own volume; at 100% a quiet moment plays them at 35%). **Atmosphere sounds** plays a small fire's crackle under the scene (the game's `sound/doodad/campfiresmallloop.ogg`, file 565519) and, with **Rain outside**, light rain (`rainlightloop.ogg`, 538983); the cloth sounds are `sound/foley/cloth/10.0_foley_cloth_movementlong_01..07`. These are files of the game by their ids, fetched like the rest; none was made up. The partner sounds' playback rate is not changed.

**Atmosphere.** **Glowing motes** (and, by default, always in cinematic mode): 48 embers and dust motes drifting slowly up through the light round what the camera looks at, fading in and out, soft and additive under the cinematic grade (`cpp/engine/particles.h`; seeded, so the same every time). **Camera sway**: in cinematic mode the camera drifts a little as if held (at most about 3.5 cm at the eye, half at the target, from slow sines; `HandheldSway`). The light's flicker is cinematic mode's own. Depth of field and the other picture effects are Options' Graphics page (Graphics and performance, below).

Tested on the desktop only: the settings and their migration, the intensity, its easing, the HUD's timing, the soft controls, the mix numbers, the sway (`unit-tests/.../simulation/ImmersionTest`), the flow (`SceneFlowTest`), the motion layer's maths and the motes' determinism and bounds (`engine-test/motionlayer_test`), and stills of two clothed NPCs in the Lion's Pride bedroom with the layer off and on (their heads turn to each other) and with the motes in cinematic light (they are small and few). The screens, the flow's orders, the re-dressing and the sound are compiled by CI only; none of it has run on a phone, and how it feels is yours to judge: every layer has its switch or slider.

## Cinematic mode: the camera

While an animation plays, cinematic mode's camera uses a shot library made from the usual camera conventions for each kind
of position. Its research and per-position table are in `docs/cinematic-shots.md`; its data is
`assets/shot_library.json`. Each animation is sorted into a class: kissing, partner-facing lying, from behind, on top,
seated, standing, side by side, oral, manual, two people otherwise, or one person. The class comes from the animation's
names, or from its bodies' postures when no name says it (`simulation/ShotLibrary.kt`). Each class has 3 to 6 candidate
shots with weights: an establishing wide, profile and three-quarter mediums, low and top-down angles, face close-ups and
reactions, over the shoulder, a high POV-like view, and hip and hand details.

The director picks again when the animation or its stage changes. In **Classic film** it follows a cadence: a wide shot,
then medium, close and detail shots. It never picks the same shot twice running. It keeps the camera on one side of the
pair for the whole scene (the 180-degree rule), preferring the side they face and the side with more room. Face shots are
taken from in front of the face.

Each shot drifts on a seeded random walk round, up and in, within its own range. Every eye passes the director's
clearance tests: walls, furniture boxes, the floor and ceiling, no one blocking a face, and no furniture between the camera
and the action. The furniture is taken at its real measured shape: a piece's base up to its surface, its solid parts (a
headboard, posts, a chair's back) and, on a canopy bed, the canopy with its valance, a little wider than the bed. No camera
sits under a canopy or inside a piece's box, no sight line passes through a canopy or a solid part, and a canopy may take
no more than a sliver of the picture. An over-the-shoulder shot sits back and to the side of the near person, and is turned
down when their body would cover more than a quarter of the picture. The low hip detail of a partner on top is set just
over the bed or couch the pair is on, and is never used on the floor. A wide shot is brought in to fit the room (its walls
and ceiling); where the room is too small that way, it moves to the corner with the most room, as high as the ceiling
allows. The people's hip and head points come from the playing pair clip's own skeleton as the animation reader measured
it (its pelvis, where its head is, its posture), placed where the pair is played. With nothing playing, the director uses its
own mix of shots, as before.

Options > Cinematic adds three settings under **Shots during animations**:

- **Shot style**: Classic film, Intimate, Close-ups, Wide or Random.
- **Randomness**: how loosely the cadence and weights are followed, and how far each shot drifts.
- **Camera cuts**: on allows cuts between shots; off allows smooth moves only.

The existing settings keep working. Slow orbit only still orbits, and Cuts still cuts everything.

Tested on the desktop only. JVM tests (`ShotLibraryTest`, `CinematicShotRulesTest`) cover the classification, each class's
shots, the style weights, no repeats, the side kept, clearance, the drift's bounds, determinism, the canopy bed, the shoulder's
share of the picture, the low hip detail's height, the wide shots in small rooms and the hip and head points. Preview stills
were rendered with the desktop engine in the Lion's Pride Inn room, using synthetic clothed poses on the floor and on its
canopy bed (`tools/ShotPreviewRecorder.kt`).

Not yet checked:

- No phone test.
- No real animation packs. The hip and head points come from the animation reader's summary of a pair clip (one pelvis
  and head position for the whole clip, not each frame), and the head's height is an estimate by posture. Single clips,
  emotes and pairs the reader could not measure keep the rough points by activity; a body resting on a bed is taken to lie
  toward its facing.
- The canopy's drapes are not measured on their own: the canopy is taken as a slab over the whole bed, about a third of its
  rise deep, and its long sides as open where the measure found no solid part. On the Lion's Pride's canopy bed this
  leaves only wide and medium shots from beside the bed: close-ups and the low hip detail would need a camera under the
  canopy.

### Cinematic mode: auto-play and the 4:3 frame

**Auto-play.** With Options > Cinematic **Auto-play scene** on (the default), starting cinematic mode starts a scene: the
controlled NPC and their partner (else the flow's pair, else the first other person in the cast) play the installed partner
animations one after another (`simulation/CinematicAutoplay.kt`). Each one is chosen with a place: the bed, the couch, a chair,
the table or the open floor, wherever every one of its two bodies lands on something that takes its posture at its height
(`FurnitureFit`, for these two bodies' size: a dwarf or a gnome only where the existing rules let them). It plays, looping, for
25 to 90 seconds at the middle **Pace** (1.5 times as long at the slowest, half at the quickest), then the next: the same place
about half the time while it has something fresh, else another place. The same animation never plays twice running while
another can, the last four wait their turn, and one in the same posture as the last is less likely. The open floor is where the
pair stood, or a spot clear of the furniture. With nothing installed (or nothing that fits a place here) it plays the models'
own partner emotes and its panel says so; with one person nobody plays.

**Every change happens during a cut.** The picture goes to black (**Cut style**: a quick fade of 0.12 s, or a hard cut), the next
animation starts with both already on their places (no walk, no settle: `SceneSimulation.autoplay`), the camera cuts to a new
shot of it (`CinematicDirector.cutNow`: another size than the last, or the same size from at least 30 degrees round; never by a
move), and only then the picture comes back. The black lasts about 0.4 s: the engine's own 0.25 s crossfade of the pose has to
pass in it, so the change of pose is never seen (a hard cut is black just as long, without the fades). Within a place the camera
keeps its side of the pair (the 180-degree rule); at a change of place the side is chosen again. With **Show movement** on, a
change of place is walked in view and the cut comes as the next animation starts.

**The interface hides itself.** In cinematic mode with Build mode off, a screen not touched for 5 seconds hides all of the interface (every chip, bar, panel, button, hint and the Cinematic button: only the 3D view and the film frame stay) until the next touch, which shows it again and restarts the 5 seconds; that touch only shows it and is not a tap in the scene. Any finger down or moving keeps it up; it never hides while Build mode is on or a window or menu is open over the scene. Pure logic: `CinematicIdle` (clock injected), tested in `CinematicIdleTest`; the layout is compiled by CI only.

While the HUD is up, a small panel shows what plays and has **Next scene**, **Pause** (the clock stops), **Stay** (this
animation until pressed again) and a **Pace** slider for this scene. Leaving cinematic mode lets the last animation finish its pass.

**The frame.** Options > Cinematic **Picture**: **Auto (4:3)** (the default), **Wide (16:9)** or **Full screen**. In the mode the 3D
view itself is resized to the largest rectangle of that shape that fits the screen, centred, black round it (above and below on a
screen narrower than 4:3, such as a fold's open inner screen; at the sides on a wider one; on a 4:3 screen it is the whole screen),
so its picture, projection, effects and taps are the frame's and the bars take no tap (a tap on them only brings the HUD back).
**Letterbox** now draws thin film bars inside it (a 1.43:1 picture in Auto: about 3.4% top and bottom on a 4:3 screen; 1.9:1 in
Wide); Full screen keeps the old wide 2.39:1 bars.

Tested on the desktop only: `CinematicAutoplayTest` (synthetic bed, chair, table and floor with synthetic two-person clips: only
animations that fit each place, no repeat running and the recent ones held back, the dwell's bounds at every pace, the same seed
the same sequence, no furniture, no animations, nothing fitting, no partner, the order cover, swap, cut, reveal with the cut
always under full black, a refused change retried under the same cover, then the base animations, Pause, Stay, Next scene, Show
movement; the director's forced cut on six seeds, twelve changes each, always a cut to a new size or angle; the frame on eleven
screens from 4:3 to 20:9 both ways and the fold's screens; taps in and out of the frame; the thin bars; the settings' JSON). A
desktop render of the Lion's Pride room at 1440x1080 with the thin bars was looked at. Not run on a phone: how the resize of the
3D view feels when the mode starts, the panel and the black's timing on a real frame rate are unseen. Heuristic: the pace's
times, the place's chance to stay, the floor spot (not tested against walls), and auto-play plays each pair's loop (a staged
pack's start and end parts are not played by it); it does not undress anyone (the flow's Continue does). Speech bubbles, if shown
in the mode, are placed for the whole screen, not the frame.

## Life simulation

With **Life simulation** on (Options > Time; on by default: `Settings.lifeSimulation`; its HUD below shows only with Options > Immersion > **Show life HUD**; turned off in a running scene, needs stand still and NPCs choose as before), the live scene's NPCs live as in The Sims, in Warcraft's words (`simulation/LifeSim.kt` and its `Life*.kt` files; the screen's side is pinned in `simulation/LifeApi.kt`). It drives the same runtime as everything else (walking, sitting, resting on the bed, reading, talking, partner animations, emotes); it adds no second one.

- **Needs.** Seven bars from empty to full: Rested (energy), Well Fed (hunger), Refreshed (thirst), Company (social), Amused (fun), Clean (hygiene) and At Ease (comfort). They fall with game time (`LifeClock`, or the world clock when it drives it: a game minute a real second at x1, so a day is 24 real minutes; x3, x6 and Pause): about 19 waking hours to tire out, 12 to go hungry, 9 to thirst, so a day holds a night's sleep, about three meals and some company. What an NPC does fills them: sleeping in the bed (or a night on the couch when the bed is taken), napping on the couch, in a chair or, with nowhere to lie or sit, sat on the floor (the game's SitGround: the Embassy has no bed or seat), sitting, eating or drinking at the table (an inn meal: the game's Eat emote, bite after bite) or from the pack standing, reading at the study table or a bookshelf, dancing, freshening up, stretching, chatting. A conversation's lines are read in real time, so at x6 one can last hours of game time: it costs the needs at most 0.4 game hours of their fall however long it runs. A fireplace, a basin or a bookshelf is used where the room has the game's own model of one (locations.json "props", `simulation/LifeProps`): bookshelves at the Lion's Pride upstairs, the Brill house upstairs and the Telaar hut; no room has a fireplace or basin of the game's (no washing or warming up, only freshening up).
- **Traits.** Three each, the same every time for the same NPC (from their id, race and sex): Early Bird, Night Owl, Social, Loner, Hearty Appetite, Ascetic, Romantic, Playful, Bookish, Brave, Mischievous, Calm. Some lean lightly by race (dwarves love their food and ale, night elves keep the night, tauren and draenei are calm, gnomes read, goblins scheme, trolls play); sex never decides one. They change how fast needs fall, what an NPC likes doing and talking about, when they sleep (22 to 6; an early bird 21 to 5, a night owl 1 to 9) and who they get on with.
- **Moods and thoughts.** A mood from the needs and from what happened lately (Well Rested, Inspired by a Book, Shared a Laugh, Had an Argument...), a mood score, a thought bubble now and then ("I could use a drink.", "Is it nap time?", "Not a free bed in sight.") and a wish ("wants a hot meal", "wants a friend", "wants to hear a story").
- **Choosing.** When an NPC is free (not under your order), everything that can happen now is scored: how urgent the need is, what the thing gives, the traits, the distance, the hour, a little chance, and less of what they did lately; the best wins. A need under a fifth is seen to first (a quick meal or drink before a night's sleep) and company waits for it; nobody eats or drinks above two thirds full, mealtime or not. What it's for is cut short when the need is full or when another becomes urgent (under 0.08) and lower than the one being filled by 0.15, never in its first 4 real seconds (so two low needs never take turns cutting each other off), and one begun on a need under a fifth runs until that need is 70% full (never your orders). A night's sleep runs to the sleeper's morning (or full energy by day), broken only by hunger or thirst gone urgent after half a night; up rested, back to bed only under 55% energy for six hours. A pressing need (not company) makes an NPC leave a conversation of their own politely ("I should get something to eat."). What can't be had (a bed when it's taken) is wanted aloud in a thought, not faked. Getting up from furniture, an NPC steps off its floor spot onto free floor beside it, and nobody walks to where another stands to talk or wander (half a yard apart). The greetings follow the hour ("Morning, ...", "Evening, ...", "Still up?" at night).
- **Relationships.** Each pair has a friendship in the game's reputation standings (Hated, Hostile, Unfriendly, Neutral, Friendly, Honored, Revered, Exalted) and a romance (Spurned, Stranger, Acquaintance, Flirting, Sweethearts, Romantic, Soulbound). Chats (their topic suiting both or not, and both moods), jokes, compliments, flirting, gifts, hugs, dances and time sitting together raise them; arguments and refusals lower them; apart, they drift back slowly. By themselves NPCs hug and dance from Friendly, kiss from Sweethearts and play the library's other (intimate) pairs from Romantic; the one asked may refuse (a line in a bubble and a small cost). **Play animation** and **Send to partner** are never held back. The Chatter and Partner settings still apply: Off means NPCs don't start those by themselves.
- **The feed.** What happened ("Jaina went to bed.", "Thrall and Valeera became friends.") in a list of the last forty.
- **Your interactions.** From an NPC: Rest, Eat, Drink, Read, Dance, Wash, Wander; to another: Chat, Tell a joke, Compliment, Hug, Flirt, Kiss, Argue, Invite to sit, Dance together, Give a gift. They are your orders; the other accepts or refuses and the answer is shown. One asked while carrying out another of your orders is asked again as soon as they're free (for 20 seconds: "Elena is busy. Fizzwick will ask again in a moment."). A partner animation on furniture that can't start says why ("They can't reach the bed from where they stand."): its places beside the furniture are tried farther out, round its other sides and at its own floor spot first (the Lion's Pride's canopy bed is bigger than a usual one). Each NPC's needs can be frozen.
- **Saving.** The whole life (clock, needs, traits, moods, relationships, the feed) saves as JSON per cast (`files/life/` + `LifeSim.fileName`, the world clock beside it as `clock_<same>.json`, each written whole or not at all) every half minute and when the scene ends, and comes back with the scene.
- **On screen** (`ui/LifeHud.kt`, pure and unit-tested; `ui/LifeOverlay.kt`, `ui/LifePanels.kt`). Over each NPC's head a mood gem, WoW's quest-marker diamond in the mood's colour (green happy, yellow content, orange a need, red irritable, lonely or sad, blue bored or inspired), glowing and bobbing gently (still with the phone's animations off), hidden while they speak. Now and then a thought cloud (pale, with puffs trailing to the head: never mistaken for the dark speech bubbles) with the game's own icon for it (a mug, bread, sleep, a book, a drum, a valentine, the sun and moon, water...: `ui/LifeIcons.kt`, item and spell icons by their file ids from the listfile, a drawn glyph until they're fetched or if they can't be): at most three at once, one per NPC, each up a few seconds, a rest after it, never the same words twice running, the controlled NPC's first. Each party frame rings the NPC's plate with their seven needs (red to green) and carries their gem (dimmer while they're off screen) and, beside the controlled NPC, a heart (Flirting or more), linked rings (Friendly or more) or crossed blades (below Neutral); its line reads "Happy · Reading". A tap on the plate opens their **Life** window (Needs: mood, wish, what coloured it lately, seven reputation-style bars, a long press on one says what fills it, Freeze needs; Relationships: each friendship as the game's reputation bar in its standing's colour and the romance on a rose bar, closest first; Traits). **Hold** an NPC (or its frame) for the radial menu: your own Rest, Eat, Drink, Read, Dance, Wash, Wander, or, with someone controlled, Chat, Tell a joke, Compliment, Hug, Flirt, Kiss, Argue, Invite to sit, Dance together, Give a gift to the one held; hold a piece of furniture for what it gives (and its Play animation window). The action bar's **Actions** opens it too (with the partner when there is one). What can't be had is greyed and says why when tapped; a request that may well be refused is marked. The answer comes as a short toast (green tick or red cross). The top bar shows the world clock (sun or moon, the time, "Day 2 · Morning"); a tap gives Pause, x1, x3, x6 for this scene. The book beside the gear opens the last twenty events, a gold dot when something new happened. The scene menu has the controlled NPC's life and Freeze. Taps that controlled an NPC or chose the partner do exactly as before; the numbers refresh four times a second.

Tested on the desktop only (`unit-tests/.../simulation/LifeSimTest`): each need sends an NPC to what fills it, traits change the rates, friends are made by chatting and gate what pairs are picked, refusals cost, saving round-trips, the same seed lives the same life, and three game days of six NPCs in a room with one bed, one couch, a chair and a table (at x3) pass with nobody in one waking state over about 90 real seconds and everyone sleeping every night (in turns: some end the night tired), about 6 microseconds a simulation step for all six and at most one decision per NPC every 1.25 seconds. The screen's logic (gem colours, bar colours and words, reputation bars, the relation marks, the thought throttle, the radial menu's angles, spacing and hit test, the clock's face and speeds, every icon key having an icon) is unit-tested (`unit-tests/.../ui/LifeHudTest`) and the screens were type-checked against Compose Desktop; nothing of it has been compiled for Android here (CI builds it), the tuning has not been played by a person, and none of it has run on a phone.

## Partner sounds

Options > Sound > Partner sounds plays sounds with partner animations from the user's own files. ## Graphics and performance

Options > **Graphics** (`ui/GraphicsOptions.kt`; `graphics/GraphicsSettings.kt`, kept as JSON in the app's preferences under `graphics`) has preset chips (**Standard**, **High**, **Ultra**, **Custom**), the **Target frame rate** (60, 90 or 120; 60 by default), **Adaptive quality** (on by default), **Show performance overlay**, a line saying what the governor has lowered now, **Test for 10 seconds** (average fps, the 95th-percentile frame time, late frames), and a switch or slider for each effect. Moving one makes the preset Custom (or the preset it now matches). **Standard draws exactly as before**: with every effect off the frame goes straight to the screen (checked pixel for pixel against the previous build's render). High and Ultra turn on:

- **Render scale** (60 to 125 %; Ultra 125 %, supersampled) with contrast-adaptive sharpening on the resample (**Sharpen**, and more by itself below 100 %).
- **Anti-aliasing**: 2x or 4x MSAA on the scene's target (multisampled renderbuffers, resolved by a blit).
- **Texture filtering**: 2x to 16x anisotropic (GL_EXT_texture_filter_anisotropic) on every texture the scene loads and on composed skins; every texture loaded from the game is mipmapped. **Texture detail** (Standard, High, Full) takes a larger mip of the game's file where it has one: each kind of thing's biggest texture (places 512, props 256, people 1024; composed skins are 2048 whatever it is) times 1, 2 or 4, for what is loaded after the change (High in Ultra; Full is the Options page's alone). Each doubling can take up to four times the memory of what it enlarges; measured with the harness's `texmem`: the Lion's Pride bedroom with two people 23.1 MB at Standard, 25.1 MB at High (the people's textures), 25.1 MB at Full, and Goldshire outside 22.0, 24.0 and 24.0 MB: no file in either place has a mip larger than High's caps, so Full adds nothing there and helps only where the game's files are bigger (4096 people, 1024 and 2048 places and props).
- **Tone curve** on a half-float (RGBA16F, else RGBA8) picture: **Filmic** leaves everything under the brightest light as it was (skin tones kept) and rolls the highlights off; **ACES** is the fitted ACES curve with mid-grey kept (deeper shadows); **Exposure** in stops.
- **Bloom**: a soft threshold and a dual-filter (Kawase) pyramid from half size down to 1/32; subtle (windows, candles, the sky).
- **Depth of field**: each pixel's circle of confusion from its depth, zero over the band from the nearest to the farthest person near the camera's target (feet to head, a little either side: the pair stays sharp), a 24-tap disc gather at half size that keeps a sharp near thing from taking the blur behind it; **Aperture** sets how shallow. Off in the creator and the placement editor unless **Depth of field in the creator** is on.
- **Ambient occlusion** (half size, from the depth with normals from its neighbours; 6, 10 or 16 samples by **Occlusion quality**; a depth-aware blur) and **Contact shadows** (a short march toward the key light in the depth), both weighted to the light's ambient share.
- **Shadows**: the key light's shadow map (the sun or moon; in cinematic mode its key) over a box 2.6 yards round the camera's target, 1024 px, or 2048 px with **Soft shadows** (PCSS-lite: the penumbra widens with the blocker's distance), sloped bias and a normal offset. In a room only the people cast (the room's own light is baked into it); outside, the walls, props and ground near them cast too. Applied in screen space from the depth (the lit picture darkened by the key light's share), so the scene's own shaders are unchanged.
- **Skin shading** (wrap light, a warm scatter at the light's edge, a soft Fresnel sheen and highlight) and **Hair highlights** (Kajiya-Kay, the strands taken as running down) on the people's skin and hair textures (types 1 and 6; the composed skin includes clothes painted on it, so they get it too); the Eve body and cloth flaps keep the plain light.
- **Eye highlights**: the game's people have eyes of their own (geoset 3301, texture type 19, weighted to the L_Eye and R_Eye bones), a flat lens whose normals all face forward, so the eyeball is taken as a sphere round its bone's pivot (its centre in every model checked: human women and men, a draenei and a night elf woman, a dwarf and a goblin man) and given a small sharp catchlight and a broad wet sheen. The catchlight sits on the key light's side of the point that faces the camera and a little under it, where the lids leave it seen (as a film's eye light puts it), so it shows whichever way the key comes from, brighter as the key faces the eye.
- **Thin-skin tint**: ears, the nose, the lips and the fingers lit through, warm: a wider wrap, the warm scatter, and the key light shining through toward the eye when it is behind them. What is thin comes from the flesh profile (`flesh.h`, measured once per look): in the face, what sticks out past the head's measured flesh in front (nose, lips) or at the sides (ears), in the head's own frame as it is posed; in the hands, past the middle of the hand's measured length (the fingers). Found on the HD skin's layout (a 2048 x 1024 material: the face its right half, the hands the band under the arms); a person of the old layout keeps the plain light there.
- **Skin sheen follows intensity** with **Maximum sheen** (High 40 %, Ultra 60 %): each person's skin grows a wet sheen (glossier highlights, the room's light mirrored from above, the colour 10 % darker at full) from the moment's intensity (immersive mode's `IntensityModel`, eased): none below 0.35, the maximum at 0.9, eased again with a 6 s rise and a 12 s fall so it builds and lingers (`graphics/GraphicsSettings.kt`'s `SkinSheen`; `Native.sheen`, `Scene::setSheen`).
- **Light shafts** (the open sky, most round the sun, and a little of the brightest windows and lamps, blurred toward the sun's place on the screen in 32 jittered steps at quarter size, only with the sun ahead: rays through foliage and past roofs, a warm haze round the sun; subtle, not noisy, at Ultra's 50 %) and **Haze** (depth haze in the area's fog colour): Ultra only by default. The atmosphere's motes are Options' Immersion (Glowing motes).
- **Vignette** (High and Ultra, light), **Film grain** and **Chromatic aberration** (off). While cinematic mode's grade is on it draws its own vignette and grain and these are left to it; the chain hands it the finished picture (`engine/grade.cpp` grades after `engine/postfx.cpp`).

**Frames.** The scene view is paced on the screen's vsync (`graphics/Graphics.kt`: a Choreographer callback asks for a frame on every n-th vsync, 60 fps on a 120 Hz screen being every second one; the screen is also asked for the target's rate on Android 11 and later). Each pass is timed on the GPU where the driver has EXT_disjoint_timer_query. The **governor** (`graphics/Governor.kt`) takes the 95th percentile of the last half second of frames four times a second: over budget (frames missed, or the work over 97 % of the frame) for 1.5 s, it lowers one step, then waits 2 s; with 30 % to spare for 8 s it raises one back, and a step up that doesn't hold doubles that wait (up to 64 s). The steps, in order: render scale to 85 %, AO and shadow quality, bloom, depth of field, MSAA, light shafts and haze, render scale to 70 %, AO and shadows, the thin-skin tint, skin, hair and sheen shading, the eyes' highlights (a few pixels: the cheapest, last). Texture detail is memory, not frame time, and is never lowered. The phone's thermal status (moderate, severe, or headroom nearly gone) keeps one or two steps down. Adaptive quality off: the settings are drawn as set.

What each effect cost in the desktop harness (`engine-test`: `gfx`, `gfxtiming`, `gfxstats`; Mesa llvmpipe, software GL on a 4-core VM, 1280x720, the Lion's Pride bedroom with two people; the frame itself took about 72 ms): the chain with tone mapping alone about +13 ms, bloom +13, depth of field +19, AO +25 to +40, contact shadows +25, shadows +16, skin and hair 0, shafts and haze +9, 4x MSAA +61, render scale 70 % -5, 125 % +15, everything at once about 230 ms. **These are software-rendering numbers and say nothing about a phone's GPU; no effect has been measured on a phone.** 60 fps on a Snapdragon 8 Elite-class phone is the design target the governor holds to by lowering effects, not a measured fact. The settings, presets, steps, governor and pacing arithmetic are unit-tested (`unit-tests/.../graphics/`); the effects were rendered on and off in the harness with no GL error and looked at; the Options page, the pacer and the overlay are compiled by CI only.

The people's eye highlights, thin-skin tint and sheen were rendered on and off in the harness (`gfx eyes=1`, `gfx thinskin=1`, `sheen NAME VALUE`; a human woman's face close up in the Lion's Pride, a night elf's ears in profile and her fingers, and the night elf backlit by the evening sun outside Goldshire), and the light shafts in Goldshire at 8 in the morning with the sun behind the inn's roof and the trees (`sun` prints where the sun is): all with no GL error, looked at (skin tones kept, no noise). Their cost in the scene pass was within the harness's run-to-run noise (software GL). With every effect off the frame is pixel for pixel the previous build's (the face, the bedroom and Goldshire checked), and the bedroom with skin and hair shading alone too. The sheen's maths and its easing, the settings, presets and steps are unit-tested; the sheen's path from the scene to the renderer (`SceneSessionScreen`, `SceneView.glSheen`) is compiled by CI only, and none of it has run on a phone.

## Smoothness

Menus, windows and buttons share one motion kit (`ui/MenuMotion.kt`, `ui/MenuMotionUi.kt`): windows fade and grow in over 160 ms and out over 120 ms, dropdowns take 90 ms, a press shrinks to 0.96 over 100 ms, and all of it is off when the phone's "remove animations" setting is on. Every tap target ignores a second tap within 300 ms. Controls take their size from the layout, never from a picture or font that has not arrived yet. Download progress reaches the screen at most four times a second and only to the parts that show it. Pictures decode on two background threads into a size-limited cache, newest request first, and a request is cancelled when its cell leaves the screen. The live scene runs on a fixed 1/120 s step (same speed at 60 and 120 Hz, no jump after a pause), draws people interpolated between steps, and writes the camera and positions straight to the renderer; the menus see a snapshot at most ten times a second. The camera uses critically damped springs with inertia and soft limits, eases between the room and NPC cameras over 350 ms, and an 8 dp touch slop separates a tap from a drag. A loading cover hides the scene until its first frames are drawn. Skins are composed at most one per frame. Desktop measurements only (Mesa llvmpipe): the first frame after dressing six NPCs went from one 427 ms frame to a worst frame of 94 ms; steady walking runs at p95 20.6 ms. Not run on a phone.
