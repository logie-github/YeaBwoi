# Wifecraft

An Android scene simulation. Choose a location, pick or create NPCs, and select the objects available in the room.

1. Open **Choose Scene** and pick the Stormwind Embassy.
2. Select up to six NPCs. **Create NPC** saves a character to your roster. NPCs from older projects also appear in the roster.
3. Enable interactions for objects already in the scene. Stormwind Embassy uses its existing study table and books.
4. Begin the scene. NPCs go about their day (Life simulation, below; with it off they wander) until you give them an order (with **Partner** on, two may also start a partner animation together: Sims 4 animations, below). The scene fills the screen with its HUD over it, in the menus' look: a top bar (the place, the clock, and the gear for the scene menu: Resume, Options where offered, **End scene**), the cast as party frames (down the left on a wide screen, a strip under the top bar on the cover screen standing; the NPC you control lit gold, their partner in rose), a line of help, and an action bar. Tap an NPC (in the room or their frame) to control them; tap another NPC to make them the partner (the **Partner: name ×** chip clears it); tap the frame again, **Release** or empty floor to let go. The action bar shows six orders at a time (two rows of three on the cover screen standing), with a page button at its end for the rest: **Walk** (the camera follows the NPC; tap the floor to send them there, drag to orbit, pinch or use the zoom buttons), **Play animation**, **Partner animation** (with a partner), **Say…**, **Chat with…**, **Stop** while an animation plays, **Stop repeat**, **Skip** and **Clear queue** while there is one, **Release**, then the scene's objects (**Sit in chair**, **Read a book**...) and **Free will**. Tapping an object in the room opens its **Play animation** window with only the animations that fit on it (Furniture, below); when none does, it uses the object as before. With an NPC controlled, the **Move** chip puts them on a tapped item and holds them there to be nudged and reported (Move mode and item reports, below).

The Stormwind Embassy uses the existing wago.tools location pack. The title and scene setup use the original Wifecraft menu backdrop. The title emblem and first-launch download art also come from the earlier Wifecraft builds. Warcraft controls and selector icons load from wago.tools. The character, furniture, and book models load through the app's existing wago.tools pipeline.

## Places: the Embassy and the bedrooms of Azeroth

**Choose Scene** lists eleven places. Each is one of the game's own buildings, loaded from its files as the game stands it in the world: the building, the props it is furnished with, the map's props and ground around it, and its music and ambience. None is made up or modelled for the app. Each is fetched when first chosen (10 to 30 MB a place), and the preview shows it once fetched.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stormwind Embassy | human | `7hu_garrison_barracks_v2_embassy.wmo`, Stormwind | real |
| Lion's Pride Inn, Goldshire | human | `goldshireinn.wmo`, the canopy bed upstairs, the writing desk and its chair | real |
| Thunderbrew Distillery, Kharanos | dwarf | `snow_inn.wmo`, the guest beds in the cellar | real |
| Dolanaar Inn, Teldrassil | night elf | `nightelfinn.wmo`, the loft's beds | real |
| Razor Hill Inn, Durotar | orc | `orcinn.wmo`, its beds among the sleeping mats | real |
| Bloodhoof Village Inn, Mulgore | tauren | `taurenlonghouse.wmo` furnished as the inn, the loft's beds | real |
| Fairbreeze Village Inn, Eversong | blood elf | `bloodelf_twostory01.wmo`, the upstairs bed and a chair | real |
| A House in Brill, Tirisfal | Forsaken | `nd_forsaken_house.wmo`, a bed among the coffins | real |

Two are composed, where no troll or draenei building the game stands has a bed: the Sen'jin hut stands where the game stands it but is furnished with the same building's other furnishing set from the game (its bunk-hammocks and sleeping mats) instead of the one Sen'jin uses; the Telaar hut is the game's own, as it stands and is furnished in Telaar, with one bed added: a draenei bed of Draenor (`6dr_draenei_bed01`, the hut's own ancient draenei style) on its rug. Nothing else in either was added or moved.

Each bedroom's **Rest on bed** lies on its bed (on the mattress, along its length), walked to from the floor beside it; the Lion's Pride also has **Sit in chair** and **Read a book** at its desk, the Fairbreeze inn a chair (each chair's sitter gets up onto the floor beside it, not into the seat). In a room whose view stands far from its people (the Embassy's, across the hall from the doorway), the room camera moves in along its own line of sight until the cast fits the picture, no nearer than 3.2 yards and no farther than the floor is clear, and glides after them as they move about (`CastFraming`). Only these are interactive: the rest of each room is scenery. The places are worked out by `tools/locations.py` (the building's placement on the map, its furniture by index, the camera and spots measured against its floors and walls) into `assets/locations.json`: file ids, positions and sizes only, never the game's files. Every place was drawn on the desktop (`engine-test`, inside and outside views) and its camera picked by looking; nothing has been seen on a phone, and the lying pose on each bed (which end is the head) is worked out from the bed's model, not watched with an NPC on it.

## The character creator (Create a Character)

**Create NPC** opens the creator as a Create-a-Sim style screen in the Warcraft look (slate panels with a gold trim, the game's own creator pictures cut on the phone, Friz Quadrata). The tabs run down the left side (across under her on a tall screen): **Race & Sex**, **Body**, **Face**, **Hair**, **Makeup & Details**, **Outfit**, **Accessories** and **Personality**. She stands in the middle of the space the panels leave; a drag turns her, a pinch brings her nearer, and the camera buttons go to **Full** body, **Upper** body, **Face** or **Legs**, with **Turn** (a slow turntable) and **Light** (a warm evening light). **Tap a part of her** to open its tab: the engine finds the triangle under your finger on her body as posed (`engine/bodypick.h`) and the bones it moves with (head, hair, neck, chest, waist, hips, legs, arms, hands, feet), the camera goes to that part and a soft gold ring shows where you touched; the hips, chest, waist or legs open the Body tab's sculpt sliders with that part's sliders first and lit. The top bar has **Back**, her **Name** (with a dice for a random one), **Undo** and **Redo** (every change, the look, the sculpt and the personality, back and forth), **Presets**, **Randomize** and **Done**. **Randomize** gives every unlocked tab new choices from the race's real option lists, kept right with each other as the old dice did (a face for the skin drawn), the body options and a moderate sculpt on Body, three traits that go together on Personality, a wardrobe outfit on Outfit; **long-press a tab or tap its small padlock** to lock it, and Randomize leaves it as it is.

Every option Shadowlands' creator has for the race and sex is still there, on the tab its name belongs to: Face (face, eyes, eyebrows, features, markings), Hair (style, colour, facial hair), Makeup & Details (skin colour, makeup, overlays, scars, paint, tattoos, the Undead switch), Accessories (ears, horns, jewellery, earrings), Body (the skin colour again, Upright, and a woman's Shape, Style and Density). Each is a line with the creator's own arrows and box; a tap on the box opens all the choices under it (colours as swatches, the others as numbered tiles), and a switch is a switch. **Outfit** has the **Gear** switch and the **Class** buttons (see Gear on and off below). The old two-step Wrath++ layout is kept whole in the code (`ClassicCreatorScreen`).

**Body: Sculpt.** A full-body editor like Create-a-Sim's, on both bodies (see "Bodies"): the game's own default body of every race, women and men, and the Liard HD figures. Thirty-six sliders from -100 to +100, grouped by part (the part strip: Overall, Neck, Shoulders, Chest, Arms, Waist, Hips, Legs): the eleven it always had, Height, Shoulders, Arms, Bust Size, Bust Lift, Waist, Belly, Hips, Butt, Thighs and Leg Length (same names, same ranges, same places in a save), and eleven more, **Neck** (thickness), **Chest** (the pectorals forward, a man's or a woman's), **Bust Width**, **Bust Spacing**, **Shoulder Slope**, **Upper Arms**, **Forearms**, **Calves**, **Butt Lift**, **Body Fat** (overall softness: shares of the waist, belly, hips, butt, thighs, arms, calves, bust and neck) and **Muscle** (shares of the shoulders, arms, chest, thighs, calves and neck, the waist and belly a little less); and the Bust & Butt designer's fourteen shapes (below), saved under their own keys after those (`bustProjection`, `bustDroop`, `bustUpper`, `bustRound`, `bustSide`, `underbust`, `buttWidth`, `buttProjection`, `buttRound`, `cheekGap`, `hipDip`, `underbutt`, `pecs`, `abs`: a look saved before reads them as 0 and is otherwise as it was). Every slider is 0 by default and 0 is the body exactly as made; a long press on a name puts it back to 0, **Reset** *part* puts that part's back and **Reset all** the lot. Height is her size (the actor's scale, 6% either way). **Leg Length** edits the actor's own skeleton (`engine/bodyrig.h`), each thigh and shin stretched along its bone from 0.85 times as long at -100 to 1.2 times at +100 (girths, feet and everything above the hips as they were), the whole body raised or lowered every frame so the feet meet the ground where the animation puts them on the model's own skeleton (standing, and the planted foot while walking), so her hips stand higher or lower; everything skinned on her follows with no refit (the body, a tabard's cloth, what she holds), and the flesh measure the pair poses read is taken on the edited skeleton. A walk's stride grows and shrinks with the legs while the walking speed doesn't, so the feet slide a little at the ends of the range. **Drag handles:** on the Body tab, hold a finger still on her for about 0.4 s (a tick) and the part under it is picked (the tap-to-edit's pick, `engine/bodypick.h`): the drag that follows sculpts it on her body, up for bigger and down for smaller, left and right for its width (the bust's size and width, a man's chest and shoulders, the belly and waist, the butt and hips, the thighs and calves, the upper arms and forearms, the neck and shoulders), and with **Lift** on (or a second finger put down while dragging) up and down lifts it and left and right spaces it (the bust's lift and spacing, the butt's lift, the legs' length, the shoulders' slope). Half the stage's height sweeps a slider end to end; the slider on the panel moves with it, the name and value show by the finger ("Bust Size +35"), the phone ticks as a value crosses 0 or reaches an end, and each drag is one step of Undo. A hold on the head, hair, hands or feet turns her as ever. **Symmetry** (on by default): off, a drag changes only the side of her body you hold (her left or right: the pick tells which), and the panel shows each side's value with **Even** to make them alike again (saved as `"sides"` in the sculpt; Height, Leg Length, Neck, Belly, Bust Spacing, Body Fat and Muscle are always both sides). **How it is drawn** (`engine/sculpt.h`, `engine/scene.cpp` refreshBody): the model's vertices are moved in its bind pose in smooth regions made from their own bone weights (each bone's region by its WoW-side name: breasts where the model has breast bones, every playable woman measured here has them, else the chest's front by place and weight; on the game's own women the bust is a soft mound by place round each breast bone, on the chest's front, as far as the skin there is the chest's, because their breast bones' weights are light and patchy: most of a breast's skin is weighted under a tenth to them, which made Bust Size move 1.5 cm at +100 where it now moves about 5), scaled about the limbs' axes, across the body for the hips and waist, pushed forward or back for the belly, chest and butt, up for the lifts, and then skinned as before, so every animation bends the sculpted body exactly as it bent the old one. The spine is followed along its own curve (an orc's or a Forsaken's hunched chest), the limbs' moves fade out before the wrists, ankles and knees, the head, hands and feet never move, copies of a vertex at one place (two geosets meeting) move as one so no seam opens, a vertex at the body's middle takes both sides' mean so the skin stays mirror-symmetric, any triangle that would turn over or shrink below a tenth is eased back, and no vertex moves more than 12% of the body's own hip height (each race's caps scale with its size). The normals of what moved are made again (shared by the copies of a place that faced alike before, so an overlapping hidden geoset's never darkens the skin). The texture coordinates are not changed: the skin and underwear stretch with the surface, by as much as it grows (at the ends: the bust about 40%, the limbs about 30%, the waist and hips about 20%, within the 12% cap), which reads as the same skin on a larger shape. **Live while you drag** (`SculptMorph` in `engine/sculpt.h`, `engine/bodyrig.cpp` sculptSync, the vertex shader's `morphed` in `engine/scene.cpp`): every move above is a slider times a fixed field (its masks come from the body before the sculpt, not from the values), so the first time a slider moves on a body each move slider's field for a value of 1 is made once (45 to 62 ms on this machine) and uploaded as two float textures (per vertex its first entry, how many and its side; per entry the slider and the move; 2048 texels wide, 7.6 to 9.4 MB per body on the five measured, the CPU copy then dropped); from then on a change sets two uniform arrays (the change since the body was last sculpted, both sides and the balance) and the vertex shader adds the fields in the bind pose before the skinning: no CPU sculpt and no upload while you drag, so it follows the finger every frame (the creator sends every 16 ms). The CPU sculpt (each time from the body as it was before the sculpt, not the shape again) is made once the sliders have rested 150 ms, or after a second of moving: it puts back what the fields leave out (the 12% cap, the triangles it eases, Body Fat's and Muscle's 1.5 clamps, the normals), and the body is then exactly the CPU's again; the result is kept until the look changes; the flesh profile the pair poses read, the soft body, the tabard's cloth and the tap-to-edit pick all use the sculpted body. **Gear:** the clothing geosets the body model carries itself (robes, skirts, trousers, sleeves, boots, cloaks, tabards: they are in its own vertex list) take the same moves and follow the body (a robe's vertices near the skin moved within about 15% of the skin under them on the five bodies measured); armour textures are painted on the skin and follow with it. What is a separate model does not follow: shoulder pads, helmets, belt buckles and held things are rigid on their bones, and a piece drawn as its own skinned model (some newer chest pieces and capes) keeps its shape, so at the ends (bust, chest, shoulders or hips past about +60) the body can show through or poke out of those. **Measured on the desktop** (`engine-test/sculpt_test FILES_DIR 1000764 1011653 949470 921844 959310`: the human woman and man, the orc woman, the night elf woman, the Forsaken man; their default models have 226,000 to 266,000 vertices with every geoset variant and 48,000 to 57,000 triangles): every slider at both ends bounded, no triangle turned over, the skin mirror-symmetric, the head, hands and feet unmoved, no seam opening between geosets drawn together, the same input giving the same body, and a sculpt costing 20 to 29 ms each on this machine (a phone will be slower); the live sculpt's fields against the CPU sculpt (`morphCheck`): a drag's 0.2 step from a mixed sculpt within 0.001 cm on four bodies and 0.07 cm on the Forsaken man, a mixed sculpt from the body as made within 0.001 cm (0.11 on the Forsaken man), one slider at ±50 from the body as made within 0.001 cm on the night elf and up to 0.6 to 2.4 cm on the others at a few hundred vertices (0.002% of all vertex results: the shoulders' and the orc's small bust where the CPU sculpt eases a triangle that would collapse; the CPU sculpt 150 ms after the drag puts them back), the fields the same twice and none on the head, hands, feet or ankle rings, their weights 0.2 µs to work out; drawn by the harness (`sculptlive on`), every bust and butt slider at ±100 on the human woman by the fields alone matches the CPU's silhouettes exactly, only the light differs (the normals are the old ones until the CPU sculpt lands); a frame drawn while a slider moves cost 4.5 ms (4.0 standing still) against 27 ms when the CPU sculpted each change (`sculpttime`); and in desktop renders of those five, every slider group at -100 and +100 from the front, three-quarters and side, and with a robe and a plate set at bust and hips +100. Not run on a phone: its frame rate while dragging and the fields' memory (7.6 to 9.4 MB of float textures per body sculpted, the Liard figure none: it is fitted again instead) are measured on the desktop only; the drag handles, the haptics and the panels are compiled by CI only. The Liard HD figure takes every slider too (sculpted after it is fitted, about its own joints), but Neck: its neck ring meets the Warcraft head. The Body tab's **Skin & Shape** part says which body she has (**Body: HD (Liard)** or **Body: Game model**) with, where a Liard set exists for her race and sex, the **Use game model instead** switch; for a Liard figure it holds the set's own skin presets and body marks (see "Bodies" and "HD Models"). The sculpt is saved with the NPC (`"sculpt":{...}` in its look, left out at all zeros); the sculpt follows her into every scene, her Height shows in the creator only so far.

**Bust & Butt** (a man's **Chest & Glutes**): the Body tab's third part, a designer for the bust and the butt rather than a list. Its groups, **Bust** (Size, Width, Spacing, Projection, Lift, Droop, Upper Fullness, Roundness, Side Fullness, Underbust) and **Butt** (Butt, Width, Lift, Projection, Roundness, Cheek Gap, Hip Dip, Underbutt, with Hips, Waist and Thighs as they are), on a man **Chest** (Chest, Pec Definition, Abs, Shoulders) and **Glutes** (his Butt, its width, lift, projection and roundness, Hips, Waist, Thighs). Opening it turns the camera to the group's view (the bust from the side, the butt from behind) and **Front**, **3/4**, **Side** and **Back** turn and frame her there. Big sliders with their value, drawn live as they move (above). The drag on her body: hold the bust or the butt, then drag, in the mode chosen, **Size** (up and down its size, left and right its width), **Lift** (its lift and spacing; a second finger down while dragging does the same) or **Projection** (the bust's projection and roundness, a man's pectorals; the butt's projection and roundness). **Mirror** (on by default; the same switch as Sculpt's Symmetry): off, each slider of the group shows its sides' balance (her left above the value, her right below, L and R shown) and a drag changes the side you hold; turned back on, the group's sides are made alike. **Shapes**: Slim, Natural, Athletic, Curvy and Full set the group's sliders (the rest of the group to 0) and the sliders glide there over a third of a second, drawn live; **Randomize** a plausible shape for the group (its size leading, the others leaning its way, within ±70), **Reset** the group to 0, and **Undo** and **Redo** (each slider drag, body drag, shape or Randomize one step, as the top bar's). On the game's women the designer's shapes are drawn about the bust's mound (Projection forward from the chest, the tip leading; Droop turning each breast down about its root, the underside rolled out over the fold; Upper Fullness the slope above; Roundness fuller round the tip; Side Fullness the outer side toward the arm; Underbust the underside fuller) and about each cheek's centre under the hip joints (Width the back half wider, Projection each cheek's crown back, Roundness fuller and mostly down, Cheek Gap the cleft deeper, Hip Dip the side under the hip bone drawn in by how far round to the side of the spine it lies, Underbutt the fold where the cheek meets the thigh fuller); a Liard figure takes them as its own sculpt (its fit, on the CPU). Checked on the desktop in renders of the human, orc and night elf women (every bust and butt slider at -100, 0 and +100 from the front, three-quarters and side, the butt from behind, three-quarters behind and side) and the human and Forsaken men (Chest, Pec Definition, Abs and the glutes): bounded, none turned over, symmetric. Limits: the texture is stretched with the surface (a large bust or butt shows the skin's detail larger); armour drawn as its own model doesn't follow (as above); at the ends Droop folds the underside against the chest in a crease, Lift at -100 likewise, and Cheek Gap and Underbutt are small changes on these models' meshes.

**Personality.** Choose up to three of the twelve life traits (opposites, such as Social and Loner, don't go together; none chosen: she gets three of her own as before; **Choose for me** picks three from her look), her **Preferences** (when someone else starts something: Yes, Ask me or No; Pace: Slow, Either or Intense; which stages she allows: Kiss, Embrace, Cuddle, Dance, Play, Intimate), a short **About her** and a favourite colour. The life simulation reads them (`simulation/LifePreferences`): her chosen traits replace the assigned ones; NPCs never start a stage with her that she doesn't allow or when she said No, ask her (she says yes less often) when she said Ask me, and the most two NPCs do together by themselves is the least of what their standing and both of their boundaries allow; a slow pace makes her want kisses and cuddles less, an intense one more. What you order yourself is yours to choose. Saved with the NPC as `"personality":{...}`.

**Presets** keeps whole looks under a name (race, every choice, body options, sculpt, personality, outfit) in `files/cas-presets/`: **Save look**, **Load**, **Delete**, **Export** (a `.json` file you can share) and **Import a file**. The named characters' looks are built in (Chromie's gnome look with her own golden hair).

Looks saved before read back exactly as they were. The tabs' sorting, the history, Randomize with locks, the sculpt and personality models, the presets' files and the old saves are unit-tested (`CasTest`, `SculptModelTest`, `BustButtDesignerTest`: the designer's groups, views, shapes and their glide, Randomize, Reset, Mirror, the new keys in saves and the Projection drag; `LifePreferencesTest`, `CasLayoutTest`); the sculpt's maths and the body pick on a made-up body (`engine-test/sculpt_test`), and the sliders were drawn on the desktop on the human woman's (Eve) body before it was scrapped; the tap pick was checked on her there too (head, hair, chest, waist, arms, hands, legs, feet). The screen itself is Compose, compiled by CI only: it has not been run, on a desktop or a phone.

## Gear on and off

There is no wardrobe: every NPC has one switch, **Gear**. **Gear on** wears the NPC's class's default clothing: the game's own starting outfit for that race, sex and class (the worn pieces of `CharStartOutfit`: shirt, chest or robe, belt, legs, feet, wrists, hands, and for a death knight the whole set from helm to cloak). **Gear off** is nude: every armour slot empty and the skin colour's underwear left off too (the named characters' bare look; the game's and an imported set's underwear alike), while the face, hair, makeup, markings, horns and the rest stay as they are; a Liard figure is bare, a man with an imported body-parts set wears his parts, as with any unclothed look.

- **Where the switch is:** the creator's **Outfit** tab (the **Gear** switch, and the **Class** buttons: Civilian and the classes the race may be, a class tap also turning Gear on); the classic two-step creator has a **Class** button at its foot beside the Gear row of every category; every NPC's menu in the NPC picker (the round button on its card) has a **Gear** row; in a live scene the bar over the actions has **Gear on / Gear off** for the selected NPC and **All gear on / All gear off** for everyone in the scene (they are dressed again at once; the switches are kept for the NPCs when the scene ends). Randomize keeps the switch and the class while Outfit is locked.
- **Classes:** warrior, paladin, hunter, rogue, priest, death knight, shaman, mage, warlock, monk and druid, as each race may be them (`CharBaseInfo`); **Civilian** (no class, the default for every NPC made before) wears the recruit's shirt, pants and boots, the clothing new NPCs always started in. Demon hunters and evokers are not offered: the game has no starting outfit for them in any client that still has the table.
- **The outfits** (`assets/class_outfits.json`, ids only, made by `tools/class_outfits.py`): `CharStartOutfit` from Mists of Pandaria Classic (5.5.4: the newest client with the table, retail dropped it; races 1 to 11, the worgen 22 and the pandaren 24), each item turned into its look at the app's build (12.1.7: `ItemModifiedAppearance`, `ItemAppearance`, `Item`'s slot). Where the table has no row: an allied or newer race takes the race it was made from (a void elf a blood elf's, a Kul Tiran a human's, a Mag'har an orc's...), a pair the table never had (an orc priest, a dwarf druid...) a human's or another race's of that class and sex, and anything else the civilian clothes. The pieces' models and textures are fetched from wago.tools as an NPC first wears them (no download pack lists them).
- **The named characters:** Gear on is her signature outfit (her first outfit, or another outfit of hers if one is chosen), Gear off her nude look (her **bare** look; else her outfit's nude one; else her underwear one); her **Look** picker stays as it was, and choosing a look there sets the switch with it (an outfit, tabard or partial look is on; underwear, bare and nude are off). Chromie has her outfit only, so her switch stays on.
- **Saves:** the appearance keeps `"gear"` and, for a class, `"class"`; nothing of the clothing itself is saved. NPCs, scenes and presets saved with the Transmog window's outfits load as Gear on (any piece worn, or the recruit clothes) or Gear off (`"gear":false`, or every slot cleared), their pieces not kept; a field the app doesn't know is ignored. The switch set on the menu or in a scene is kept in `files/npc-looks.json` (`"gear"`, over the NPC's own; a named character's as her chosen look).
- **What was removed:** the Transmogrify window (slot buttons, item and set lists, Custom Sets, search, filter, the cards and their pictures in `files/thumbs/transmog/`, which are no longer made), its art (`assets/transmog_art.json`), the starter-clothing catalog's tables loaded at the creator, and the **Transmog items (large)** download pack. The named characters' underwear, nude, tabard-only and partial looks never depended on it and are all kept.

- **Bare toes in boots:** a death knight's Acherus Knight's Greaves show the toes, as in the game: the boots' foot texture is see-through over the toes and the item's display (140716) asks for the toes' geoset (`GeosetGroup_1` 1: geoset 2001; 0, the shod foot, is 2002). The classes with no boots at all in their starting outfit (the monks, the pandaren, the tauren and draenei of several classes, the robed orc, troll and night elf casters) go barefoot, as they start in the game.

Tested on the JVM (`GearTest`: the classes per race, each class's outfit per race and sex and the fallbacks, Gear on and off, the Liard figure bare and in its outfit and the men's parts, older saves, the creator's class and switch, npc-looks.json, everyone's switch, the named characters; `ImportedLooksTest`: Gear off draws no underwear; `GearRealTest` on the game's tables: every outfit with boots, every race, sex and class, sets the boots' and the feet's geosets as its item says and places the boots' lower leg and foot layers). Desktop renders of a human woman and man in four classes, on and off, were drawn by engine-test; the switch's screens are compiled by CI only and have not run on a phone.

**Download everything** (Options → Downloads) fetches every file the app can otherwise fetch from wago.tools as it is first wanted, so the game can be played offline afterwards. Its packs, in order, each with its files, size and state (Not downloaded, Partial 62%, Complete, Needs update) and its own Download button: **Game basics** (the first launch's gathering: the interface, the tables, Dalaran, every character creator option for every race and sex, about 6.1 GB), **Places** (the eleven locations and every race's backdrop, 0.21 GB), **Menu and creator art** (the creator's and the menus' atlas sheets, kept in their own caches), **Music, ambience and voices** (sounds.json, the time of day's flavour and bells, fire, rain and cloth, every emote's voice for every race and sex: 4,364 files, about 0.15 GB), **Character animations** (every animation file of every character model, about 1.6 GB), **Named characters** (every display they are drawn as or cut from, about 0.26 GB), and three big packs ticked by default that can be left out: **Library creatures (large)** (the placement library's creature models and the game's named creatures: 16,063 displays, about 7.6 GB), **Library props (very large)** (51,111 props, about 3.8 GB) and **Library buildings (huge)** (12,584 buildings with their rooms, about 22 GB): about 42 GB in all. The sizes are estimates (`assets/files.json`'s `estimates`, made by `tools/pack_sizes.py` from the game's tables, the asset lists and the averages of files measured while making the app: wago.tools gives no sizes ahead) until a pack has run, then its real count. Before it starts the free space is checked (1 GB always kept free): with too little room it says how much is needed and offers **Download what fits** (the required packs and the optional ones that fit, smallest first). It runs as a foreground service (`data/DownloadService.kt`, type dataSync) with a notification (the pack, the progress, Pause and Cancel), so it goes on with the app in the background or the screen off, the CPU kept awake; the notification says how it ended. **Wi-Fi only** waits for Wi-Fi; **Also on mobile data** lets this download go on without it. Pause, Resume and Cancel keep every file already here. A file that fails is tried again by itself after 30 s, 1 and 2 minutes (each file also has the limiter's five tries with 429/5xx backoff, the download speed's 24 to 96 at once); after that, or when wago.tools has no such file, it is listed under **Failed files** for **Retry failed**. The state is kept (`files/cache/download-state.txt`): a download the app's closing or Android stopped carries on when the app is opened again (Android 15 lets a background download run six hours a day; at that limit it pauses until the app is opened). At the end **Verify** checks every file of every pack (each pack's list in `files/cache/packs/<pack>.ids`: there, and the length it came with or the length the asset lists give; the lists carry no checksums) and fetches the damaged ones again: "Everything is here (N files, X GB)". **Pre-build caches** (off by default) also prepares the scene sounds' index after it. Still fetched as wanted: what an imported or user-made look needs that no pack lists, the game's sounds for every creature and object placed in a scene (the whole game's sound library is too large to fetch blindly), and the community animation packs (installed from the Animation library, not here). The packs' logic is unit-tested (`DownloadPlanTest`: the packs, sizes, free-space refusal, states, failed queue, kept state, verify's check); the service, the notification and the page are compiled by CI only and have not run on a phone, and how Android 14 and 15 treat the service has not been seen on a device. Sims 4 rigs and clips are imported from your own files.

## HD Models

(Which race and sex has one, and the rule that a Liard figure is used wherever there is one and the game's model everywhere else, is in "Bodies".) Your own high-detail characters can **replace the game's models**: for a race and sex with an imported HD set, the HD figure is the character everywhere (the creator, the scene, partner animations, cinematic mode, portraits and pictures, Gear on and off): its own body, head, face, ears, eyes, brows, lashes, hair, jewellery and, where the set has them, its own outfits. The game's model of that race and sex is no longer drawn. The sets are made from your own downloaded model files (FBX zips such as Liard's Art's edited World of Warcraft models, or a Blender `.blend`); none of them is part of the app.

**Importing.** Options → Mods → **HD Models**: **Choose files** (the converted zip, `Wifecraft-HD-*.zip`, or its `.whd` files); each set lands in `files/bodyparts/hd/<race>_<sex>.whd`, one per race and sex (a new one replaces that race's alone). Each set's row says what it has (hair styles, outfits, eyes, marks, skin tints, jewellery, triangles, texture memory) and who made it, with its own **Remove**; **Remove all**. The files are copied and checked (the header, its index, every part inside the file) on a thread of their own (`bodyparts/HdModels.kt` HdImportRunner), and the folder is replaced only when every picked set was good: a damaged file keeps the old sets and says why. **Use HD Models** (on once sets are imported; off draws everyone on the game's models again, the sets kept) applies to looks dressed after it changes.

**Converting** (on a computer, `tools/hd/whd_convert.py`, inside Blender's Python): `python -I tools/hd/whd_convert.py RECIPE.json MODEL.fbx TEXTURE_DIR OUT_DIR --zip Wifecraft-HD-Name.zip`. A **recipe** (`tools/hd/recipes/*.json`, data, not code: the next races are recipes) names the model's body, its always-drawn pieces (lashes, brows), its hair styles, jewellery and outfits (each outfit with the classes it is for), each material's image (an FBX names none), the skin and hair base textures and the presets that replace them (Liard's Art's other skin tones and hair colours), the eye textures, the face and body marks, and its rig: `weights` (each rig bone to the game bone it does the work of; `{S}` for each side) and `joints` (each game bone's joint on the HD figure). A recipe may `extends` another (`liard_rig.json`: the joints every Liard's Art model shares). The converter imports the FBX (or opens the `.blend`), evaluates every named object at the rig's rest pose with its shape keys (armature, subdivision and mask modifiers off), decimates a piece over its budget, writes Warcraft axes in metres, four normalised weights per vertex, the textures as PNG (at most 1024 px, the body's skin 2048), each outfit's covered body triangles (the skin under it isn't drawn, so it can't poke through) and the presets. Recipes now: Liard's Art's **Human Female 4.0** (`liard_human_female.json`: 32 hair styles, 12 jewellery pieces, 2 skin tones and 2 tinted, 13 hair colours, 23 eye colours; she is the human woman's HD body, with human ears), **Nelf Female 4.0** (`liard_nightelf_female.json`: 18 hair styles, 15 pieces of jewellery and horns, 4 skin tints, the hair colours, 10 eyes), **Human Male 3.0** (`liard_human_male.json`: his hair and beard painted on his skin, 4 skin tints), and a night elf woman from a `.blend` (`nightelf_female.json`, with the Tyrande, Warden, druid, ranger and swimsuit outfits mapped to classes, and its **human variant**: the ears made human (the blend's EarsSmall key, then each ear vertex's distance along the ear folded to 3.4 cm, its reach cut by a third, its top rounded), the brows shortened, the skin turned toward a human tone in Lab).

**The set** (`.whd`, version 1): `WHD1`, u32 version, u32 JSON length, u32 blob count, the JSON (padded to 16), a table of (u64 offset, u64 size) per blob, the blobs at 16-byte boundaries. The JSON: race and sex (ChrRaces id; 0 male, 1 female), label, credit, the bone names the weights use, the joints, the textures (PNG blobs), the meshes (vertex and index blobs, parts by texture, opaque or alpha-tested), the always-drawn meshes, eye textures, hair, outfits (classes, covered body triangles), jewellery, overlays, skin and hair presets (a colour gain and/or a texture of their own), the base skin and hair textures, the texture memory and triangle counts. A vertex is 40 bytes: position f32 x3 (metres, x forward, y left, z up, feet at 0), normal f32 x3, UV f32 x2 (top-left origin, repeating), four bone indices and four u8 weights summing to 255; triangles u32.

**On the game's skeleton** (`engine/hdbody.cpp` fitHd, `hdscene.cpp`). The actor keeps its game model underneath (skeleton, animations, imported Sims clips, attachments, speech anchors, scale), only it isn't drawn. The HD figure is scaled so its neck stands as high as the model's, its pelvis over the model's; its arms, hands, fingers, legs and feet are turned to point as the model's rest pose does (skinned by its own weights about its own joints); every bone's pivot is put at the HD figure's joint (`bodyrig.h` bodyEditFrom), so the game's animations bend it at its own joints. Its weights are the model's own authored ones mapped onto the game's bones by name: a nearest-vertex transfer from the game's body was measured against them and stretches the body by 6 to 60 cm in the game's poses where the authored ones stretch it 0.4 to 5 cm, about as the game's own body does; the transfer remains for a bone the model lacks. The soft body is found on the HD body (its breasts measured on its own vertices), the flesh measure (partner alignment) and the creator's sculpt sliders work on it (the sculpt about its own joints, outfits and hair following); Leg Length too. Fitting takes 1 to 5 ms; each texture is decoded once and shared by everyone drawn with it.

**Clothing.** Gear on wears the set's outfit for her class (the Class picker; Civilian the civilian outfit), or the first other outfit when the set has none for it (the Outfit tab says so); Gear off is the bare HD body. A set without outfits (the three Liard's Art models: their FBX carry no clothing) is drawn bare with Gear on too, and the creator says so. Each NPC can also pick an outfit of the set or bare whatever Gear says.

**In the creator.** For an HD race the game's own Face, Hair, Makeup and Accessories rows are replaced by the set's: eyes and face marks, hair style and colour, jewellery; the Body tab's Skin & Shape has the skin presets and body marks (there is no other body than the Liard figure and the game's model: see "Bodies"); the sculpt sliders and Bust & Butt shape the HD figure. Saved in the look as `"hd":{...}`.

**Memory.** Liard's Art's woman: 66,000 triangles for the body and up to 3,000 for her hair (the game's body has 3,000 to 4,500), about 54 MB of textures when every preset is decoded (about 25 MB as usually drawn: a 2048 px skin, a hair and an eye texture, with mipmaps). Drawn on the GPU like the game's body.

Tested on the desktop: `HdModelsTest` (the header's checks, imports from zips and loose sets, one per race and sex, a failed import keeping the old sets, Remove per race and all, the options' save, the decision table: off, no set, Gear off bare, Gear on by class, Civilian, a class without an outfit, an NPC's own outfit, a set without clothing, the pictures' mark), the whole JVM suite; `engine-test/hdbody_test SET.whd FILES_DIR MODEL` with the three Liard's Art sets on the game's human woman, night elf woman and human man (the file written back and read again unchanged, a cut file refused, every weight summing to 255 on bones the model has, the limbs within 0.03 degrees of the model's rest directions, the neck's height and the pelvis within 2 cm, the edge stretch and collapsed triangles in standing, walking, a chair, kneeling, asleep, cheering and bowing against the model's own body); the harness (`hdset`, `hd`, `hdstatus`) drew them side by side with the game's models in those poses and twenty of the game's emotes, the outfits standing, walking and from behind, and the faces close up. **Storage.** The library keeps each animation as a small clip file (`files/anims/clips/<id>.wclip`, `anim/ClipStore`: rotations as smallest-three 15-bit numbers, positions as 16- or 24-bit steps of each track's own range, key times as whole ticks, deflated, with a version byte and checksums), with keys dropped where the keys either side reproduce them (within 0.1 degree on the body's chain from the pelvis to the wrists and ankles, 0.25 degree on hands, face and toes, 0.5 mm on positions; the first and last key always kept, so poses hold and loops close). Renaming, moving or deleting touches only the index. The review can keep **Only two-person animations** or **The first N**, and says how much the kept ones take. ## Pack import

**One zip for your own animation, toy, furniture and layer packages.** Options → Mods → **Import pack** takes a single `pack.zip` (made on a computer by `tools/build_pack.py` from your own Sims 4 `.package` files, a `.zip`, `.rar` or `.7z` of them, or a folder: `python3 -I tools/build_pack.py --out pack.zip PATH...`) and puts everything in the right place in one go. The packages are yours; none is in the app or in this repository, and the zip is only ever copied to your phone.

**What is in the pack.** `pack/manifest.json` (format `wifecraft-pack`, schema 1) and the files under `pack/animations/`, `pack/bodylayers/`, `pack/props/`, `pack/furniture/`, `pack/info/` and `pack/other/`. For each file the manifest has an id, its type, its path, the name it came from, its sha-256 and size, and a summary (the library category, clip, stage and object counts, the resource types). The builder tells each package by what it holds, never by its file name: **animations** (clips with a WickedWhims tuning that names actor clips), **props** (object models, such as toys, dolls and handcuffs, that the package's own stages name), **body layers** (object models only another package's stages name: the cum mesh), **furniture** (object models no stage names: a bed), **script notes** (a text note of what a `.ts4script` mentions: the script itself is never in the pack and never run) and **unused** (any other package, such as a purchase menu's tuning: kept and listed, never imported). What the packs of this release hold is in `docs/pack-inventory.md` (every package, its resource types, how an animation finds its props, every stage as the app reads it).

**How it imports.** Pick the zip. It is unpacked into `files/pack/staging` by `pack/SafeZip`: any entry with `..`, an absolute path, a drive letter, a backslash or a null in its name, a duplicate name, too many files, one file over 256 MB, a total over 1 GB or a file that swells over 400 times its packed size refuses the whole pack before anything is used; directories, anything outside `pack/`, and every `.ts4script`, `.py` and `.pyc` are left alone (the importer never runs or reads a script). The manifest is read and checked (`pack/PackManifest`: the format, the schema, kinds, paths of the form `pack/<kind's folder>/<file>`, digests, sizes, unique ids and paths); every file's size and sha-256 must match it (a file that does not fails alone). Each package is read (`pack/PackClassifier`) and **routed by its content**, not by what the manifest says (a note says when they differ), to its importer: animations to the animation library (below), toys, dolls, handcuffs, the cum-mesh layer and the bed to the prop store (`files/pack/props`: `props/PropStore`), notes shown as they are. The screen shows the step and file as it goes, then one line per file: **Imported** (what: "76 clips and 34 partner animations added to LPGS", "9 props loaded, 1 prop animations, 1 stage links"), **Already imported**, **Skipped** (why) or **Failed** (why). It is resumable: what is finished is kept in `files/pack/state.json` after each file (its id and sha-256), so a stopped import (the app closed, the battery out) goes on from the next file when the same zip is picked again and a finished file is not done twice; **Import again** does everything again. The staging folder is emptied at the start and the end. A bad file fails alone; the rest carries on.

**Animations (the library).** Each animation package goes through the same importer as Import rig and clips (`anim/ImportPipeline`: the retarget onto the Warcraft skeleton, partner pairing from the stage tuning, the checks) and is saved under the pack's category (`LPGS`, `CherryPie`, `FEMDOMANIA · Foot Fetish`...) without a review step. **They need the Sims 4 skeleton**: import WickedWhims first (Animation library → Import WickedWhims keeps its skeleton) or load one under Import rig and clips; without it the animation entries say so (Skipped) and the props are still loaded; import the pack again afterwards. The stage tuning is read in full (`sims/StageTuning`): both generations of the field names (`animation_clip_name` / `animation_name`, `<L><U>` / `<T><T>`), the author, stage names and links, the category, the furniture, the actors with their preferred sex, the props and the events. How the new packs' names become stage kinds, roles and positions (`anim/StageNaming`): LPGS and WickedWhims write display names in words with bracketed tags (`Pinned Down Missionary 3 [Dialogue+HandCuffed]`: the position, the variant 3, the tags dialogue and handcuffed); CherryPie's clips are `CherryPie:PosePack_..._set_1` with stage names `blowjob1`, `blowjobS`; FEMDOMANIA's names are upper case with a people count (`FOOT TABLE (3P)`, `WORSHIP YOUR QUEEN(8P)`); 0nizu's toys write `Toy | Position` (`Dildo 01 | Cowgirl`: the toy before the bar). The roles are A, B, C by actor id. The kind is the pack's `animation_category` (TEASING, HANDJOB, FOOTJOB, ORALJOB, VAGINAL, ANAL, CLIMAX and the typos packs have) and drives, as before, the arousal map (`assets/arousal_map.json`: the stage class and the entry; the authors' names LPGS, CherryPie, FEMDOMANIA and 0nizu now count as sexual hints for a stage with no category), the JOI handjob classifier and the dialogue layer: a stage whose category is a hand, foot, oral, vaginal, anal or climax one is **intimate** whatever its name says (`PairKinds.of(name, category)`: "FEET LOVE" holds the word love, "Stand and carry" the word carry). The furniture the packs name (`DOUBLE_BED`, `CHAIR_LIVING`, `CHAIR_DINING`, `CHAIR_LOUNGE`, `CHAIR_LOUNGE_SOFA`, `TABLE_DINING_LONG`, `DESK`, `OTTOMAN`, `LOVESEAT`, `SOFA`, `MIRROR`, `FLOOR`, `NONE`, in lists or one text with commas) becomes the app's classes (`anim/StageLocations`: bed, couch, chair, table; the floor, none and a mirror: anywhere) in the three places that read them (the pair's locations, the furniture kinds, the partner places); the "marked surface is a preference, never a gate" rule is unchanged: every stage plays anywhere.

**Stages for three or more people are not played.** A scene plays one or two people and the simulation's partner animations have two roles; supporting more would need a group of NPCs walking to places round one origin, which is not a small change. Of the 130 stages in these packs 74 are for two, 23 for one (played as single clips) and **33 for three to eight are not played** (Foot Fetish's 3P and 4P ones, Cuckold's, New Cleopatra's `WORSHIP YOUR QUEEN(3P)` to `(8P)`, LPGS's `Threesome double penetration`...; each is listed in `docs/pack-inventory.md`). The importer leaves out the clips only those stages use, and the pack's result line says how many there were. Many of them have a two-person version in the same pack (`(2P)`), which plays.

**Props: toys, dolls, handcuffs (`props/`).** A stage's props are objects: the stage names an object id (`prop_guids`, an OBJD's instance) and a clip that moves the object's own rig (`prop_animation_clip_name`). The loader (`props/SimsObject`, `props/Rcol`; `tools/sims_object.py` is the reference reader, `tools/prop_preview.py` draws it) reads an object package's OBJD (its model, rig and material variant), MODL (its LODs), MLOD (the meshes: vertex format, vertex and index buffers, the indices stored as differences, the material set and the variant it names) and the DDS/DST textures (`bodyparts/SimsTexture`, downscaled to 512), and writes a `.wcprop` file per object (`props/PropFile`: Warcraft axes, a flat colour or a texture a mesh, the object's bone names, a checksum). The prop's own clip is sampled at 30 Hz at its root bone into a `.wcpa` (`props/PropClip`). The links (which stage uses which props: by the stage's actor clip names) are in `links.json`. The engine reads the file (`engine/propmesh.h`: parse, upload on first draw, a small lit program: a hemisphere light and a sun, two-sided) and draws each frame's copies (`Scene::setProps`); in a scene the stage that plays is found from what each person plays, the first actor's place is the animation's origin and the prop stands there moved by its clip (`props/PropStageDriver`; `SceneView.setProps`; wired in `SceneSessionScreen`'s sink). **Rigid:** a prop is drawn at its root bone's place; its other bones (a doll's limbs, the cum mesh's strands, the cuffs' chain) are not skinned, so a prop whose bind pose is not its resting shape looks wrong (the thick doll v2's is folded). Model units per Sims metre is the adult skeleton's 1.4233 (`PropStageDriver.DEFAULT_SCALE`). Props the game itself provides (a plate, a cup: small object ids) have no model here: their stage plays without them.

**The cum mesh (body layer).** The `WW_CherryPie_Animation_CumMesh` package is one stage, `CumMeshTestV2` (a one-person stage on a double bed), whose prop is the object in `CherryPie_CumMesh` and whose clip moves it: it is a prop that follows a stage, not a skin layer, so it is drawn like a prop, by the stage that names it and when that stage plays (the mechanism is the WickedWhims prop list; the mesh is a 4065-vertex droplet strand over 16 bones, drawn rigid, so it shows as a small white blob at its resting place). Options → Mods → Import pack → **Draw the body layer** switches it (on by default; the others: **Draw the pack's props**, **Pack furniture in Build mode**). The tunings' `CUM` and `EFFECT` events (particles at a joint) and WickedWhims' own cum texture layers (`receiving_actor_cum_layers`) are read but not drawn: the pack has no texture layers.

**Furniture: the bed.** `CherryPie_BondageBed` loads as ten colour variants of one model (a wooden bed, 1.35 × 0.99 m, 0.6 m high, 23,828 triangles; the loaded ones are in the store as `furniture` models). `props/PropFootprint` finds what Build mode's New tab needs to make it a Bed spot: the footprint's half sizes and the surface height (the highest wide upward-facing layer: 0.96 × 0.70 yd and 0.43 yd for this bed). **The New tab does not offer it yet** and the spot does not draw the model: that needs a model reference on the user spot, its build-file schema and the draw in the build view, not done (the model is drawn by the engine and `PropStageDriver` can place it).

**Sounds.** LPGS and the electric masturbator carry SNR audio (117 and 8 files, with 111 and 2 sound configurations) that the stages' sound events name. No decoder is built (sound is deferred): they are counted in the manifest and the inventory and not played.

**Not done, and not tested.** Not run on a phone at all. The importer's logic, the zip safety, the manifest, the routing, the object and prop readers, the prop files and the prop driver have JVM tests (`unit-tests/.../pack/`, with synthetic packages built in the tests); the same readers were run on the real packages on the desktop (`PackRealFilesTest`, `PackStageAuditTest`, run only with `WIFECRAFT_PACK_FILES`, `WIFECRAFT_PACK_ZIP` set); the real pack imported through the importer on the desktop with the prop handlers (the animation handler needs the Sims skeleton, which was not available, so animations were routed and counted, not converted). ## Build mode

**Where to find it (every scene, two ways).** (1) The **Build** button sits in the top right, directly under the **Cinematic** button (the same pill, the same size; it reads **Building** and is lit gold while Build mode is on). It is there while the HUD is, and it goes with the rest of the interface when cinematic mode's idle hide takes the HUD (never while Build mode is on). (2) The scene menu (the gear in the top bar, the same menu as Options) always has a **Build mode** button, which becomes **Leave Build mode** while it is on. Every scene opens in this one live screen (there is no separate preset-scene screen), so both are there from the first moment the scene is shown. A one-line hint ("New: Build mode...", with **Got it**) shows under the cast until it is dismissed or build mode has been entered once (remembered on the phone). While it is on, the Build bar replaces the action bar at the bottom: that is the sign it is on. Pure logic: `BuildEntry`, tested in `BuildEntryTest`; the layout is compiled by CI only.

The **Build** button (it replaces the old Move chip, and Move mode is part of it) turns on build mode, in any scene. The scene never stops for it: the NPCs go on with their day and their animations while you work. **No animation ends while it is on.** Whatever plays keeps looping until you leave Build mode: a pair's or a solo play (its natural end, the End part and the return to standing are held; a staged play loops the part it is on, on the same clock, so it never pops), a queued order, an NPC's own next pick (walks, chats, sitting down, partner animations, the life simulation's choices and invitations) and the cinematic auto-play (below) all wait. One flag does it, `holdAnimations`, fed by Build mode being on (`SceneSimulation.holdAnimations`, `CinematicAutoplay.hold`, `BuildEntry.holdAnimations`); a walk stands still, a conversation stops where it is. Your own orders (Stop, Play, Switch positions) still act. Leaving Build mode lets everything go on where it was.

**Build mode works in cinematic mode.** They are no longer exclusive: pressing Build keeps cinematic mode (the 4:3 film frame, the light, the grade), turning cinematic on keeps Build mode. While both are on the director's camera steps aside for Build's orbit round the selection, auto-play stands on the current shot (nothing is changed, Next scene does nothing, the dwell does not run), and the HUD and the Build bar stay up (the interface never hides while Build mode is on). Leaving Build mode returns to normal cinematic mode: the director's camera comes back and auto-play goes on with the rest of the dwell. The Back button ends cinematic mode only when Build mode is off. In immersive mode the HUD stays up while it is on.

**The camera orbits round what you selected.** One finger drags the orbit round the pivot, two fingers pan it along the view and pinch to zoom; there is no free flight. A tap on anything (a prop of the place, even one that was never interactive, a piece of furniture, an NPC, a marker, the floor) selects it and makes it the pivot: the camera glides to centre it. The selected item gets a blue-white box round it (the placing ghost's colour).

**The axis gizmo (no free move).** What can be moved shows three arrows (**X** red: its front, **Y** green: its left, **Z** blue: up) and a gold ring. Drag an arrow to move it along that axis only, drag the ring to turn it; a drag that starts on a handle (within 24 dp) moves the thing, any other drag orbits. The steps: **Snap** cycles **1 cm**, **5 cm**, **10 cm** and **Fine** (no snap, a quarter of the finger's move, 1° turns); the ring turns in 15° steps. The panel shows x, y, z (yards, the place's frame) and the facing, with fields for exact values and **Set**. Every gesture is one step of **Undo** and **Redo** on the bar.

**The Build bar** (at the bottom, in the action bar's place): **Select**, **Marker**, **Move**, **Interact**, **Send**, Undo, Redo and **Done**. The panel at the side (under the camera's buttons, never over the middle where the gizmo is) shows what is selected and what the tool does with it.

- **Markers.** With **Marker**, pick a type (**Stand**, **Sit**, **Lie**, **Kneel**, **Pair origin**: a pair's shared origin, **Camera spot**) and tap the floor, or **Add marker** at the selection (an item's anchor, a pair's origin, a marker) or at the middle of the view. A marker is a small pin with a ring on the floor and an arrow for its facing, coloured by type; it is named ("Sit 2"), can be renamed, retyped, deleted and moved with the gizmo, and belongs to the item that was selected when it was made. The list shows yours and the shipped ones (**Go** selects one). **Show markers** shows them small outside build mode too.
- **Interact (the classifying).** Select any item and choose its class (**Bed/couch**, **Chair/seat**, **Table top**, **Floor/rug**, **Wall/stand**, **Other**), tick the animation classes it takes (**Lying**, **Sitting**, **Kneeling**, **Standing**, **Leaning**) and set its surface's height (from its box; − and + by 2 cm, **Box** for its top), then **Make interactive**. It takes effect at once in this scene: the item's anchor (where a body goes; move it with the gizmo, the Z arrow sets the surface) becomes its place and its **Play animation** window offers what its ticks allow. A prop that was not one of the scene's objects takes the place of the scene's bed (lying ticked or Bed/couch), chair (sitting or Chair/seat) or table (leaning, Table top or Wall/stand), so any prop can be made a bed and the "no bed found" cases go. Your items are kept in `files/build/items.json` (every place's) and win over the shipped file (`assets/item_overrides.json`), which wins over the furniture's own measure. **Hold NPC here (item report)** on one of the scene's objects is Move mode (below).
- **Moving a playing animation live.** Select an NPC who is playing an animation: the gizmo stands on its pair's shared origin, and moving or turning it moves both of them while they keep playing on the same clock (their contact and lifts are the clip's, kept). **Re-place** puts the pair back on its computed place.
- **Remember for races** (a race's own placement, not a separate animation). Under **Remember**, choose the scope (**Only this race pair**, **This race in this role (any partner)**, **This size class**: gnome-size, dwarf-size, human-size or tall) and the roles (A, B), then **Remember for gnome female** (or whichever): the correction is kept as a delta from the computed placement in the pair's own frame (x its front, y its left, z up, the turn), for this animation, the role, the race and sex (and the other's race for a pair), the furniture class it was on (bed, couch, chair, table, floor) and, for one NPC on an item, the item. The engine draws them (`engine/sparkle.cpp`: no texture, procedural point sprites, additive, no state: the motes are made from the clock, 4 to 14 a spot); the Graphics governor lowers their number (60%, 35%, then none as it steps effects down). **Show sparkles** is a switch on Options' Graphics page and in the New tab (default on); each user spot also has its own **Sparkles on this spot** switch. `engine-test/sparkle_test` checks the motes' maths (`engine-test/build.sh`); the harness's `sparkles DENSITY X Y Z HALFX HALFY YAW` command draws them for a picture.

### Move mode and item reports (part of Build mode)

With an NPC controlled, in build mode select one of the scene's objects and press **Hold NPC here (item report)**: that is Move mode, the item mapper (its panel's **Done** lets go and the build panel comes back). Tap a piece of furniture in the room and a small window asks **What is this?**: **Chair/seat**, **Table top**, **Floor** or **Bed/couch** (the one the furniture's own measure gives is lit). The NPC is put there at once and held: on a seat sitting on its seat, on a bed lying on its lying place, at a table standing in front of it, on the floor standing in front; a tap on open floor holds them on that spot (Floor, no question). They stay until you tap another item, press **Done** (or **Done moving**, or Back), or give another order. The panel at the side nudges them in the item's own frame (up is the item's front): the arrows (held, they repeat), the drag pad in the middle, ⟲ ⟳ to turn, **Up** and **Down**, **Fine** (2 cm, 5°) or **Coarse** (10 cm, 15°), and **Pose** where the class has more than one (a seat: Sit or Stand; a top or the floor: Stand or Sit; a bed: Lie or Sit). The held place is shown under it (x, y, z yards from the item, the facing in degrees from its front).

**Report…** takes a screenshot of the scene (without the HUD), asks once whether reports may be sent (*This sends item data to the developer's repository*), then opens the report: an optional note and the checkboxes **Fits two people**, **Too small**, **Wrong facing**, **Floating**, **Clips**, **Other**, **Share screenshot** and **Send report**. The report becomes a GitHub issue on this repository, titled `Item report: <class> <item> <model> @ <place>` and labelled `item-report`; the panel then says **Report sent: #123**. Without a connection it waits in `files/reports/queue/` and goes by itself when the phone is online (while a scene is up), the next time Move mode opens, or with **Send waiting reports** in Options; a refusal says why, with **Retry**.

**What is sent:** the place (its id and name), the item (its object and model file id, its position and facing in the place's frame, its box in yards, its surfaces and their heights, the furniture fit's own measurements: its kind, surface and places, and the class that measure gives, to compare), the class you chose, the NPC's race, sex and size (and a name only for the game's named characters, never one of your own NPCs'), the held place in the item's frame (x, y, z yards, facing degrees), the pose and its game animation, your note and checkboxes, and the app's version and build number. Nothing else: no account, device, file path or name of yours. The issue's body carries a summary and the same as a fenced JSON block (schema 1). **The screenshot is never sent** (GitHub's API takes no images in an issue): it stays in `files/reports/shots/` (640 pixels wide, at most 200 KB), and **Share screenshot** hands it to Android's share sheet if you want to pass it on yourself.

**Your mappings take effect at once.** Each report is also kept on the phone (`files/reports/mapped.json`): the item is taken as the class you gave it (a table called a seat is a seat), your held place becomes its first place for that class at its height (the surface a sit or a lie rests on is moved to that height), and its **Play animation** window offers only what belongs on that class (a seat the seated ones, a top those at a table or counter, the floor the floor's, a bed those on a bed or couch and the lying ones). **Options → Mods → Mapped items** lists what you classified (the item, the place, the class and pose, Reported: #n, Waiting to send or Not sent), each with **Delete** (it stops changing the item on this phone; a sent issue stays). Items nobody mapped behave as before. Precedence: your own mapping, then the shipped file, then the furniture's own measure.

**Setting it up (the repository's owner):** the app opens issues with a token built into the release by CI. Add a repository secret named **`WIFECRAFT_REPORT_TOKEN`**: a fine-grained personal access token limited to this repository with **Issues: Read and write** (nothing else; the label is made on the first report). `.github/workflows/build-wifecraft.yml` passes it to Gradle (`-PwcReportToken`), which puts it in `BuildConfig.WC_REPORT_TOKEN`; the issues go to `WC_UPDATE_REPO` (this repository). Without the secret the build sends nothing: reports are kept on the phone (the panel says so) and go once a build with the token is installed.

**Reading the reports (the developer):** `python3 tools/item_reports.py` (standard library only; the token from `$GITHUB_TOKEN`, `$GH_TOKEN` or `gh auth token`; `--repo OWNER/NAME`, `--dry-run`, `--from-json FILE` for a saved list of issues, `--state all`) lists the open `item-report` issues, checks each JSON block (bad ones are named and skipped), and writes `android/app/src/main/assets/item_overrides.json`, keyed by `place/object/model`: the class most reports gave (a tie: the newest), and for each class reported the median held place and facing, the commonest pose, the surface's height, the notes and the issue numbers; with three reports or more of a class, those over 0.35 yards or 45° from the median are left out and listed as outliers (the same rules as the app's `ItemOverrides.merge`). It prints a summary (each item, where the measure differed, the outliers, the skipped issues). It never closes or edits an issue: close them once their item is merged, commit the file, and the next release ships it.

Pure and unit-tested on the desktop (`ItemMapperTest`: the report's JSON and its round trip through the issue body, the frame maths for turned and scaled items, the state machine from tap to reported or queued, the queue and its retry, the request with a fake HTTP layer, the answers' classification, the merge's medians and outliers, the lookup's precedence, the overrides' effect on the furniture, the picker's filter per class, the hold in the scene); the tool was run on a hand-made list of issues. The GitHub call itself can't be tried without the token, and the panel, the windows, the screenshot, the share sheet and the hold's drawing are compiled by CI only: nothing of it has run on a phone.

## Time of day

A live scene has a world clock (`simulation/WorldClock.kt`): a real second is a game minute at x1, so a day takes 24 real minutes (8 at x3, 4 at x6; Paused stands still), counted in days from Day 1, its hours named Midnight, Night, Dawn, Morning, Noon, Afternoon, Dusk and Evening. A scene starts in the morning (8:00) unless another start hour is chosen; the clock is saved and put back as JSON, and the life simulation follows it (`LifeTime`). Its settings (speed, start hour, and **Day/night cycle**: off keeps the start hour's light and sound while the clock still runs) are `ClockSettings`, on Options' **Time** page (`Settings.clock`): the speed there is where each scene starts, the start hour is where a new cast's first day begins (a cast played before goes on from its own saved time), and the cycle applies at once. The live screen drives the clock every frame on the GL thread, hands the hour to the light (`LightFeed`, `SceneView.setTimeOfDay`) and, four times a second, to the sound (`Audio.timeOfDay`); the life simulation follows it (`LifeControl.setTimeSource`), and its speed buttons go through life to it (`LifeSim.onSpeed`: Pause, x1, x3, x6 are `LifeSpeed` PAUSE, NORMAL, FAST, FASTEST, the same multipliers: `LifeHud.lifeSpeed`/`clockSpeed`).

The light follows the hour (`SceneView.setTimeOfDay`, cpp `engine/daylight.cpp`) with the game's own day for each place: its map light's LightData keyframes (`dayCycle` in `assets/locations.json`, from `tools/locations.py CACHE --day-cycle`), sampled between keyframes and round midnight with no jump: an orange dawn at 6, the day's light, a golden and then violet dusk from 17, a blue moonlit night from 20:30. The sun crosses from the east at 6 through the south at noon to the west at 18; by night the light comes from the moon in the north. At noon the colours are those the scene had before (only the sun's direction is the hour's). Inside, a room keeps its own baked light (the game bakes lamps and windows into its vertex colours): by night it is scaled to about two thirds and warmed, which reads as lamplight but can't light a lamp or darken a window by itself; what is seen through its doors and windows (the outside's light, sky and fog) changes with the hour. Lanterns' and windows' glowing textures stay lit at night. Checked in desktop renders of the Embassy outside and inside and the Lion's Pride bedroom at 6, 9, 12, 17, 19, 22 and 2 o'clock (`engine-test`: `time HOURS`; `build/daylight_test` checks the cycles); not seen on a phone.

**Cinematic light.** Cinematic mode's look is the place's own light made low-key, not one warm look laid over everything (cpp `engine/daylight.h`: `cineProfile`). Each frame it is worked out from the place's light at the hour (the map light's ambient, sun or moon, sky and fog; inside, the room's own ambient and baked light, with a little of the day through its windows) and from the practical lights standing near (`cinematic.practicals` in `assets/locations.json`: candles, torches, braziers, lanterns and lamps, which flicker, and crystals and arcane lamps, which don't; found by their model's file name by `tools/locations.py --cinematic=LISTFILE`). The profile gives the people a key from a low side angle in the place's colour (pulled toward the sun by day, the moon by night, or toward the practicals inside) and a rim from behind, deepens the shadows, and grades the picture toward the place's hue (lift, gamma, gain, a gentle S-curve, saturation, vignette). So a cold room stays cold: the Brill house is a moonlit blue-violet with its candles showing only as a warm rim and a flicker; the Lion's Pride stays warm, its key the colour of its flames; a daylit exterior gets a sun key and a soft grade, a night one a moon key and a deeper grade; a place without a flame (Bloodhoof's longhouse, Dolanaar) doesn't flicker. Limits keep it in hand: no colour cast past a chroma limit, black never washed out and the shadows never crushed, highlights never blown, a person's light kept between a floor and a ceiling, and a place that is already dark isn't made darker. The profile eases over about a second when the camera passes from a room to the outside or the hour moves on, and changes at once when the clock jumps. Options' Cinematic **Lighting** (0 to 100 %) scales every difference from neutral (0: the light as it is) and fades in and out with the mode. A place can set any part of it by hand (`cinematic.override.inside`/`outside` in its entry: `key`, `keyGain`, `rim`, `rimGain`, `lift`, `gamma`, `gain`, `contrast`, `saturation`, `vignette`, `flicker`, `hue` and the rest; kept when the tool runs again). Unit-tested on the desktop (`engine-test/daylight_test`: identity, limits, continuity over the day, the blend, the key's direction, the JSON, every place at every half hour; `mood_test`), and seen in desktop renders of all eleven places with two clothed people, off, the former warm look and the new one, inside at 8 and 22 and outside at 8, 18 and 22. Not seen on a phone; a room's baked light can still only be dimmed and tinted as a whole.

**Zone mood.** On top of that, cinematic mode takes the colour of the zone itself: the light the game draws at the place (`tools/zone_light.py CACHE_DIR` reads the game's `ZoneLight`, `ZoneLightPoint`, `Light`, `LightParams` and `LightData` tables from wago.tools at the app's build, picks each place's light as the game does: the zone light polygon it stands in, else a point light whose falloff reaches it, else the map's default, and writes every hour's ambient, sun, sky top and middle, fog and end-fog colours and fog distance, and the sun's glow, the clouds', the two sky bands' and the horizon and ground ambient colours, to `assets/zone_light.json` and to each entry's `cinematic.zone`). Brill takes Tirisfal's own zone light (a mauve ambient, a dim blue sun, a dark teal sky and a deep purple fog, the same all day); Goldshire and the Embassy Elwynn's and Stormwind's, Dolanaar Teldrassil's violet, Razor Hill and Sen'jin Durotar's dusty reds, Bloodhoof Mulgore's, Thunderbrew Dun Morogh's, Gadgetzan Tanaris', Telaar Nagrand's; Fairbreeze stands in no Eversong zone light at this build, so it takes the Outland map's default (a warm gold). The location's ambient, sun, sky and fog are pulled toward the zone's (at a brightness between the two, so night stays night), the fog comes nearer where the zone's is thick (Tirisfal's to about 50 yards; never past the area's edge), the grade splits the picture by brightness: a haze of the fog's colour in the blacks, the sky's middle and its clouds' colour in the shadows, the ambient in the mid-tones and the sun's glow in the highlights (Tirisfal: purple blacks, green-teal shadows, mauve mid-tones, pale green highlights; warm hues such as skin and candle-lit wood take little of the shadows' colour), and a room's own light is white-balanced part of the way toward the zone (its candles and hearths stay the warm part). People keep their skin: the light on them keeps its brightness with its colour held within a limit. Rooms stay moody but readable: a dark room's ambient is raised to a floor in its own colour (the zone's under a zone mood, by day a little of the sky's through its windows) and the frame's exposure goes up with a soft shoulder (black and white stay put), so faces and furniture read while the candles and hearths stay the warm part; a place's `cinematic.exposure` (a number, or `{"inside": n, "outside": n}`, 0.5 to 2) tweaks it by hand (Razor Hill's room 0.9, Brill's yard 1.15). Options' Cinematic **Zone mood** (0 to 100 %, default 70 %; 0 changes nothing) sets how far, and **Mood boost** exaggerates the zone's colours (more saturation and contrast) for a stylised look. Tested on the desktop (`engine-test/zonelight_test`: every place's zone read and the same as `zone_light.json`, continuous over the day, zone mood 0 exactly the look without it, the tone limits for every place, hour, side, strength and boost, skin hue and brightness against the look without it, Tirisfal's mauve, teal and purple, its green shadows and highlights; `daylight_test`: every room at every half hour, with and without the zone mood, a mid surface graded at least 0.12 and at most 0.50 and never under 0.6 of the look without it) and in desktop renders of Brill (house and outside), Goldshire, Dolanaar, Razor Hill, Telaar and Gadgetzan at 8, 19 and 22, the former look against the new one and the boost. Not seen on a phone.

The sound follows it too (`audio/TimeOfDaySound.kt`, played by `Audio.timeOfDay`): from 20:00 to 5:30 the Embassy's ambience crosses over eight seconds to its night loop (SoundAmbience 569's night kit; its day kit by day) while its music plays on, a little quieter by night (70 %, eased over dusk and dawn); now and then (every 25 to 70 seconds) one of the place's ambience flavour sounds plays (SoundAmbienceFlavor: for the Embassy the high winds of kit 130139), and Stormwind's bell (kit 6594, belltollalliance.ogg) tolls the hour at 6, 9, 12, 15, 18 and 21 (as many strokes as fit before the next). The bedrooms have no sound entry yet, so no day and night sound either; nothing is made up where the tables have none (there are no rooster or cricket kits for these places). Not heard on a phone.

## Immersive mode

A live scene opens immersive (Options > **Immersion**; `simulation/Immersion.kt`, `ImmersiveSettings` kept as `Settings.immersive`): no HUD at all until you tap the view. A tap brings back a quiet one for a few seconds (**Controls stay**, 2 to 10 s, 4 by default; held while a window or menu is open; every tap keeps it a little longer; the first tap only shows it, it picks nothing): the cast frames with their names only, the camera and Cinematic buttons, the action bar without Actions, and no counters or numbers anywhere (the "Stage 2 · Loop 3/5" line is gone; only what the scene answers is shown). The Sims layer (need bars and rings, mood gems, thought clouds, the clock, the feed, the radial and Actions menus) is behind one switch, **Show life HUD**, off by default; the life simulation keeps running underneath either way. Updating moves everyone to immersive mode once (the life HUD off); every other option is kept, and after that your own choice stays.

**A flow, not a menu.** With an NPC controlled and a partner chosen, the soft controls (a shallow arc of plain words at the right, quieter than the action bar) offer **Closer** and **Continue**. Continue starts the flow (`simulation/SceneFlow.kt`): the lead-in (they come together, face each other and share a slow kiss, the game's own emote), undressing (a piece at a time, about a second apart, alternating between them, in a natural order: cloak, helm, shoulders, gloves, bracers, boots, belt, legs, tabard, chest, shirt; each piece re-dresses the NPC through the same dresser without that slot and plays one of the game's cloth movement sounds; Options' **Keep on** chips say what stays on for everyone, `Settings.keepOn` holds a list per NPC; **Undress slowly** off skips it), the stages (the author's chain from its first stage when the library has one, queued as Queue stages does, else another two-person animation, else a partner animation; your repeat options apply), the afterglow (they stop, and with a bed in the room the lead lies down on it) and rest (the life simulation's Sleep, with it on). Each part moves on with **Continue**, or by itself with **Auto flow** (the lead-in 9 s, the stages when their queue has played, the afterglow 40 s). During the stages the controls are **Slower**, **Faster**, **Closer**, **Change position** (the queue's next stage, or the Play window with the partner animations first), **Continue** and **End** (which also dresses them again). Slower and Faster move the moment's intensity, not the clips' speed (the engine has no playback rate for imported clips yet). The long-press radial and Actions stay for the life menus, with Show life HUD on.

**Body language** (`cpp/engine/motionlayer.h`): every NPC in a live scene breathes, shifts their weight a little and tilts their head now and then, and looks at whoever they are with (their partner or the one they talk to; in the flow, each other): the neck and head turn within 60° either way, 20° up and 26° down, eased over about a third of a second, given up for someone behind them, and given up to the clip where a clip turns the head itself (its Head and Neck keys turning more than about 9° fade it out by 20°). It is laid over whatever pose the clip or emote made, as small turns of the posed bones about their own joints: nothing below the waist is turned, so the feet never move. The breathing's rate (12 to 30 a minute) and depth follow an intensity from 0 to 1 (`IntensityModel`): 0.2 standing about, 0.3 a solo animation, a two-person one from 0.4 at its first stage up 0.07 a stage to 0.85, a start a little under its loop, the loops climbing with each pass and with time, an end easing back; the lead-in 0.3, undressing 0.4, the afterglow 0.15; Slower and Faster move it 0.08 a step. It rises over about 2.5 s and settles over 5 s, never a jump; the idle motions quieten as it rises. Options: **Breathing** and **Small movements** (percent) and **Eye contact**. Measured on the desktop on the game's human woman and man standing, at intensity 1 with both at 100%: the most anything moves in 30 s is the head 1.1 to 1.2 cm, the neck about 1 cm, the shoulders 0.6 to 0.8 cm, a hand 1.9 cm, the feet 0 (for a 170 cm body); eye contact turned the head about 45 to 49° toward someone 45° aside and never past its limits for targets all round (`engine-test/motionlayer_test FILES_DIR`). On a rendered still the breathing is too small to see; the head turns are plain.

**Sound that carries the mood.** The scene's intensity (its most intense person's) sets the mix through the same mixer: the music swells from 55% to full as it rises, the room's ambience recedes to 70%, slowly (one music bed's levels, not stems or other tracks: the game has no quiet and full layers of these tracks); the partner sounds follow it by **Voice intensity** (0: always their own volume; at 100% a quiet moment plays them at 35%). **Atmosphere sounds** plays a small fire's crackle under the scene (the game's `sound/doodad/campfiresmallloop.ogg`, file 565519) and, with **Rain outside**, light rain (`rainlightloop.ogg`, 538983); the cloth sounds are `sound/foley/cloth/10.0_foley_cloth_movementlong_01..07`. These are files of the game by their ids, fetched like the rest; none was made up. The partner sounds' playback rate is not changed.

**Atmosphere.** **Glowing motes** (and, by default, always in cinematic mode): 48 embers and dust motes drifting slowly up through the light round what the camera looks at, fading in and out, soft and additive under the cinematic grade (`cpp/engine/particles.h`; seeded, so the same every time). **Camera sway**: in cinematic mode the camera drifts a little as if held (at most about 3.5 cm at the eye, half at the target, from slow sines; `HandheldSway`). The light's flicker is cinematic mode's own. Depth of field and the other picture effects are Options' Graphics page (Graphics and performance, below).

Tested on the desktop only: the settings and their migration, the intensity, its easing, the HUD's timing, the soft controls, the mix numbers, the sway (`unit-tests/.../simulation/ImmersionTest`), the flow (`SceneFlowTest`), the motion layer's maths and the motes' determinism and bounds (`engine-test/motionlayer_test`), and stills of two clothed NPCs in the Lion's Pride bedroom with the layer off and on (their heads turn to each other) and with the motes in cinematic light (they are small and few). The screens, the flow's orders, the re-dressing and the sound are compiled by CI only; none of it has run on a phone, and how it feels is yours to judge: every layer has its switch or slider.

## Cinematic mode: the camera

While an animation plays, cinematic mode's camera uses a shot library made from the usual camera conventions for each kind
of position. Its research and per-position table are in `docs/cinematic-shots.md`; its data is
`assets/shot_library.json`. Each animation is sorted into a class: kissing, partner-facing lying, from behind, on top,
seated, standing, side by side, oral, manual, two people otherwise, or one person. The class comes from the animation's
names, or from its bodies' postures when no name says it (`simulation/ShotLibrary.kt`). Each class has 3 to 6 candidate
shots with weights: an establishing wide, profile and three-quarter mediums, low and top-down angles, face close-ups and
reactions, over the shoulder, a high POV-like view, and hip and hand details.

The director picks again when the animation or its stage changes. In **Classic film** it follows a cadence: a wide shot,
then medium, close and detail shots. It never picks the same shot twice running. It keeps the camera on one side of the
pair for the whole scene (the 180-degree rule), preferring the side they face and the side with more room. Face shots are
taken from in front of the face.

Each shot drifts on a seeded random walk round, up and in, within its own range. Every eye passes the director's
clearance tests: walls, furniture boxes, the floor and ceiling, no one blocking a face, and no furniture between the camera
and the action. The furniture is taken at its real measured shape: a piece's base up to its surface, its solid parts (a
headboard, posts, a chair's back) and, on a canopy bed, the canopy with its valance, a little wider than the bed. No camera
sits under a canopy or inside a piece's box, no sight line passes through a canopy or a solid part, and a canopy may take
no more than a sliver of the picture. An over-the-shoulder shot sits back and to the side of the near person, and is turned
down when their body would cover more than a quarter of the picture. The low hip detail of a partner on top is set just
over the bed or couch the pair is on, and is never used on the floor. A wide shot is brought in to fit the room (its walls
and ceiling); where the room is too small that way, it moves to the corner with the most room, as high as the ceiling
allows. The people's hip and head points come from the playing pair clip's own skeleton as the animation reader measured
it (its pelvis, where its head is, its posture), placed where the pair is played. With nothing playing, the director uses its
own mix of shots, as before.

Options > Cinematic adds three settings under **Shots during animations**:

- **Shot style**: Classic film, Intimate, Close-ups, Wide or Random.
- **Randomness**: how loosely the cadence and weights are followed, and how far each shot drifts.
- **Camera cuts**: on allows cuts between shots; off allows smooth moves only.

The existing settings keep working. Slow orbit only still orbits, and Cuts still cuts everything.

Tested on the desktop only. JVM tests (`ShotLibraryTest`, `CinematicShotRulesTest`) cover the classification, each class's
shots, the style weights, no repeats, the side kept, clearance, the drift's bounds, determinism, the canopy bed, the shoulder's
share of the picture, the low hip detail's height, the wide shots in small rooms and the hip and head points. Preview stills
were rendered with the desktop engine in the Lion's Pride Inn room, using synthetic clothed poses on the floor and on its
canopy bed (`tools/ShotPreviewRecorder.kt`).

Not yet checked:

- No phone test.
- No real animation packs. The hip and head points come from the animation reader's summary of a pair clip (one pelvis
  and head position for the whole clip, not each frame), and the head's height is an estimate by posture. Single clips,
  emotes and pairs the reader could not measure keep the rough points by activity; a body resting on a bed is taken to lie
  toward its facing.
- The canopy's drapes are not measured on their own: the canopy is taken as a slab over the whole bed, about a third of its
  rise deep, and its long sides as open where the measure found no solid part. On the Lion's Pride's canopy bed this
  leaves only wide and medium shots from beside the bed: close-ups and the low hip detail would need a camera under the
  canopy.

### Cinematic mode: auto-play and the 4:3 frame

**Auto-play.** With Options > Cinematic **Auto-play scene** on (the default), starting cinematic mode starts a scene: the
controlled NPC and their partner (else the flow's pair, else the first other person in the cast) play the installed partner
animations one after another (`simulation/CinematicAutoplay.kt`). Each one is chosen with a place: the bed, the couch, a chair,
the table or the open floor, wherever every one of its two bodies lands on something that takes its posture at its height
(`FurnitureFit`, for these two bodies' size: a dwarf or a gnome only where the existing rules let them). It plays, looping, for
25 to 90 seconds at the middle **Pace** (1.5 times as long at the slowest, half at the quickest), then the next: the same place
about half the time while it has something fresh, else another place. The same animation never plays twice running while
another can, the last four wait their turn, and one in the same posture as the last is less likely. The open floor is where the
pair stood, or a spot clear of the furniture. With nothing installed (or nothing that fits a place here) it plays the models'
own partner emotes and its panel says so; with one person nobody plays.

**Every change happens during a cut.** The picture goes to black (**Cut style**: a quick fade of 0.12 s, or a hard cut), the next
animation starts with both already on their places (no walk, no settle: `SceneSimulation.autoplay`), the camera cuts to a new
shot of it (`CinematicDirector.cutNow`: another size than the last, or the same size from at least 30 degrees round; never by a
move), and only then the picture comes back. The black lasts about 0.4 s: the engine's own 0.25 s crossfade of the pose has to
pass in it, so the change of pose is never seen (a hard cut is black just as long, without the fades). Within a place the camera
keeps its side of the pair (the 180-degree rule); at a change of place the side is chosen again. With **Show movement** on, a
change of place is walked in view and the cut comes as the next animation starts.

**The interface hides itself.** In cinematic mode with Build mode off, a screen not touched for 5 seconds hides all of the interface (every chip, bar, panel, button, hint and the Cinematic button: only the 3D view and the film frame stay) until the next touch, which shows it again and restarts the 5 seconds; that touch only shows it and is not a tap in the scene. Any finger down or moving keeps it up; it never hides while Build mode is on or a window or menu is open over the scene. ## Life simulation

With **Life simulation** on (Options > Time; on by default: `Settings.lifeSimulation`; its HUD below shows only with Options > Immersion > **Show life HUD**; turned off in a running scene, needs stand still and NPCs choose as before), the live scene's NPCs live as in The Sims, in Warcraft's words (`simulation/LifeSim.kt` and its `Life*.kt` files; the screen's side is pinned in `simulation/LifeApi.kt`). It drives the same runtime as everything else (walking, sitting, resting on the bed, reading, talking, partner animations, emotes); it adds no second one.

- **Needs.** Seven bars from empty to full: Rested (energy), Well Fed (hunger), Refreshed (thirst), Company (social), Amused (fun), Clean (hygiene) and At Ease (comfort). They fall with game time (`LifeClock`, or the world clock when it drives it: a game minute a real second at x1, so a day is 24 real minutes; x3, x6 and Pause): about 19 waking hours to tire out, 12 to go hungry, 9 to thirst, so a day holds a night's sleep, about three meals and some company. What an NPC does fills them: sleeping in the bed (or a night on the couch when the bed is taken), napping on the couch, in a chair or, with nowhere to lie or sit, sat on the floor (the game's SitGround: the Embassy has no bed or seat), sitting, eating or drinking at the table (an inn meal: the game's Eat emote, bite after bite) or from the pack standing, reading at the study table or a bookshelf, dancing, freshening up, stretching, chatting. A conversation's lines are read in real time, so at x6 one can last hours of game time: it costs the needs at most 0.4 game hours of their fall however long it runs. A fireplace, a basin or a bookshelf is used where the room has the game's own model of one (locations.json "props", `simulation/LifeProps`): bookshelves at the Lion's Pride upstairs, the Brill house upstairs and the Telaar hut; no room has a fireplace or basin of the game's (no washing or warming up, only freshening up).
- **Traits.** Three each, the same every time for the same NPC (from their id, race and sex): Early Bird, Night Owl, Social, Loner, Hearty Appetite, Ascetic, Romantic, Playful, Bookish, Brave, Mischievous, Calm. Some lean lightly by race (dwarves love their food and ale, night elves keep the night, tauren and draenei are calm, gnomes read, goblins scheme, trolls play); sex never decides one. They change how fast needs fall, what an NPC likes doing and talking about, when they sleep (22 to 6; an early bird 21 to 5, a night owl 1 to 9) and who they get on with.
- **Moods and thoughts.** A mood from the needs and from what happened lately (Well Rested, Inspired by a Book, Shared a Laugh, Had an Argument...), a mood score, a thought bubble now and then ("I could use a drink.", "Is it nap time?", "Not a free bed in sight.") and a wish ("wants a hot meal", "wants a friend", "wants to hear a story").
- **Choosing.** When an NPC is free (not under your order), everything that can happen now is scored: how urgent the need is, what the thing gives, the traits, the distance, the hour, a little chance, and less of what they did lately; the best wins. A need under a fifth is seen to first (a quick meal or drink before a night's sleep) and company waits for it; nobody eats or drinks above two thirds full, mealtime or not. What it's for is cut short when the need is full or when another becomes urgent (under 0.08) and lower than the one being filled by 0.15, never in its first 4 real seconds (so two low needs never take turns cutting each other off), and one begun on a need under a fifth runs until that need is 70% full (never your orders). A night's sleep runs to the sleeper's morning (or full energy by day), broken only by hunger or thirst gone urgent after half a night; up rested, back to bed only under 55% energy for six hours. A pressing need (not company) makes an NPC leave a conversation of their own politely ("I should get something to eat."). What can't be had (a bed when it's taken) is wanted aloud in a thought, not faked. Getting up from furniture, an NPC steps off its floor spot onto free floor beside it, and nobody walks to where another stands to talk or wander (half a yard apart). The greetings follow the hour ("Morning, ...", "Evening, ...", "Still up?" at night).
- **Relationships.** Each pair has a friendship in the game's reputation standings (Hated, Hostile, Unfriendly, Neutral, Friendly, Honored, Revered, Exalted) and a romance (Spurned, Stranger, Acquaintance, Flirting, Sweethearts, Romantic, Soulbound). Chats (their topic suiting both or not, and both moods), jokes, compliments, flirting, gifts, hugs, dances and time sitting together raise them; arguments and refusals lower them; apart, they drift back slowly. By themselves NPCs hug and dance from Friendly, kiss from Sweethearts and play the library's other (intimate) pairs from Romantic; the one asked may refuse (a line in a bubble and a small cost). **Play animation** and **Send to partner** are never held back. The Chatter and Partner settings still apply: Off means NPCs don't start those by themselves.
- **The feed.** What happened ("Jaina went to bed.", "Thrall and Valeera became friends.") in a list of the last forty.
- **Your interactions.** From an NPC: Rest, Eat, Drink, Read, Dance, Wash, Wander; to another: Chat, Tell a joke, Compliment, Hug, Flirt, Kiss, Argue, Invite to sit, Dance together, Give a gift. They are your orders; the other accepts or refuses and the answer is shown. One asked while carrying out another of your orders is asked again as soon as they're free (for 20 seconds: "Elena is busy. Fizzwick will ask again in a moment."). A partner animation on furniture that can't start says why ("They can't reach the bed from where they stand."): its places beside the furniture are tried farther out, round its other sides and at its own floor spot first (the Lion's Pride's canopy bed is bigger than a usual one). Each NPC's needs can be frozen.
- **Saving.** The whole life (clock, needs, traits, moods, relationships, the feed) saves as JSON per cast (`files/life/` + `LifeSim.fileName`, the world clock beside it as `clock_<same>.json`, each written whole or not at all) every half minute and when the scene ends, and comes back with the scene.
- **On screen** (`ui/LifeHud.kt`, pure and unit-tested; `ui/LifeOverlay.kt`, `ui/LifePanels.kt`). Over each NPC's head a mood gem, WoW's quest-marker diamond in the mood's colour (green happy, yellow content, orange a need, red irritable, lonely or sad, blue bored or inspired), glowing and bobbing gently (still with the phone's animations off), hidden while they speak. Now and then a thought cloud (pale, with puffs trailing to the head: never mistaken for the dark speech bubbles) with the game's own icon for it (a mug, bread, sleep, a book, a drum, a valentine, the sun and moon, water...: `ui/LifeIcons.kt`, item and spell icons by their file ids from the listfile, a drawn glyph until they're fetched or if they can't be): at most three at once, one per NPC, each up a few seconds, a rest after it, never the same words twice running, the controlled NPC's first. Each party frame rings the NPC's plate with their seven needs (red to green) and carries their gem (dimmer while they're off screen) and, beside the controlled NPC, a heart (Flirting or more), linked rings (Friendly or more) or crossed blades (below Neutral); its line reads "Happy · Reading". A tap on the plate opens their **Life** window (Needs: mood, wish, what coloured it lately, seven reputation-style bars, a long press on one says what fills it, Freeze needs; Relationships: each friendship as the game's reputation bar in its standing's colour and the romance on a rose bar, closest first; Traits). **Hold** an NPC (or its frame) for the radial menu: your own Rest, Eat, Drink, Read, Dance, Wash, Wander, or, with someone controlled, Chat, Tell a joke, Compliment, Hug, Flirt, Kiss, Argue, Invite to sit, Dance together, Give a gift to the one held; hold a piece of furniture for what it gives (and its Play animation window). The action bar's **Actions** opens it too (with the partner when there is one). What can't be had is greyed and says why when tapped; a request that may well be refused is marked. The answer comes as a short toast (green tick or red cross). The top bar shows the world clock (sun or moon, the time, "Day 2 · Morning"); a tap gives Pause, x1, x3, x6 for this scene. The book beside the gear opens the last twenty events, a gold dot when something new happened. The scene menu has the controlled NPC's life and Freeze. Taps that controlled an NPC or chose the partner do exactly as before; the numbers refresh four times a second.

Tested on the desktop only (`unit-tests/.../simulation/LifeSimTest`): each need sends an NPC to what fills it, traits change the rates, friends are made by chatting and gate what pairs are picked, refusals cost, saving round-trips, the same seed lives the same life, and three game days of six NPCs in a room with one bed, one couch, a chair and a table (at x3) pass with nobody in one waking state over about 90 real seconds and everyone sleeping every night (in turns: some end the night tired), about 6 microseconds a simulation step for all six and at most one decision per NPC every 1.25 seconds. The screen's logic (gem colours, bar colours and words, reputation bars, the relation marks, the thought throttle, the radial menu's angles, spacing and hit test, the clock's face and speeds, every icon key having an icon) is unit-tested (`unit-tests/.../ui/LifeHudTest`) and the screens were type-checked against Compose Desktop; nothing of it has been compiled for Android here (CI builds it), the tuning has not been played by a person, and none of it has run on a phone.

### Conversation gestures and the pose check

NPCs talking to each other (small talk, the dialogue layer's chat, a free Say) played the game's emote animations as gestures: Laugh (70) and Exclamation (64) among them. Both bend the whole body (Laugh leans the spine 49 degrees and drops the head to half its height, Exclamation 33 degrees), so on a woman with shoulder armour the pauldron came up over her head, her arms flew out behind her and the other's head dropped sideways (the two phone screenshots of Tess Greymane and Sally Whitemane). The armour was never displaced: it rides the shoulder attachments and follows the bones in every clip, the body was posed that way. Now every gesture a conversation plays goes through a pose check (`simulation/PoseSanity.kt`): `PoseSanity.judge` rejects an animation whose worst pose leans the spine over 20 degrees beyond the body's own stand, bends the head over 30 degrees against the chest, drops the head under 0.92 of its height, throws a hand over 2.4 torso heights from the chest or 0.9 over the head. The measurements (`simulation/GesturePoseData.kt`) are made on the desktop by `tools/gesture_poses.py` (the engine-test harness, nine bodies: human, dwarf, night elf, draenei, blood elf, orc, undead, gnome, 4 s of each animation every 100 ms); the table holds each animation's worst body. `ConversationPoses.vet` lets through Talk (60), Talk Question (65), a wave (67) and the stand; a Laugh, an Exclamation, a Bow, a Cheer, the nod (Yes, 185: it bends a dwarf and a night elf woman 33 degrees) and anything never measured play as plain Talk (a listener's nod stands), and the id is logged once to standard error (logcat: "conversation gesture N rejected (why)"). Tested: `unit-tests/.../simulation/PoseSanityTest` (synthetic poses, the measured table's verdicts, the log, the conversation clock). Desktop renders of two women and a man and a woman, before and after, looked at; not run on a phone.

## Partner sounds

Options > Sound > Partner sounds plays sounds with partner animations from the user's own files. A clip's sound event carries a sound name; put audio files (ogg, wav, mp3, m4a, aac, flac), single or in a zip, through Add sounds, named after those names (the exact name, the name without a `vo_` or `sfx_` prefix, numbered variants `name_01`, `name_02` of which one is picked at random, a file named by the name's 32- or 64-bit FNV-1a hash in hex, or a file called `default` for the rest). A sound with no file is simply silent. The files are kept in `files/partner-sounds`; there is an On box, a volume and Remove all. The game's own EA audio (SNR/SNS) is not decoded yet. The folder (`audio/PartnerSounds`, `SoundNames`) is unit-tested; the playback (`SceneAudio.partnerSound`) and the Options rows are compiled by CI only and have not run on a phone.

## Graphics and performance

Options > **Graphics** (`ui/GraphicsOptions.kt`; `graphics/GraphicsSettings.kt`, kept as JSON in the app's preferences under `graphics`) has preset chips (**Standard**, **High**, **Ultra**, **Custom**), the **Target frame rate** (60, 90 or 120; 60 by default), **Adaptive quality** (on by default), **Show performance overlay**, a line saying what the governor has lowered now, **Test for 10 seconds** (average fps, the 95th-percentile frame time, late frames), and a switch or slider for each effect. Moving one makes the preset Custom (or the preset it now matches). **Standard draws exactly as before**: with every effect off the frame goes straight to the screen (checked pixel for pixel against the previous build's render). High and Ultra turn on:

- **Render scale** (60 to 125 %; Ultra 125 %, supersampled) with contrast-adaptive sharpening on the resample (**Sharpen**, and more by itself below 100 %).
- **Anti-aliasing**: 2x or 4x MSAA on the scene's target (multisampled renderbuffers, resolved by a blit).
- **Texture filtering**: 2x to 16x anisotropic (GL_EXT_texture_filter_anisotropic) on every texture the scene loads and on composed skins; every texture loaded from the game is mipmapped. **Texture detail** (Standard, High, Full) takes a larger mip of the game's file where it has one: each kind of thing's biggest texture (places 512, props 256, people 1024; composed skins are 2048 whatever it is) times 1, 2 or 4, for what is loaded after the change (High in Ultra; Full is the Options page's alone). Each doubling can take up to four times the memory of what it enlarges; measured with the harness's `texmem`: the Lion's Pride bedroom with two people 23.1 MB at Standard, 25.1 MB at High (the people's textures), 25.1 MB at Full, and Goldshire outside 22.0, 24.0 and 24.0 MB: no file in either place has a mip larger than High's caps, so Full adds nothing there and helps only where the game's files are bigger (4096 people, 1024 and 2048 places and props).
- **Tone curve** on a half-float (RGBA16F, else RGBA8) picture: **Filmic** leaves everything under the brightest light as it was (skin tones kept) and rolls the highlights off; **ACES** is the fitted ACES curve with mid-grey kept (deeper shadows); **Exposure** in stops.
- **Bloom**: a soft threshold and a dual-filter (Kawase) pyramid from half size down to 1/32; subtle (windows, candles, the sky).
- **Depth of field**: each pixel's circle of confusion from its depth, zero over a band in view depth (the renderer's own, when the app gives none: the nearest to the farthest person near the camera's target, feet to head, a little either side; **Focus follows the NPC**, below, gives it the subject's), a 24-tap disc gather at half size that keeps a sharp near thing from taking the blur behind it; **Aperture** sets how shallow. Off in the creator and the placement editor unless **Depth of field in the creator** is on.
- **Ambient occlusion** (half size, from the depth with normals from its neighbours; 6, 10 or 16 samples by **Occlusion quality**; a depth-aware blur) and **Contact shadows** (a short march toward the key light in the depth), both weighted to the light's ambient share.
- **Shadows**: the key light's shadow map (the sun or moon; in cinematic mode its key) over a box 2.6 yards round the camera's target, 1024 px, or 2048 px with **Soft shadows** (PCSS-lite: the penumbra widens with the blocker's distance), sloped bias and a normal offset. In a room only the people cast (the room's own light is baked into it); outside, the walls, props and ground near them cast too. Applied in screen space from the depth (the lit picture darkened by the key light's share), so the scene's own shaders are unchanged.
- **Skin shading** (wrap light, a warm scatter at the light's edge, a soft Fresnel sheen and highlight) and **Hair highlights** (Kajiya-Kay, the strands taken as running down) on the people's skin and hair textures (types 1 and 6; the composed skin includes clothes painted on it, so they get it too); cloth flaps keep the plain light.
- **Eye highlights**: the game's people have eyes of their own (geoset 3301, texture type 19, weighted to the L_Eye and R_Eye bones), a flat lens whose normals all face forward, so the eyeball is taken as a sphere round its bone's pivot (its centre in every model checked: human women and men, a draenei and a night elf woman, a dwarf and a goblin man) and given a small sharp catchlight and a broad wet sheen. The catchlight sits on the key light's side of the point that faces the camera and a little under it, where the lids leave it seen (as a film's eye light puts it), so it shows whichever way the key comes from, brighter as the key faces the eye.
- **Thin-skin tint**: ears, the nose, the lips and the fingers lit through, warm: a wider wrap, the warm scatter, and the key light shining through toward the eye when it is behind them. What is thin comes from the flesh profile (`flesh.h`, measured once per look): in the face, what sticks out past the head's measured flesh in front (nose, lips) or at the sides (ears), in the head's own frame as it is posed; in the hands, past the middle of the hand's measured length (the fingers). Found on the HD skin's layout (a 2048 x 1024 material: the face its right half, the hands the band under the arms); a person of the old layout keeps the plain light there.
- **Skin sheen follows intensity** with **Maximum sheen** (High 40 %, Ultra 60 %): each person's skin grows a wet sheen (glossier highlights, the room's light mirrored from above, the colour 10 % darker at full) from the moment's intensity (immersive mode's `IntensityModel`, eased): none below 0.35, the maximum at 0.9, eased again with a 6 s rise and a 12 s fall so it builds and lingers (`graphics/GraphicsSettings.kt`'s `SkinSheen`; `Native.sheen`, `Scene::setSheen`).
- **Light shafts** (the open sky, most round the sun, and a little of the brightest windows and lamps, blurred toward the sun's place on the screen in 32 jittered steps at quarter size, only with the sun ahead: rays through foliage and past roofs, a warm haze round the sun; subtle, not noisy, at Ultra's 50 %) and **Haze** (depth haze in the area's fog colour): Ultra only by default. The atmosphere's motes are Options' Immersion (Glowing motes).
- **Vignette** (High and Ultra, light), **Film grain** and **Chromatic aberration** (off). While cinematic mode's grade is on it draws its own vignette and grain and these are left to it; the chain hands it the finished picture (`engine/grade.cpp` grades after `engine/postfx.cpp`).
- **Focus follows the NPC** (Graphics page, Camera, on by default; `simulation/FocusTarget.kt`, pure and JVM-tested in `FocusTargetTest`; `LiveScene.focusFrame` feeds it, `Native.focus` -> `Scene::setFocus` hands the band to the renderer). Depth of field's sharp band used to be the people within 4 yards of the camera's *target* plus the target's own distance, so in Orbit (which turns round a point of the room, not round the NPC) it sat on that point and kept everyone round it sharp. Now it is on whom the camera is about, recomputed every frame while orbiting, panning, zooming or following: the selected NPC's chest (the skeleton's bones as drawn, as JOI's camera reads them), or the midpoint between her chest and her partner's while they play a two-person animation (within 6 yards of each other; else her alone); in a cinematic director's shot the point it looks at and the people within 2 yards of it; in JOI and Chat mode her (the point the camera tracks; head, chest and feet give the depth); in Build mode, with nobody selected, the pivot. The band is the depth along the view axis of the centre and of head, chest and feet, padded 0.25 yd in front and 0.35 yd behind (less when close), and never thinner than a face is deep (0.18 yd + 0.05 per yard of distance), so a close-up keeps the face sharp and the background soft. The near and far are eased by critically damped springs (about 0.2 s to settle, the same at any frame rate), so a cut or a new selection glides instead of popping. The blur's strength is full up to 3 yards and falls 6 % a yard (to 60 %) for far shots. The renderer's depth of field is a plane in depth (each pixel's circle of confusion from its linear depth), not a screen point, so a subject off the middle of the screen stays sharp wherever she is drawn; `FocusTarget` still reports where her centre falls on the screen (`screenX`, `screenY`). Build mode's **Sharp image** override still turns depth of field off. Off: the old band (the setting is `focusFollows`). Rendered in the harness (`focus NEAR FAR [STRENGTH]` command; `unit-tests/.../tools/FocusStillsRecorder.kt` writes the numbers): an orbit of four angles round a point 3 yards from a standing woman, the camera looking past her so she is off-centre, with the old band on the left and this on the right. In the old stills everyone near the orbit point is sharp, she with them; in the new ones she is the sharp one and the people nearer and farther than her band blur. Not run on a phone; the live selection, partner and shot hooks (`LiveScene`, `SceneSessionScreen`) are compiled by CI only.
- **Sharp image in Build mode** (Graphics page, on by default; `graphics/GraphicsSettings.kt`'s `SharpImage`, pure and JVM-tested in `SharpImageTest`). The renderer blends no frame into the next (no temporal accumulation, history or reprojection anywhere in `engine/postfx.cpp`, `grade.cpp` or the scene), so what can look like motion blur while the camera orbits or people move is a spatial blur laid over the picture: depth of field (the live scene keeps it on; only the creator and the placement editor skip it), the light shafts' radial blur toward the sun, bloom's halo, chromatic aberration's fringes, film grain, the haze, a render scale the governor pulled under the screen's, and cinematic mode's grade. While Build mode is on (also with cinematic mode on) the renderer is given the settings with depth of field, bloom, light shafts, haze, chromatic aberration and grain at 0, the cinematic mood, vignette and grain at 0 (the letterbox bars stay: they are framing), and the governor skips both render-scale steps and lowers shadows and AO first, then MSAA and the people's shading. Everything else (MSAA, anisotropy, tone, shadows, AO, sharpen) stays the user's, the render scale is the user's exactly, and the user's own settings come back when Build mode ends (the override never writes them). Turn **Sharp image in Build mode** off to keep Build mode drawn like the rest of the scene. Not run on a phone; the desktop harness's before and after stills are the only picture of it.

**Frames.** The scene view is paced on the screen's vsync (`graphics/Graphics.kt`: a Choreographer callback asks for a frame on every n-th vsync, 60 fps on a 120 Hz screen being every second one; the screen is also asked for the target's rate on Android 11 and later). Each pass is timed on the GPU where the driver has EXT_disjoint_timer_query. The **governor** (`graphics/Governor.kt`) takes the 95th percentile of the last half second of frames four times a second: over budget (frames missed, or the work over 97 % of the frame) for 1.5 s, it lowers one step, then waits 2 s; with 30 % to spare for 8 s it raises one back, and a step up that doesn't hold doubles that wait (up to 64 s). The steps, in order: render scale to 85 %, AO and shadow quality, bloom, depth of field, MSAA, light shafts and haze, render scale to 70 %, AO and shadows, the thin-skin tint, skin, hair and sheen shading, the eyes' highlights (a few pixels: the cheapest, last). Texture detail is memory, not frame time, and is never lowered. The phone's thermal status (moderate, severe, or headroom nearly gone) keeps one or two steps down. Adaptive quality off: the settings are drawn as set.

**Contact shadows without the mesh's triangles** (the blocky pale and dark patches on a neck, collarbone and shoulder). With Contact shadows on, a body under hair or a jaw showed its triangles: stair-stepped patches with straight edges, worst on the dense HD bodies (the shadow darkens only the light a surface takes, and the lit pass worked that share out from a normal made of `dFdx`/`dFdy` of the depth, which is one triangle's flat face; the contact test was also a yes or no per half-size pixel). Now (`engine/postfx.cpp`) the lit pass takes the surface's direction from the depth over about 2.5 cm round the pixel (`smoothNormal`: four taps, each side counting by how near it is in depth so a hair card over a shoulder fades out of the slope instead of switching it); the contact term is a coverage (0 to 1 as the ray's point passes behind the depth, the deepest of 16 steps) and not a hit or a miss; the two blur passes' taps are 1.6 texels apart. Shadow-map shadows and AO are unchanged; with Contact shadows off the picture is the same as before. Checked in desktop renders only (the HD human and night elf woman, the game's body and the since scrapped Eve body, day and dark cinematic light, front and three-quarter views; before and after side by side): the triangles are gone from the neck and shoulders; what is left is the contact shadow's own shape under the hair (a soft edge with a little grain) and a faint sliver along a shoulder's silhouette. No automated test: a measure of the facets that told the old picture from the new was tried (the lit factor's gradient inside the shadow) and did not separate them, so none is shipped. Not seen on a phone.

What each effect cost in the desktop harness (`engine-test`: `gfx`, `gfxtiming`, `gfxstats`; Mesa llvmpipe, software GL on a 4-core VM, 1280x720, the Lion's Pride bedroom with two people; the frame itself took about 72 ms): the chain with tone mapping alone about +13 ms, bloom +13, depth of field +19, AO +25 to +40, contact shadows +25, shadows +16, skin and hair 0, shafts and haze +9, 4x MSAA +61, render scale 70 % -5, 125 % +15, everything at once about 230 ms. **These are software-rendering numbers and say nothing about a phone's GPU; no effect has been measured on a phone.** 60 fps on a Snapdragon 8 Elite-class phone is the design target the governor holds to by lowering effects, not a measured fact. The settings, presets, steps, governor and pacing arithmetic are unit-tested (`unit-tests/.../graphics/`); the effects were rendered on and off in the harness with no GL error and looked at; the Options page, the pacer and the overlay are compiled by CI only.

The people's eye highlights, thin-skin tint and sheen were rendered on and off in the harness (`gfx eyes=1`, `gfx thinskin=1`, `sheen NAME VALUE`; a human woman's face close up in the Lion's Pride, a night elf's ears in profile and her fingers, and the night elf backlit by the evening sun outside Goldshire), and the light shafts in Goldshire at 8 in the morning with the sun behind the inn's roof and the trees (`sun` prints where the sun is): all with no GL error, looked at (skin tones kept, no noise). Their cost in the scene pass was within the harness's run-to-run noise (software GL). With every effect off the frame is pixel for pixel the previous build's (the face, the bedroom and Goldshire checked), and the bedroom with skin and hair shading alone too. The sheen's maths and its easing, the settings, presets and steps are unit-tested; the sheen's path from the scene to the renderer (`SceneSessionScreen`, `SceneView.glSheen`) is compiled by CI only, and none of it has run on a phone.

## Dialogue

NPCs speak with an on-device language model (the runtime's one shared model, llama.cpp), one instance for all of them, each NPC a person of Azeroth. Every spoken line is the model's: the only authored text is prompts and lore data (`assets/dialogue/lore.json`, `assets/dialogue/kink/*`); with no model loaded the layer is inactive and the NPCs keep the scripted small talk (`Chatter`) exactly as before. Nothing is kept between sessions: the rolling history, the comfort between pairs, the steers and the willingness sessions live in memory and are cleared with the scene (`DialogueDirector.reset`, a new director per scene). Code: `dialogue/` (pure Kotlin, JVM-tested), the simulation's hook (`SceneSimulation.dialogue`), `LiveScene.dialogue` (the feed), `ui/DialogueUi.kt`, `ui/DialogueOptions.kt`.

**The model's side.** `DialogueEngine.complete(DialogueRequest)` is the one interface (`dialogue/DialogueEngine.kt`; the runtime implements it and sets `DialogueRuntime.engine` when its model is loaded, clears it on unload; directors ask for it each time, so a model loaded mid-scene is used from then on). `ModelRunner` runs one request at a time in priority order (willingness and persuasion first, then the NPCs near the camera and the selected one), answers are handed over on the scene's thread, a request waiting too long is dropped. Options: the Dialogue page (switch, Quiet/Normal/Chatty; `Settings.dialogue`) has a slot (`DownloadOptions.dialogueExtra`) for the runtime's model picker.

**Persona and prompt** (`Persona.kt`, `Lore.kt`, `SceneContext.kt`). The system prompt is stable per NPC (name, race, class, sex, the traits chosen at creation or the ones the life simulation gives them, the bio, the voice of their race, how they are in bed, the rules of the world, their people's lore) so the runtime can cache its prefix. It makes them a full-grown adult of Azeroth speaking in their own idiom (gold, ale, the Light, Orgrimmar; no modern speech, slang, machines or brands), in short lines, saying only the words spoken; it never names what it forbids (anything beyond Azeroth, or talk of unseen hands moving them, simply puzzles them). What changes per line goes into the user message: where they are (place, indoors or out, the hour, the weather, the furniture at hand), who else is here (name, race, class, faction, what they are doing now), what just happened (arrivals, a stage begun or ended), the lore the scene needs (retrieved by who is present: their place, the stance of their race and faction on each other race present, and, only when allowed, the body of the partner's race), the comfort with the partner, the steers, the mode and the task. Race and class lore (`races`, `classes`, `factions`, `places`, `stances`) is original text in the lore file; stances are asymmetric (a Forsaken on the Alliance is not the Alliance on the Forsaken). `Disposition` (caution, warmth, boldness, prejudice, curiosity, devotion) is worked out from the traits and the bio's words and tunes the voice, the pace of loosening and the willingness.

**The director** (`DialogueDirector.kt`). Driven by the live scene every 0.2 s with a `SceneView` (`LiveScene.dialogueView`); it gives speech turns from a budget: a sexual stage's pair take turns (mode LIVE, every 5.5 s at Normal), a conversation's pair alternate for two to six turns (more for curious, warm natures) on a topic seed or the steer, a lone NPC remarks now and then; cooldowns, the talkativeness setting, priority to the selected and the near. A conversation's words come from the model: the simulation plays it with its walk-up, gestures and ending but with blank lines (`VOICED_LINES`) and the director speaks through `SceneSimulation.say`. Lines are cut to one or two sentences and 180 characters at a sentence end, stripped of name tags, quotes and stage directions; an answer with an anachronism (`Anachronisms`: okay, guys, dude, phone, internet, coffee, weekend, boyfriend...) is asked for again once and then dropped; a model that says nothing leaves them silent (no canned line). The per-NPC history is eight turns, in memory.

**Gating.** Two modes (`TalkMode`). WORLD: ordinary talk about the world, the day, each other, the place, lore, and, by nature and ease, desire, bodies, memories and plans: topic talk is allowed at any time, never forced (a roll tuned by nature and comfort decides whether a line may turn to it). LIVE: only while a sexual stage is playing: the sounds and the running commentary of what is happening now (what is being done, what they like, what they want next, a different position). `LiveFilter` drops or rewrites a live-vocalisation line ("oh yeah", "harder", "don't stop", "I'm coming", "you're inside me") spoken outside a sexual stage, keeping what is left when it still says something. Cinematic mode: the director idles (no model calls, no bubbles, a line that arrives late is dropped, no question is asked before a stage). **What is a sexual stage:** `DialogueGate.isSexual` uses the app's own classifications: a library pair whose name `PairKinds` reads as `Intimacy.INTIMATE` (the libraries' unnamed pairs are lovers'; kisses, embraces, cuddles, dances, hugs, play and handshakes are not), unless the pack's category is CALM; a Warcraft emote never is; the screen may plug the arousal map's `StageClass.intimate` in (`SceneSimulation.sexualStage`).

**Comfort** (`Comfort.kt`). Per pair, this scene only: stages together (1 each, up to 3 for a long one), trust (the life simulation's friendship and romance), kindness, minus hesitations and refusals, read through each NPC's own pace (a shy one loosens slowly, a bold one fast): strangers, familiar, comfortable, very comfortable, uninhibited. As the band rises the prompt lets the talk get bolder, kinkier and stranger (`kinks` in the lore file), and from comfortable up the partner's race and body (`bodies`: Forsaken cold skin, no pulse and loose flesh, gnomes small and light, orcs and tauren huge, worgen fur and claws, draenei hooves and tails, ...) is on offer in the moment and in the desire talk. The band maps onto the kink pack's (`ComfortBand.kink`: familiar to acquaintances, comfortable to friendly, very comfortable to intimate), whose notes, a sample, a consent-care line and, from intimate, an in-world JOI plan (one adult instructing the other, `KinkPack.joiPlan`, in LIVE turns only) join the prompt. Only adults; everyone decides for themselves.

**Willingness** (`Willingness.kt`, `Consent.kt`). Before a sexual stage involving them starts (a player's order or an NPC's own invitation; the invitee decides, and the initiator too when the player ordered it) the simulation asks `DialogueHook.consent`. The resistance (0 to 1) starts from a prior: a base, the faction and race attitude (Alliance toward the Forsaken highest, then other Horde; Horde toward the Alliance, the Forsaken toward the living, a night elf after Teldrassil, a blood elf's fastidiousness...), scaled by prejudice, lowered by curiosity, warmth and boldness, raised by caution and devotion, lowered by trust and familiarity. The model answers `{"willing": yes|hesitant|no|maybe-later, "reason": "..."}` (parsed robustly; unreadable: the prior stands, which mostly says yes) and moves it within 0.25 of the prior. Under 0.25 they are willing (about nine pairs in ten of ordinary people); from 0.25 to 0.72 **hesitant**: they voice the fear in character, the stage waits where they stand, and they can be **calmed**: the partner NPC tries (the model, in character, with the approach that fits the fear), or the player taps a chip on the persuasion bar (Reassure, Flatter, Be patient, Joke, Appeal to curiosity, Show kindness). Each try moves the resistance by how well it fits their nature and fear (`WillingnessModel.fit`, tuned by the model's reading of the reply, within 0.15 of the heuristic); under 0.22 they agree, in their own words, and the stage begins; three poor tries, five of any kind, or 60 s unanswered end it. From 0.72 they refuse for now: their own line, the invitation ends with no stage, a cooldown of 150 s (`maybe-later` the same) and a mark in the comfort ledger. Most hesitant NPCs are won within one to three tries. With no model, or the layer off, or cinematic on, nothing is asked and the stage starts as it always did; a decision that takes over 12 s goes on as if willing.

**Steer** (`Steer.kt`, scene menu, "Steer the talk"). The player nudges, never scripts: topic chips (the Scourge, the war, gossip, flirt, their past, the weather, the inn, the Light, old grudges, desire, a fantasy, the others here) or free text, Gentle or Firm, on the whole scene, one NPC or a pair, shown as chips and clearable. A steer is a soft hint in the prompt; the NPC weaves it in, in character, and may resist if it goes against their nature.

**Memory budget (computed, not measured).** Mistral Nemo: 40 layers, 8 KV heads of 128, so one token of cache is 40 x 8 x 128 x 2 = 81,920 values, 87 KB at q8: 6,144 tokens are **0.53 GB**. Weights **7.48 GB** (a CPU-repacked file counts in full as anonymous memory; a mapped one is file-backed and the OS can drop it under pressure), compute buffers about **0.3 GB**: the model takes about **8.3 GB**. The rest of the app (the engine's models, textures, the renderer) is taken as 2 to 3 GB, so the whole is about **10.8 GB, roughly 70% of the 15 GB a "16 GB" phone reports**: the top of the 60 to 70% an Android phone tolerates in practice for a foreground app. A phone with 12 GB (about 11 GB reported) cannot hold it comfortably: the Options page warns when the total memory is below 13 GiB (tight from 11.5, "made for 16 GB phones" below that) and lets the player try anyway. The Standard 4B (3.3 GB) and Lite 1.7B (1.5 GB) models are **gone**: an old model's id in the settings moves to this one, an old file is never loaded, and Options offers to delete the old files (and their saved NPC places) to free the space.

**Speed estimate (an estimate, not a measurement; no phone here).** Token generation is memory-bandwidth bound: 7.48 GB are read per token, and a Snapdragon 8 Gen 2, 8 Gen 3 or 8 Elite gives about 40 to 70 GB/s, so at best 5 to 9 tokens a second and, at 60 to 75% of that in practice, **about 4 to 6** (a 100-token line in 15 to 25 s, streaming at about reading speed). Reading a prompt without i8mm (the first build used only `armv8.2-a+dotprod+fp16`; the build now carries i8mm kernels too and picks at run time) is perhaps **15 to 30 tokens a second** for a 12 B model: a persona of 600 tokens takes **20 to 40 s once** and is then kept per NPC (the slot cache, and the file for evicted NPCs). So the defaults changed with the model: unprompted talk is paid from **300 tokens a minute** (was 900; at 4 to 6 tokens a second the old figure was never a limit), requests wait up to **240 s (selected), 120 (pair), 60 (nearby), 40 (background)** (twice as long), prompts are read in 128-token chunks (a cancel or a pause between chunks is felt in seconds, not tens of seconds), and the start of text mark Nemo wants (`<s>`) is added to every prompt by the core (Qwen took none; the same for every prompt, so cached prefixes still match). Loading reads the whole file: it happens on a background thread and the Options page shows a percentage (`NativeDialogue.nativeLoadProgress`); a failed load (out of memory, a bad file) frees what was loaded and shows why. The runtime-selected i8mm kernels, which roughly double prompt reading, are in the build now (see "Why replies are slow and what we did" below).

**What was checked of this model here:** the file's header (the first 16 MB, read with a Range request: nothing else was downloaded, the disk had 3.6 GB free): architecture `llama`, 40 blocks, 8 KV heads, tokenizer `tekken` with `<|im_start|>` = token 14 and `<|im_end|>` = token 15 as single tokens, EOS 15, BOS `<s>` added, and the embedded chat template is plain ChatML, identical to `ChatTemplate.CHATML` (a test pins the rendered text). llama.cpp itself loaded that header as a vocabulary (a sparse file padded to full size, `vocab_only`) and tokenized a rendered ChatML prompt: `<|im_start|>` and `<|im_end|>` come out as the single tokens 14 and 15, `<|im_end|>` is an end-of-generation token, `add_bos` is true and with the core's start-of-text rule the prompt begins with token 1 (`<s>`). Built against the llama.cpp checkout at v1.0.0 (a later tree than the app's pinned tag, same API for these calls). **Not checked: the model's actual answers.** Its erotic-writing ability is taken from its model card, the UGI leaderboard's scores and community reputation, not from a generation here (7.5 GB did not fit on the disk): run `engine-test/dialogue_build.sh LLAMA_DIR` then `build/dialogue/dialogue_test MODEL.gguf` on a machine with room.

**How it runs** (`dialogue_core.cpp`, plain C++17, `Core`): one model and one context; each NPC owns a **sequence of the KV cache** (6 slots by default, the cache shared: 6,144 tokens, quantised to 8 bits, about 0.53 GB for the 12B model). A turn finds the NPC's slot, keeps the prefix of the new prompt the slot already holds, and reads only the rest, so a persona is read once; **a new NPC starts from the longest prefix another NPC's slot holds** (the shared world preamble costs nothing: `seq_cp` shares cells); when the slots run out the oldest NPC's state is **written to a file** (`files/dialogue/state/<model>/`) and read back when it speaks again, and every slot is written when the app leaves the foreground. Prompts are read in 256-token chunks and the answer is sampled one token at a time, streamed as whole UTF-8 characters, with stop strings (held back so a half-written stop never shows); cancelling (a ticket, from any thread) ends a prompt chunk or a token within milliseconds and leaves the slot consistent. Threads: **Options -> Dialogue -> Speed** (Auto, or 3 to 6 by hand): Auto writes with as many threads as the phone has performance cores up to 4 and reads the prompt with up to 6, kept on those cores (`CpuProfile`, `ThreadPlan`: the cores within 70% of the fastest one's frequency; the model's threads inherit the generating thread's core mask, set with `sched_setaffinity`); the worker runs at the normal priority for the selected NPC and a pair and a low one for background talk (it was the lowest for everything, the nice value the model's threads inherit).

**The queue** (`DialogueScheduler`, pure Kotlin, JVM-tested): one worker thread, one generation at a time. `DialogueRequest` keeps the layer's signature and adds optional fields (`priority`, `maxWaitMs`, `topP`, `repeatPenalty`, `seed`, `prefillOnly`, `onPartial`); `DialogueEngine` gains `ready`, `forget(npcId)` and `setFocus(selected, pair)` with defaults. Order: the selected NPC (and any NPC `setFocus` names), then a talking pair, then nearby NPCs, then the background; a running background answer is cancelled for an urgent request and run again after it. Unprompted talk is paid from a **token budget** (300 tokens a minute by default, 400 saved up; the selected NPC and a pair are never held but still pay). **The renderer comes first:** `Graphics` hands the governor's step to `DialogueHost.renderLevel`; at step 1 unprompted prompts are read slowly (30 ms between chunks) and each streamed piece waits 25 ms, at step 2 or more only the selected NPC is answered (its prompt chunks wait 20 ms, pieces 40 ms) and everything else waits. A request waits at most 240 s (selected), 120 (pair), 60 (nearby) or 40 s (background), a newer unprompted line of an NPC replaces its older one in the queue, the background queue holds six. Cancelling the calling coroutine cancels the request. `DialogueModel.generate(npcId, messages, maxTokens, stop, temperature, priority): Flow<String>` streams; `DialogueModel.load` sets `DialogueRuntime.engine` and unloading clears it. With no model the engine is `NoDialogueEngine` and answers null.

**Measured on this desktop** (4 cores, x86-64 AVX2, `engine-test/dialogue_test.cpp` against real GGUF files, 4 threads, q8 KV cache, 4,096 context): Qwen2.5-0.5B Q4_K_M: 35 to 40 tokens a second, a 156-token persona read in 0.74 s the first time and 0.11 s with the prefix reused (198 of 216 tokens reused), a second NPC with the same preamble borrowed 127 tokens, an evicted NPC came back from its file (194 tokens restored), the greedy answer after another NPC's turn, after a cancel and from a fresh load were identical (the sequences don't leak into each other), cancelling took effect in well under a second, stop strings and streaming matched. Huihui-Qwen3-1.7B (a small model used only as a test file, not offered by the app any more): 18 to 19 tokens a second, prompt reading about 1,100 tokens a second, 1.4 GB of anonymous memory with two NPCs warm (the weights are repacked for the CPU, so they are not file-backed: count the whole file, plus the cache). **The 12B model has not been run here at all** (7.5 GB did not fit on the disk); what a phone will do with it is in the estimates above, and they are estimates.

**Tested and not tested.** JVM tests (`DialogueSchedulerTest`: order, focus, preemption, cancelling queued and running requests, the budget, load yielding, expiry, replacing and capping, forget, no model, errors, streaming, closing; `DialogueTemplateModelsTest` (the one-model catalogue, old ids and files migrating, the memory warning, the ChatML text, the stop marks), the longer waits and the repeat-penalty cap in `DialogueSchedulerTest`; `ModelDownloaderTest` against a local server: resume, ignored Range, dropped connections and 503s, wrong checksum, cancelling, 416, no room, a missing file) and the C++ core on the desktop as above. **Never built for Android and never run on a phone:** the JNI file (syntax-checked with g++ and clang against the JDK's `jni.h`), the CMake fetch of llama.cpp under the NDK, the Android service, the Options section, the governor hook and the model's speed and memory there. The 12B model has not been run at all (disk). Run it yourself: `engine-test/dialogue_build.sh LLAMA_DIR` then `build/dialogue/dialogue_test MODEL.gguf`.

## Why replies are slow and what we did

The player's report: "It is taking FOREVER for the LLM to answer." (the earlier round; the "under 5 seconds" round is the next section) Everything below the first table is **an estimate for a phone** (nothing here has run on one) except where it says measured; the numbers that were measured here are the **prompt sizes** (the real Mistral-Nemo tokenizer, read from the GGUF's header, over the real prompts the code builds) and the **small-model desktop run**. The phone figures use the earlier estimates: reading a prompt 15 to 30 tokens a second (dotprod only), 30 to 60 with i8mm (estimated: about double), writing 4 to 6.

**Where the time went (measured, token counts).** A reply costs `queue wait + read what is new in the prompt + write the answer`, and the read dominated. A chat turn's old prompt put the person, the place, the lore (up to 900 characters) and every rule into the *last* user message, so it differed from the turn before right after the persona, and the model re-read all of it each turn. An ambient line began with "You are <name>", so no NPC could borrow another's prompt.

| Prompt (Mistral-Nemo tokens) | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First chat prompt (persona, context, first message) | 954 | 829 |
| ... of which already in the model once warmed up (the persona when the setup sheet opens: 442; plus the context when the chat starts: 627) | 0 | 442, then 627 |
| A later, plain chat turn: tokens that are new to the prompt | 450 to 600 (the whole last message, again) | about 135, of which her own previous reply (about 35) is already in the model: about 100 to read. The three opening turns: 190 |
| A flirting turn on the turn that also cuts the history (the kink notes and the register in its message) | 605 | 604 (measured); a flirting turn without a cut is about 300 more than a plain one (estimated from the notes' size) |
| Share of the new prompt that the model already holds (chat) | 49 to 55% (the persona only) | 81 to 92% (all of the previous prompt; 90% or more from the fourth turn) |
| First ambient line (persona + scene message) | 739 | 617 |
| Tokens shared with another NPC's first prompt (the world and its rules) | 6 | 197 |

So a later chat turn read about 450 to 600 tokens, which is **15 to 40 s** at the old estimated reading speed before the first word, then 10 to 20 s of writing; and a failed word check asked it all again. Estimated now: **about 100 tokens to read** on a later turn, **2 to 4 s** at i8mm speed (3 to 7 s at dotprod speed) before the first word, then about 7 to 15 s of writing for 40 to 60 tokens (it stops at its sentences): roughly **10 to 20 s in all against 25 to 60 s**. The first turn of a chat, warmed up while the setup sheet is open and the player types, reads only its message (about 190 tokens: 3 to 6 s at i8mm speed, 6 to 13 s at dotprod speed); cold, the whole prompt (829 tokens: 14 to 28 s at i8mm speed, 28 to 55 s at dotprod speed). The turn that also cuts the history re-reads about 300 tokens more (the kept messages in their short form) once every three exchanges. These are estimates: **read the real numbers off the phone with "Copy timings" (below)**.

**What changed.**

1. **Telemetry.** Every request the scheduler runs leaves a `ReplyTiming` in a ring of the last 100 (`TimingLog`): the queue wait, the first word's time from being asked, the total, the prompt's tokens split into read / already held (hit) / borrowed from another NPC (the shared preamble) / restored from the file, the prefill time and speed, the tokens written and the writing speed, the model's own first-token time, the NPC's place size in tokens, the thread counts, how it ended (a sentence stop, a stream-check stop, a rerun after a preemption). The native core measures the model's side (`GenResult.firstTokenMs`, `kvTokens`, `threads`). **Options -> Dialogue -> Dialogue model -> Speed** shows "Last replies: first word after X s, Y tok/s" (the median of the last 10 answers) and the detail line, a **Speed test** (a 64-token answer: first word, tokens a second, prompt speed; a hint when below 3 tokens a second: "This phone is slow for this model; try Speed: Auto or close other apps") and **Copy timings** (to the clipboard: the model, the load time in all and split into model and context, the CPU's cores, frequencies and features and the kernels expected (`CpuVariants`), llama.cpp's own system line (which kernels really run: `MATMUL_INT8 = 1` means i8mm), the thread plan, then a line per request). It is not wired into the Build-report issues: the player can paste the copied text. To read it: `first word` is what the player felt; `wait` the part behind other requests; `read` the tokens the model read this time and `prefill ... tok/s` how fast; `hit / borrowed / restored / miss` how the NPC's place served the request (a miss on a later turn means the place was evicted or the prompt changed early); `decode ... tok/s` the writing speed (below 3: slow phone, look at `threads` and the kernels line); `kv` the cache in use.
2. **Prompt layout** (`PersonaPrompt`, `ChatPrompt`, `TurnPrompt`; the stable part first, the new words last). The system prompt is now **the shared world and rules** (`PersonaPrompt.SHARED`, the same bytes for every NPC and mode), then the NPC, then a closing line (ambient: `LENGTH_AMBIENT`; chat: `LENGTH_CHAT` and the chat's frame); a new NPC starts from the shared 197 tokens another NPC holds (`seq_cp`: no copy, no read), and an NPC's chat persona starts with the very bytes of her ambient persona. The chat's **context** (the place, who stands before her, what they are to her, at most two lore snippets in 420 characters, the "you are only talking" line) moved from the last message into the system message: it is read once and changes only when what they are to her changes. What is left in each turn's message (`ChatPrompt.tail`) is how at ease they are, the opening stance for the first three exchanges, the wariness, the desire notes when the talk goes there, the steers, what was said and the task. **The history repeats what was sent** (a plain turn's tail as it was), so the whole previous prompt is a prefix of the next (measured: 100% of it); a turn whose message grew big (desire notes) is kept as what was said. The history is **dropped in blocks** (12 messages max, cut to 6 at once, the kept messages going back to what was said, since the model re-reads from there anyway) because dropping one at a time changes the prompt right after the persona every turn. Caps: the persona's race and side paragraphs 220 and 110 characters, ambient scene messages at most three people listed and two lore snippets in 400 characters, the kink pack's snippets 2 (was 3). Tests pin it: `PromptCacheTest` (the system bytes the same every turn, each prompt starting with the whole previous one, at least 90% of the previous prompt shared, blocks, the caps).
3. **Warm-up** (`DialogueEngine.warm`, `DialogueScheduler.warm`). A request that only reads the prompt (maxTokens 0: nothing is sampled) into the NPC's place, never charged to the unprompted-talk budget, behind real talk when ordinary, first when urgent, replaced by a newer one for the same NPC, at most 8 waiting, a running one yields to an urgent request and carries on afterwards, the same persona asked twice is free. The director warms the selected NPC at once and the nearest four of the scene, once each, when the model is ready (again when it was reloaded). **Opening the Chat sheet warms her chat persona** (442 tokens) while the player chooses who they are, and **starting the chat warms the whole system prompt** (her persona and the context, 627 tokens) while they type the first message.
4. **Kept across runs.** Each NPC's place was already written to a file when evicted and when the app leaves the foreground, and read back on its next request (`files/dialogue/state/<model>/`, keyed by NPC; a stale file is a true prefix, the longest common prefix is used). The file is now **kept after it is read back** (it was deleted), so a crash before the next flush loses nothing.
5. **Faster CPU kernels, chosen on the phone.** The build was fixed at `armv8.2-a+dotprod+fp16`. ggml's own mechanism is used now: `GGML_BACKEND_DL` + `GGML_CPU_ALL_VARIANTS` + `GGML_NATIVE=OFF` build the CPU backend seven times (`android_armv8.0_1` plain NEON, `armv8.2_1` dotprod, `armv8.2_2` + fp16, `armv8.6_1` + **i8mm**, `armv9.0_1` + SVE2, `armv9.2_1/2` + SME) as `libggml-cpu-<variant>.so`; at load `ggml_backend_load_all_from_path` (the folder `libwcdialogue.so` is in, found with `dladdr`) loads each, asks how well it fits this CPU and keeps the best: **a variant that needs an instruction the CPU lacks scores 0 and is never run** (the floor, plain NEON, is in every arm64 phone), so no phone meets an illegal instruction. The libraries must be real files: `packaging.jniLibs.useLegacyPackaging = true` extracts them at install. `CpuVariants` is the same table in Kotlin (for the page and the tests; ggml decides). i8mm roughly doubles prompt reading and Q4_K weights are repacked for it (ggml's `repack`); every Snapdragon 8 Gen 1 and later has it. **Never built for Android here** (the NDK is not here; the CMake configuration was only checked to configure on the desktop with these options, and the sources syntax-checked with `-DGGML_BACKEND_DL`); this is the part most likely to need a fix when CI builds it.
6. **Threads** (`CpuProfile`, `ThreadPlan`, Options -> Speed): see "How it runs". The model's own threads are kept on the performance cores by the generating thread's affinity mask (a refusal by a vendor just leaves all cores allowed).
7. **Output length and retries.** A chat reply's token cap is by its length setting: **Short 45, Normal 80, Long 140** (was 60, 100, 150) with a **sentence stop**: the answer ends at its last allowed sentence (`SentenceStop`; an ellipsis is a pause, an action in asterisks has no sentences) and nothing past it is read or shown. Unprompted lines are capped at **40 tokens** (48 at the filthiest levels), 56 for a pair or the NPC the player watches (was 64 to 76), two sentences. A **stream check** (`StreamGuard`: the anachronism words and the fourth wall, on the speech as it grows, actions ignored, a word still being written not judged) ends a wrong answer after its first words and the chat asks again once with a note at once (it used to read the whole answer, then ask the whole again); a second wrong answer is no answer. Unprompted talk is **not asked again**: the line is dropped (only a pair or the selected NPC gets its one retry); JOI's tries are 2 (was 3). A selected request cancels a running ambient answer within a token (the scheduler already did; `SchedulerSpeedTest` pins it).
8. **Streaming and waiting.** The chat bubble already showed the partial text as it came (`ChatSession.partial`). While no word has come the chat says "She is answering..." and after 3 s "She is thinking... 12 s"; with the model still loading, the "needs the model" card shows "Loading the model... 42%".

**What the character profiles add (another piece of work, merged in).** A named NPC's system prompt now ends with its profile's core (up to 280 tokens, `PersonaBudget.CORE`) and the era slice (150): about 430 more tokens to read once per NPC, which is what the warm-up is for (it reads the exact system text, the kit's addition included; an era change flushes the persona, the talk and the warm-up record). Its per-turn notes (relationships of those present, topics: up to 260 tokens, `PersonaBudget.TURN`) go at the front of the turn's message, so they are read anew every turn they apply: that is the one part of the new work that works against the cache; moving them into the system prompt (as the chat's context is) when they do not change from turn to turn would remove it. A chat turn carrying them is above `ChatSession.FAITHFUL_CHARS` and is kept in the history as what was said.

**Looked at and not done.**

- **Speculative decoding:** `llama.cpp` supports it at this tag, but the draft must share the vocabulary: Mistral Nemo's tekken vocabulary (131,072 tokens) has no 0.5B sibling, and a Qwen draft does not match. Skipped; no suitable draft exists to download.
- **GPU (Adreno OpenCL / Vulkan):** the OpenCL backend in `llama.cpp` is tuned for Q4_0 (and Q8_0) weights on Adreno, not this Q4_K_M file (it would run the rest on the CPU with copies in between), needs the OpenCL headers and an ICD loader that the NDK does not ship, and the Vulkan backend on Adreno is slower than the CPU on most phones and has unstable drivers. Not wired: a Speed switch that cannot be tested here and that most likely makes it slower is worse than none. If someone wants it: a Q4_0 quantisation of the model, `-DGGML_OPENCL=ON` with the headers fetched, `n_gpu_layers` set, behind an off-by-default switch.
- **KV cache, flash attention, mmap:** the cache stays q8_0 with flash attention (a quantised V needs it; half the memory of f16: 0.53 GB for 6,144 tokens); q4_0 would save 0.25 GB at a quality cost for a model whose card warns about quantised-cache leaks. The weights stay memory-mapped (the OS can drop them; `mlock` would pin 7.5 GB). The chunk size (128 tokens) stays: reading is compute-bound far above 32 tokens a chunk and a cancel lands between chunks and inside one; a larger chunk gains little on a CPU.
- **JOI's prompt** changed line to line (the stage hint and the few-shot lines moved with the stage and the lines said), so each JOI line re-read its whole prompt (about 700 to 900 tokens). Done in the next section, "Under 5 seconds": the compact prompt's front is the same through every stage and the stage's words are in the last message.

**Tested here.** The JVM suite (1,645 tests, all passing after merging the character profiles and eras): the timing ring and its summary (`ReplyTimingsTest`), the CPU parsing, the kernels table, the thread plan (`CpuProfileTest`), the prompt layout, prefix stability and the caps (`PromptCacheTest`), the scheduler's timings, warm-up order, budget, preemption, early stops (`SchedulerSpeedTest`), the chat's retry cap and the director's warm-up and drops (`ChatSpeedTest`). The C++ core's new parts on the desktop against Qwen2.5-0.5B Q4_K_M, 1 thread (4 threads were 20 times slower: other sessions' builds were using every core and ggml's threads spin; **the speeds below are not a benchmark**): `engine-test/dialogue_test.cpp` all passed, including a prompt read with maxTokens 0 (144 tokens, nothing sampled; the next request then reused 142 of its 154 tokens and read 12), the first token's time (never below the prefill's), the place's size, the thread counts changing between generations, a state file read back (154 tokens restored) and kept, and the system line naming the kernels. The same run: 15 to 18 tokens a second writing, 555 tokens a second reading a prompt, 400 ms to load. **Not tested:** the Android build of the new CMake configuration, the Compose pages (Speed, chat wait), anything on a phone, the real speed of i8mm.
## Under 5 seconds: the Fast model, compact prompts, a warm-up, a pipeline

What changed for "a JOI line or a chat reply in under 5 seconds" (23 November 2026). **Measured here** means measured on this desktop (4 vCPU x86-64, llama.cpp at the app's tag, the real GGUF, 4 threads, q8 KV cache), which is **not a phone**: its writing speed (9 to 10 tokens a second with the 1.7 GB model, about 17 GB/s of memory traffic) is probably below a recent Snapdragon's. **Estimated** means arithmetic with the stated speeds. Nothing here ran on a phone.

**Prompt sizes (measured with the models' own tokenizers over the prompts the code builds: `PromptDump2Test` writes them, `engine-test/dialogue_tok` counts them).** "Prefix" is what is stable and read once per NPC (and level, for JOI); "new" is what every line or turn adds. Before: the full prompts (Qwen3 tokenizer over the ChatML text; Mistral Nemo's tokenizer gives 10 to 15 % more). After: the compact prompts (Gemma tokenizer, without the start-of-text mark).

| Cold prompt | Before (full) | After (compact) | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| JOI: stable prefix (persona, register, examples) | 693 to 732, and it **moved with the stage** | **330 (Hot), 330 (Nasty), 350 (WTF)**, byte for byte the same through every stage and line | at most 350 |
| JOI: new tokens per line (stage hint, chips, what she said, her hand, the task) | 172 to 180 | **72 to 75** (87 to 92 in the permit, which carries a line of its voice; chips add up to 16) | at most 80 |
| JOI: the whole cold prompt | 865 to 912 | 402 to 442 | |
| JOI: few-shot examples | 8 pairs, from the stage's family | 3 short pairs (the shortest whole order of the opening, build and edge families of the level), the same all JOI long; the stage's own voice for permit, afterglow and the count goes in the last message | 2 to 3 |
| Chat: persona | 441 | **300** | |
| Chat: persona + context (place, who stands before her, what she knows) | 622 | **413** | |
| Chat: first turn's new tokens | 196 | **75** (the later turns 40 to 60) | at most 80 |
| Chat: the whole first prompt | 818 | **488** | |
| A named NPC's persona (the character core and era slice) | up to 280 + 150 | held to 110 + 45 (`PersonaBudget.CORE_COMPACT`, `ERA_COMPACT`) | |

How it was cut (`Persona.kt`, `ChatPrompt.kt`, `JoiPrompt.kt`, `DirtyTalk.kt`, `PersonaKit.kt`; the Quality model keeps the full text): the world's rules in 60 tokens instead of 120 (`RULES_COMPACT`); the people's and calling's paragraphs cut to 100 and 90 characters, no side paragraph; the bio to 140 characters; one lore snippet of 130 characters in the chat context; the opening stance and the wariness lines in one sentence each (the mild "wary" line is left out: the stance already says it); a JOI register without the long "no poetry" paragraph; the dirty-talk examples cut to 3 in JOI and 2 in chat; the kink notes to 1 snippet. `PromptBudgetTest` holds every level and stage to the budgets (in `Tokens.approx` units, 5 to 8 % above the real count), that the compact JOI prefix is identical through every stage, chip and line, that it is under 62 % of the full one, and a named NPC's compact persona under budget in every era.

**Token caps.** A JOI line is at most **40 tokens** (28 for the line before the count) and ends at its third sentence (`DialogueRequest.sentences`); a chat reply at **30 (Short), 60 (Normal), 100 (Long)** with a sentence stop at 1, 2 and 3 sentences. The countdown's numbers are the app's, not the model's.

**Warm-up before the player waits.** `DialogueEngine.warm(npcId, system, urgent, history)` now takes the turns that follow the system prompt too (JOI's examples), so a JOI warm-up reads exactly the prefix its lines send. When an NPC is **selected** her JOI prefix is read in the background at the level Options sets (`SceneSessionScreen`, behind everything); when **JOI** is tapped it is asked again as urgent and the first line is asked for **at once** (the queue reads the prefix first, then the line, which reads only its last message); a **level change** warms the new level (another place in the runtime: one per NPC and level, `joi:<id>:<level>`; the chat's is `chat:<id>`). While the prefix is still being read and no word is on screen, JOI shows **"Getting ready..."** with a bar and chat says "Getting ready... 45%" (`DialogueEngine.warmProgress`: an estimate from the tokens to read and the prompt-reading speed the phone showed lately, held under 95 % until the read ends: `WarmEstimate`). It never blocks the first line. The warmed places are **kept on disk** per model and NPC key (the runtime already wrote them when the app leaves the foreground and when a place is evicted, and reads one back instead of re-reading the prompt: 330 tokens are about 19 MB at q8 for this model), so the next session's warm-up is a file read.

**JOI pipeline** (`simulation/JoiMode.kt`, `simulation/JoiDriver.kt`, `ui/JoiUi.kt`): `JoiMode.lead = AHEAD` asks for the next line as soon as the one before it has come, i.e. **while the current subtitle is on screen** (one line on show, one written behind it), so from the second line on the screen never waits; the old rule (ask in the last 1.6 s) stays for the pure state machine's tests. The words go to the screen **as they are written**: `JoiPrompt.line(..., onPartial)` cleans the answer so far (whole words only, no half-written word, no action, no fourth-wall word) and `JoiMode.offerPartial` shows it as the subtitle until the finished line replaces it; a line already on show is never covered by the next line's words; a skipped stage or a typed steer drops late words; a refused first try is taken off the screen before the retry. The request carries a stream guard (a fourth-wall word ends the answer at once, not after 40 tokens).

**Streaming in chat.** `ChatSession.partial` was handed each *piece* of the answer, not the answer so far, so the bubble showed only the last word; it is now the reply so far, word by word (`StreamGuard.settled`, then `ChatClean.preview`), and an abandoned try's words never reach it. Tested end to end in `FastRuntimeTest` (engine pieces -> session -> the bubble's text).

**The Gemma template** (`ChatTemplate.GEMMA`, tested in `DialogueTemplateTest`): `<start_of_turn>system|user|model\n...<end_of_turn>\n`; the start-of-text mark is added by the runtime from the model's own vocabulary rule (as for Nemo and Qwen), not written in the text; the **answer is opened with a double quote and ends at the next one** (earlier answers in the prompt are quoted the same way, so the prompt still grows by appending): a role-play tune narrates ("I reply calmly: "Good evening."") and the NPCs speak only words; with the quote open the desktop run gave only speech in 12 of 12 lines. A request whose answer is JSON (the consent decision) says `DialogueRequest.spoken = false` and gets no quote (a JSON answer would end at its first quotation mark); `FastRuntimeTest` checks both prompts and stops.

**Throughput knobs.** Threads: Auto uses the performance cores (writing up to 4, reading up to 6); **the first time the Fast model loads** a 5-second calibration writes 32 tokens with 3, 4, 5 and 6 threads (`ThreadCalibration`, run by `DialogueModel.calibrate`, kept per model in `DialogueConfig.calibratedThreads`; a higher count must be 4 % faster to beat a lower one; skipped for the 12B model and for a hand-picked count) and Auto then uses the winner. The kernel variant is chosen by ggml from the CPU (`CpuVariants`: i8mm builds on every Snapdragon 8 Gen 1 and later); flash attention with a q8 cache, a 128-token batch and mmap are unchanged. Not measured on a phone: whether 4 or 5 threads wins there.

**Speed test, timings, the report.** Options -> Dialogue -> Speed: the **Speed test** writes 64 tokens and now says "Estimated JOI line time: X s (within/over the 5 s target); first word after about Y s. Getting an NPC ready the first time: about Z s" (`SpeedTarget`: 80 new tokens read, 34 written, 0.25 s of overhead; the arithmetic is below). **Copy timings** adds each request's four phases (queue wait, prefill, first token, decode: `ReplyTiming.phases`); **Report slow replies** copies the model id, the CPU's features and cores, the thread plan, and the replies slower than 5 s with their phases, their prompt token counts (read, hit, borrowed, restored) and a one-line cause ("38.0 s of 44.3 s went to reading the prompt (800 tokens read at 21 tok/s...)"): numbers and NPC ids only, nothing that was said.

**The arithmetic (estimated for a phone: read 100 tokens a second, write 15; the ranges are 80 to 150 and 10 to 25).**

| | Seconds |
| --- | --- |
| Warm JOI line: first word | 0.25 + 78 / 100 + 1 / 15 = **1.1** |
| Warm JOI line: the whole line (34 tokens) | 0.25 + 0.8 + 34 / 15 = **3.3** (4.5 at 10 tokens a second; 2.3 at 25) |
| Getting an NPC ready (333-token prefix), once per NPC and level | 333 / 100 = **3.3** (a file read on the next session) |
| First JOI line if the NPC was selected a few seconds before | the prefix is read; **3.3** |
| First JOI line, nothing warmed (tapped at once) | 3.3 (getting ready) + 3.3 = **6.6**, with words from about 4.4 s and the bar before |
| Later JOI lines | no wait: written while the one before is on screen (2.2 to 9 s a subtitle) |
| Chat reply, persona warm from the setup sheet, context new | 0.25 + (75 + 113) / 100 + 30 / 15 = **4.1** for Short; first word at 2.2 |
| The 12B model, warm JOI line (180 new tokens read at 25, 30 written at 5) | 0.25 + 7.2 + 6 = **13**; cold 865 tokens: 35 s + 6 s, twice when refused: the 90 s the player saw |

**Measured here** (Gemmasutra-Mini-2B Q4_K_M, 4 threads): cold JOI (Hot, build) 408 tokens read in 3.2 to 3.4 s (121 to 126 tokens a second), first piece at 3.25 s, writing 9.3 to 10.1 tokens a second, 40 tokens in 7.2 to 7.5 s in all. Warm-up of the 333-token prefix 2.6 to 3.3 s; then the line reads 77 to 78 tokens in 0.9 to 1.0 s (78 to 88 a second), **first piece at 0.9 to 1.0 s**, lines of 11 to 40 tokens done in **2.1 to 5.4 s** (Hot, Nasty and the permit stage; 3.5 s typical). Chat: cold 489 tokens read in 4.4 to 4.5 s; warm persona, a reply of 2 to 6 tokens in 1.2 to 1.5 s. On a phone the reading speed could be higher (i8mm) or lower (a smaller batch reads slower: 80 a second here against 125 cold) and the writing speed higher.

**Is 5 seconds realistic?** With the Fast model, **yes for a warm NPC, on a recent Snapdragon**: a JOI line or a chat reply is done in about 3 to 4.5 s with the first words at about 1 to 2 s, and from the second JOI line on the player never waits. **Conditions:** the NPC's prefix is read first (3 to 4 s of "Getting ready...", once per NPC and level and then kept on disk; hidden if the NPC was selected a few seconds earlier); a phone that writes at least 10 tokens a second and reads at least 80; lines of about 30 tokens (a long Normal or Long chat reply, 60 to 100 tokens, takes 4 to 7 s: the first words are on screen by then); no other heavy work (the renderer asks the model to yield). **Not realistic:** the Quality (slow) model, which stays at 15 to 90 s on most phones. Quality given up: see "The two models". Not tested: any of it on a phone, the Compose screens (CI compiles them), the first-run calibration on a phone, battery and heat with the Fast model running during JOI.

**Tests** (JVM, `PromptBudgetTest`, `JoiPipelineTest`, `FastRuntimeTest`, `DialogueTemplateModelsTest`): the catalogue (two models, sizes, hashes, templates), which model is active (nothing downloaded, only Mag-Mell, both, a pick and a deleted file), the settings (round trip, clamps, migration of old ids, calibration per model), the Gemma template (turns, the quote, append-only growth, control marks, the warm-up's front), prompt budgets and the identical prefix, the token caps, the warm-up's order against the first request (with an engine that logs both), its progress and its estimate, the pipeline's lookahead, streaming into the mode and the chat bubble, "Getting ready", the calibration's choice and ties, the speed target's arithmetic, the phases and the slow report. Run with the command above (`--init-script scripts/maven-mirror.init.gradle`).

## Character profiles

Every named NPC is played by an expert on them. `assets/dialogue/profiles/<character-id>.json` is one character (the file name is the id of `assets/characters.json`; adding a named NPC later is one new data file, nothing else): fourteen today (Sylvanas Windrunner, Calia Menethil, Lilian Voss, Jaina Proudmoore, Alexstrasza, Chromie, Valeera Sanguinar, Alleria Windrunner, Sally Whitemane, Xal'atath, Tess Greymane, Lyssabel Dawnpetal, Sindragosa, and the demo's original night elf huntress Nyrelle Moonquill, whose profile is authored from `PLAN.md` and marked `"canon": false`). A made-up NPC has no profile and gets the race, class and side knowledge of the era instead (below).

- **Schema.** `id`, `name`, `canon`; `core` {`who`, `temper`, `speech` (register, vocabulary, verbal habits), `look` (self-reference), `values`, `intimate` (how they are in romantic and intimate moments, consistent with their canon temper; the Dirty talk level still sets the register of the words)}; `variants` [{`from`, `until` (era ids), and any of the core keys}] (the base overlaid by the last variant covering the era: Alleria is a high elf ranger until Draenor and the first void elf from Legion; Whitemane a Scarlet High Inquisitor until Warlords and a death knight after); `voice` (6 to 10 original lines in their voice, a string or {`t`, `from`, `until`}); `backstory` and `topics` [{`keys`, `text`, `from`, `until`}] (retrieved only when the talk mentions a key, and only once it has happened by the era); `likes`, `fears`, `secrets` (retrieved on the matching words); `relationships` {`<other id or lowercase token>`: {`name`, `address` (how they address them), `default`, `eras` {era id: a line, the latest at or before the era is used}, `from`}}; `eraStates` {era id: {`s` active, `out_of_era` or `dead`, `t`, `u` (not verified)}} (a state for every era: where they are, what they do, their attitude; for a character who does not exist yet or is dead, `t` is the in-era framing the user's placement gets: play them as the grown adult they become, or as an echo; a young-in-canon character is always played as a full-grown adult); `sources` (URLs), `unverified` (what could not be checked).
- **Research.** Written in original wording from the Warcraft wiki (warcraft.wiki.gg) pages of each character, cross-checked for the recent eras against Blizzard's Midnight pages, a Midnight press report (Sylvanas), Wowhead's Jaina guide and the Chronormu page; the `sources` of each file name the pages used. Each wiki page is a single source, several are truncated in the middle of Mists and Legion, and Wowhead's and Blizzard's pages were only seen as search results: **every profile is one source plus spot checks, not several independent ones.** What the pages did not give is in `unverified` and flagged `u` in the state (Sylvanas, Jaina, Alexstrasza, Valeera, Tess and Whitemane in the later eras; Lilian's age and the date of her death; the years between Vanilla and Legion for Calia; Lyssabel's whole backstory (only her duties and greetings are known); Xal'atath's romantic stance and Sindragosa's humanoid form, which are authored from temper). Nothing here has been played on a phone.
- **What the prompt gets** (`dialogue/PersonaKit.kt`; only compact retrieved pieces, by `Tokens.approx` = the larger of 1.35 tokens a word and a quarter of the characters). In the stable **system prompt**, after the base persona: the **character core** (`Who you are`, `Nature`, `How you speak`, `What you hold dear`, `Your look`, `In intimacy`, three sample lines of the voice sheet picked by era, and a line to stay in the voice), at most **280 tokens**: if it is over, sample lines go first, then the values, the look and last the intimacy; and the **era slice** (below), at most **150**. Per turn, in the user message and only when there is something to say: **relationship snippets** for the named NPCs present in the scene (Tess and Sally in one scene each get what they are to the other in that era), at most 2 of at most 60 tokens, and for a person the talk mentions by name; and **topic sections** the talk mentions (the NPC's last lines, what they just heard, the seed), at most 2 of at most 70 tokens. The base persona is about 430 tokens, so a whole system prompt of a named NPC is **at most 900 tokens** (measured 862 at most, over every profile and era), and a turn adds at most 260. A made-up NPC gets the era frame and a note of at most 90 tokens. `PersonaKitTest` holds every profile in every era to these numbers.
- **Checking and reviewing.** `python3 -I tools/profile_check.py` validates the schema, the budgets, era ids, that a relationship naming another profile uses its id, that no modern or out-of-world word and no term of a later era is in a state, a topic or a relationship line of an earlier one, and that every named character has a profile; `python3 -I tools/persona_probe.py <character> <era> [--present id,id] [--say "text"]` prints the pieces the prompt gets (a Python mirror of `PersonaKit` for review; the Kotlin tests are authoritative; `--list` lists characters and eras).

## Eras

`assets/dialogue/eras/<era-id>.json` (and `index.json`: the order, the default, and `placeEras`) set the age NPCs live in. Twelve eras, in order: **Vanilla (Classic)** (2004), **The Burning Crusade** (2007), **Wrath of the Lich King** (2008, the default), **Cataclysm** (2010), **Mists of Pandaria** (2012), **Warlords of Draenor** (2014), **Legion** (2016), **Battle for Azeroth** (2018), **Shadowlands** (2020), **Dragonflight** (2022), **The War Within** (2024) and **Midnight** (2026), and **Custom year**: a release year from 2004 to 2026 mapped to the era it falls in (a year before the first is Vanilla, after the last Midnight). The in-world years are not given (the wiki's timeline pages could not be read in full); the dates are the release years.

- **Schema.** `id`, `label`, `order`, `release`, `until`, `years`; `present` (the one sentence that replaces the base persona's line about the age); `world` (who rules, relations, wars, threats, cities and their state); `idiom` (oaths, coin, faith, slang); `folk` {alliance, horde, neutral: what a made-up NPC of that side knows}, `classes` and `races` (what a made-up NPC of a calling or race that does not exist yet, or means something else, is told: a death knight before Wrath, a monk before Mists, no pandaren, worgen or void elves yet); `stances` (the era's attitude of one race or faction to another, replacing `lore.json`'s in Chat and in scene context; keys as `lore.json`'s: `undead>alliance`); `places` (how Dalaran, Stormwind, Brill and Dolanaar stand in the era, added to the lore pack's place line); `introduces` (the names and terms that first appear in the era).
- **The cutoff.** The prompt says in positive terms what the world is and that nothing later is known; it never lists what it forbids. The filter does: the era's blocklist is every term `introduces`d by a later era, and `Anachronisms.find` (the line check the director and Chat already use: a line with one is regenerated once, then dropped) rejects it. A named NPC's per-era state is in their profile (`eraStates`): out of era and dead are framed (Alleria in Vanilla is the living ranger of the Second War; Tess, young in some eras, is played only as the grown adult; Sindragosa before Wrath is her spirit or her living self; Xal'atath before Legion is a voice in an old dagger; Lyssabel before The War Within is a night elf community-keeper).
- **The slice** (at most 150 tokens): `The age: <label>, <years>.`, the world, the idiom, `Nothing later than this age is known to you.`, and `Your life now: <their state>` (or, out of era, `You are placed here out of your own time, so live it as you would be then: ...`); the idiom, then the years, drop first if it is over.

## Kink and JOI knowledge

**Nothing was trained.** The phone's model (the 12 billion parameter Mag-Mell from Hugging Face) is not fine-tuned, and there is no GPU here to do it. - Not run on a phone; how well the model uses the snippets is untested.

## JOI mode

A **JOI** order in the selected NPC's action bar (beside Play animation and Partner animation; off while Build mode is on). The NPC gives guided-self-touch instructions to someone in the room: she (he) believes she is talking an NPC through it, "you", and never knows of a player, a game, a screen or a camera (the prompt names none, and a reply that does is refused and asked again). The viewer only watches: no avatar, no choices beyond the controls. Everything she says comes from the dialogue model (`dialogue/JoiPrompt.kt`: nothing is hard-coded); with no model the mode says **JOI needs the dialogue model**, offers **Download it** (when the screen is given the download) and **Try again**, and waits. For adults (18+) only, like the knowledge pack it builds on: consent, a way to stop and aftercare are in the prompts' stages (`setup` asks and names the word, `afterglow` is aftercare).

- **Stages and pacing** (`simulation/JoiMode.kt`, pure): the order is the pack's `joiPlan(band, seed)` (setup, warm-up, build, edge, hold, build..., countdown, permit, afterglow), the count and its beat are `countdown(band, seed)`. Each stage lasts 12 to 60 s (livelier bands longer) and fills with lines: the next is asked for 1.6 s before the shown one ends; a line stays for its reading time (0.9 s + 0.4 s a word, longer on slow stages, 2.2 to 9 s) and then a quiet by the stage's pace. The countdown stage first asks for one short line, then shows each number large, one a beat (1.5 to 3 s), from the start down to 1; the numbers are the only text not from the model. **Intensity** is the **Dirty talk level** slider (see below), which subsumes the old Intimate / Uninhibited choice: the plan's band follows the level (Hot and below Intimate: one or two edges, a count from 5; Nasty and up Uninhibited: more edges, a count from 10, longer stages), chosen when the JOI starts; moving the slider mid-JOI changes the words from the next line and never re-plans. Controls: **Pause**/**Resume**, **Skip stage** (a late answer for the skipped stage is dropped), **Stop**, steer chips (**Tease more**, **Softer**, **Firmer**, **Slower**, **Describe**, **Praise**) and the **custom steer** (below) that go into the next prompts. Three missing answers in a row stop the mode and show the no-model note.
- **Subtitles** replace speech bubbles while the mode runs: large text in the lower third of the 4:3 film frame (`ui/JoiUi.kt`), kept when the interface hides after 5 s untouched (cinematic idle: only the controls hide). No timing of a voice: a line's time is its reading time.
- **Framing** (switch live; `simulation/JoiCamera.kt`, pure): **Face** (head and shoulders), **Torso** (head to waist), **Legs up** (a camera at knee height looking up: thighs to head), and **Auto**, which mixes them (never the same framing twice running). Slow drift inside each shot (a few degrees round, a few percent in), a cut every 3.5 to 8 s to another angle at least 17 degrees from the last (and, in Auto, another framing), always in front of her inside a 50 degree cone of her facing; a wall in the way (the scene's clearance test) draws it in or moves it to a clear angle; the field of view widens (to +16 degrees) rather than cut off the crown. She is turned to face the camera when the mode starts and held there (`LiveScene.joiPlace`); the other NPCs keep their day (their bubbles are hidden). Entering the mode turns cinematic mode on (zone light, the film frame, depth of field as set); leaving it (Stop, Back, the Cinematic button, the end of the plan) puts back the cinematic state, camera mode and furniture view that were on.
- **Solo animations** (`simulation/JoiAnimations.kt`): the single-person clips of the library and the Warcraft Dance and Kiss emotes are classified by their names (dance, tease, flirt, kiss, gesture, pose, hair, look over the shoulder; sits, fights, walks and partner stages are left out), one is due every 8 to 28 s (sooner on livelier stages) and is picked at random by how near its energy is to the stage's (warm-up gentle: 0.3; edge 0.75; countdown 0.85), never the one just played nor any within 120 s, never two of a kind in a row; afterwards she is put back to idle where she stands. ### Stockings: fabric interaction

`engine/stockfabric.cpp` (pure maths; `engine-test/stockings_test.cpp`): a position-based layer over the welded shell. Each vertex is bound to the skinned leg by a damped spring; the edges are distance constraints; a pinch (`Native.stockGrab`: the nearest live vertex to the ray) is dragged in the view plane (`stockDrag`) with a falloff, which stretches the edges between the dragged and the held part. **Tension** is the worst edge's strain over the tearing strain (35%): 0 to 1; the weave's alpha thins with it. At full tension while pulled the stretched edge breaks and a **run** spreads along the leg's length (a stochastic path of broken edges, a few centimetres, seeded: the same seed makes the same run; longer when pulled again); the triangles on a broken edge leave the index list (skin shows), their vertices are the rim. On release it springs back, damped. `stockUndo` takes back the latest run, `stockRepair` all. Tested on a synthetic body: constraints bounded, no NaN, tension rising with the pull, spring-back within 1.5 mm, tear thresholds, deterministic runs, undo and repair. **In the app (Interact mode, Touch tool):**

- **The pinch.** Put a finger on a part her stockings cover (hip, butt, thigh, knee, calf, foot, belly) and hold it still for a fifth of a second (a quick swipe stays a stroke along her): the engine checks that there is stocking under the finger (`Native.stockProbe`: the live vertex nearest the camera within 3 cm of the ray, so the near side and not the leg's far one; a tear's edge is a live vertex too), the consent gate answers (below), and the fabric is grabbed (`stockGrab`). Dragging pulls it in the view plane (`stockDrag`); lifting lets go and it springs back (`stockRelease`). A second finger (the pinch to zoom) lets go. A **thin arc round the finger** fills as the tension rises (cream, amber from 45%, red from 80%); the sheet's Stockings tab shows the same as a bar, the status (**Fabric intact / Stretching / Torn**) and the line ("Stretching 62%", "Torn: 2 runs"). Keep pulling until the gauge is full and the stocking gives: a run. **Restore** takes back the latest tear (one tap), **Repair all** every one. The state is polled from the solver every 45 ms while pulling (250 ms otherwise).
- **Consent.** A pull is a touch act (`TouchKind.PULL`, the same category as the part: Hands for the hip, butt and thigh, Foot fetish for the foot, Friendly for the rest, the pull a little more tender than a tap). A Won't from Consent and limits declines in character and the grab never starts; a Will allows; otherwise her willingness decides: a hesitation puts up the persuasion chips (Reassure, Flatter, Be patient...) and, once she is calmed, she lets the stockings be pulled for the rest of the session. Each pull gives trust like another touch (+0.02, a refusal -0.05).
- **Her reaction** (`PinchReactions`) by the tension and her nature: at 45% a giggle (the bold and curious), a blush (the cautious) or a flinch, with a line at times; at 80% she steps back or flinches and asks you to stop; at the tear it is stronger: a step back or flinch, a look down if she is the shy kind, and always a line. The lines are templates by style and dirty talk level (Cute and Mild stay sweet, from Spicy a tear may be said plainly); with a dialogue model loaded the line comes from it (one short sentence, asked with `PinchModelLine`, the template used if it answers nothing or names something beyond the world).
- **Haptics** (the More switch and the panel's Haptics, default on): a tick for each tenth the tension rises through, stronger as it tightens; a long buzz (hard hit, gap, rolling buzz) at the tear; a soft snap on the springback.
- **Fabric sound** (panel switch, default **off**): synthesised on the phone, no files (`audio/StockSoundCore.kt`, played by `StockSound` on a background `AudioTrack` thread, low latency, small buffer; stopped when the mode ends, the switch goes off or the app pauses): friction is band-limited noise whose pitch, narrowness and loudness follow the tension and how fast it changes (nothing on slack fabric held still, a quick pull loud and high, the springback a soft hiss), and a 0.4 s rip (a sharp attack, a ragged tail of ladder cracks; deterministic) when a run starts.
- **Material** (Stockings tab): yarn thickness 8 to 80 D (applied when the slider is let go: it makes the stocking again; a heavier denier tears later, 8 D at about 26% strain and 80 D at 58%, and is less glossy), elasticity 0 to 100% (the solver's `elasticity`: how far the edges give before the yarn does), **Side light** (a warm raking light from the camera's right on the stocking that shows the weave and its stretch; off by default), **Fabric sound**, **Haptics**; kept in the app's settings (`StockOptions`). The Fresnel sheen (`uSheen`: a rim and a highlight on the stocking, glossier the sheerer) is part of the stocking now, scaled by the denier.
- **Tests (JVM):** `StockPinchTest`: the gesture machine (hold, probe, ask, grab, drag throttled, release, a refusal never grabs, a lift in flight, no stocking), the consent gate on a pull (a Won't declines, a Will allows, a hesitation puts up the persuasion and calming her allows the next), the status and the gauge, the options (defaults and their JSON), how denier and elasticity set the solver's numbers, the tracker's events (a tick per tenth, each stage once, the tear once, the springback), the haptic patterns, her reactions by stage and nature and the lines by level; `StockSoundTest`: the friction's parameters by tension and slope, the rip's envelope and its deterministic rendering. **Not run:** the Compose pieces (the touch layer's hold and arc, the Stockings panel: CI compiles them), the sound and the haptics on a phone, the model's lines.

## Flesh contact

The skin gives where a hand, a mouth or another body presses, bulges a little round the dent, springs back, and shows the pressure. Options' Graphics has **Flesh contact** (Off, Low: 4 contacts at a time, High: 8, the default) and **Softness** (0 to 200%, 100% default; scales how deep a dent goes). The governor (`graphics/Governor`) takes Flesh contact to Low after MSAA and off at its very last step.

- **Where it works.** The same vertex shader draws the game's model, the HD (Liard) figure, the stockings and the parts, so one contact block (`uCtN`, `uCtA/B/C`, `uCtLayer`, `uCtShade` in `kM2Vs`) dents all of them: the skin in full, a garment or stocking at 85% (`kGarmentLayer`, so the skin never pokes through), hair none. The vertices move along the contact's outward normal by `depth * (1-q^2)^2` (q = distance over radius), a ring from 0.7 to 1.6 radii rises by 10 to 40% of the dent's volume (softer regions give more of it back; `contactBulgeGain` is calibrated so the numbers add up, and the test integrates it), and the skin round the dent draws toward it along the surface. A vertex facing away from the contact (the far side of a thin part) does not move. The pressure shading is done per fragment, from the same block: the normal leans by the dent's slope (so a body of centimetre triangles shows a smooth dent), the middle goes pale, a flush ring surrounds it and outlives the touch (about 1.4 s), and the pressing body shades what it covers. The sculpt (a CPU refit of the HD body) and the soft body's displacement happen first; the dent is added in world space after them; the contact shadows and AO work on the depth that results; Build mode's sharp image is unaffected. API for other layers: `ContactDraw::layer(program, scale, shade)` before a draw (`engine/contactdraw.h`).
- **Dynamics** (`engine/contact.h`, pure, `engine-test/contact_test.cpp`). Each contact is a spring on its depth: a press settles in about 120 ms (critically damped), a release springs back in 200 to 400 ms with a slight rebound (under 7%), softer regions slower and deeper. Regions (`ContactRegion`): breast, butt, thigh, belly, groin (soft, 0.7 to 1.0 of the dent), arm and hip (middling), ribs, shins, skull (stiff, 0.05 to 0.15), each with a deepest dent (3 mm skull to 40 mm breast). At most 8 are drawn (4 at Low or under load); the rest fade out and in (no pop), contacts on top of one another merge. 20 and 60 frames a second give the same settle and release (tested); hostile input (NaN, infinity, a clock gone backwards) stays finite.
- **Where the contacts come from.** (a) *Stages, with no data of their own:* every frame, for each pair of people within 1.6 world units, the tools of one (palm, fingers and thumb of each hand, mouth, pelvis, thighs, knees, belly, breasts) against the regions of the other (capsules built from the posed bones and the measured flesh: breasts, butt, hips, groin, belly, ribs, thighs, shins, arms, head); a tool within 12 mm of a region's surface, or inside it, makes a contact whose depth is how far it has come in. (b) *Interact's touch:* `SceneView.pushTouch` (a touch she allows only; a refusal dents nothing) picks the skin under the finger (`pickBody`, with the hit triangle's normal) and presses it along that normal; a tap holds 250 ms, a stroke follows the finger and lets go when it lifts. (c) *Insertion:* the shaft's depth (`bodypart.h` insertionDepth) at the partner's entry anchor dents the skin there (6 mm at the entry, up to 30 mm of asked depth with the shaft's depth, a ring round it), and as it withdraws the spring takes it back; the skin round it gives through the soft region's own softness.
- **The groin as a region of Interact mode.** `TouchRegion.GROIN` (from in front, on the hips or top of the legs, between 0.555 and 0.607 of her height over the crown and within 0.04 of her middle line: measured on the default body and the HD figure), its own consent category **Intimate touch** (in Consent and limits, between Hands and Oral), the most tender part (0.95): a Won't on Intimate touch declines any touch there (tap, stroke or pull) and stops a stroke that reaches it; a Will lets it through; with nothing set it is decided by her willingness and her nature like any touch (a shy stranger hesitates or refuses, in character; a bold one who knows you may allow). From Spicy up an allowed touch gets a line of its own in her style (shy, bold, tender, dominant, natural); Cute and Mild keep the sweet ones; a refusal is the same at every level.
- **What the models have there** (the anatomy audit: `Human_F` and `NightElf_F` HD sets, the default body, the men). The HD women have a modelled closed vulva (a slit with small inner folds, on a dense radial mesh of about 0.5 mm edges, all weighted to the pelvis; no separate vertex group); the game's default female body is a low-poly smooth mons with a texture-painted cleft only; the HD man has a modelled shaft. There is no separate genital mesh to deform, so the groin dent is the same displacement over the pelvis skin. A procedural detail layer for the game's body has not been made.
- **Limits.** A dent is displacement, not a soft-body simulation: two bodies pressing hard into each other are each given their own dent, they do not push one another, and nothing pushes back on the pressing hand. The game's own body is a coarse mesh, so its dents use twice the radius (a shallower, broader dent). Hands are not posed to touch: contacts follow the clip's own hands. Two tools of one body on one region of another merge. The cost: the vertex stage does at most 8 contacts of a dozen arithmetic operations for the vertices a contact reaches (`q >= 1.6` leaves at once), fragments only for the pixels a contact covers; measured on this machine's CPU-emulated GL (llvmpipe, 800 x 800, one HD figure, 60 frames each, noisy: p50 17.5 ms with it off, 20.3 ms with 4 contacts, 22.3 ms with 8, 15.5 to 17.5 ms with it on and none pressing): at most about 4 to 5 ms of software shading for 8 live contacts. By arithmetic the vertex work is 35 to 60 thousand vertices x 8 x about 45 operations (a few MFLOP a frame) and the fragment loop runs only over the pixels a contact covers, so a phone's GPU should take it in well under a millisecond, but that is not measured; the contact count adapts anyway (8, then 4, then off by the governor). Nothing here has run on a phone.
- **Tests.** `engine-test/contact_test.cpp` (falloff, ring, the bulge's volume, a dent's volume against pi R^2 d / 3, spring timing at 60 and 20 fps, rebound, softness and stiffness, the cap and merge, no pop, hostile input, shading, derivation from proxies, the pick's regions); JVM `GraphicsSettingsTest` (options persist, clamp, the array, the governor's steps), `InteractModeTest` (the groin's bands, the category, the Won't, the stroke, the lines). There is no override: Build mode and Move mode previews are allowed and flagged only. The animation browser marks a refused act with a **Won't: <name>** pill and a note; starting it anyway, the NPC declines in character: with the dialogue model, a line in their own voice (`DialogueHook.declined`), else a speech bubble that says "declines" and the notice "<name> declines that."; the stage does not start.
- **Remembered** in the settings as JSON (`simulation/ConsentBook.kt`): per character (the default), or for this place and this cast alone with the **Remember for this scene only** switch (then the scene's own limits are used in that scene). Every change is kept as it is made.
- **Not covered yet:** JOI mode's and Chat mode's own solo/steer-driven actions (they are single-person animations and dialogue, not two-person acts; their prompts and Filters are another piece of work), and the three-or-more-person stages (the importer leaves them out).

Tested on the desktop (`unit-tests/.../simulation/ConsentPolicyTest`, `ConsentBookTest`, `ConsentEnforcementTest`): resolution order and the strictest-category rule, the categories read from names and pack kinds (including unknown packs and a fallback to Other), the defaults by nature (shy asks more than bold, never a Won't), orientation and roles, Will skipping the question and Ask keeping it, the catalogue's grouping and search, set-category, set-act, copy to all, copy from, reset, the Limits line, the JSON round trip per character and per scene and a broken file; in the simulation: an act either won't do is never picked (either NPC, any seed), the player's order refused with the bubble, a role limit, a queued stage held to it, the dialogue layer's own refusal line used when it has one, an act that becomes a Won't while it plays ends, Switch positions refused, and auto-play never choosing a clip either won't do. **Not run:** the Compose screens (the step, the window, the pills; compiled by CI only), anything on a phone, the real dialogue model's refusal lines.

## Building

Run `cd android && bash gradlew :app:assembleDebug` with Android SDK 35, NDK 29.0.14206865, and CMake installed. Run the platform-independent tests with `bash android/gradlew -p unit-tests test` from this directory.

## Smoothness

Menus, windows and buttons share one motion kit (`ui/MenuMotion.kt`, `ui/MenuMotionUi.kt`): windows fade and grow in over 160 ms and out over 120 ms, dropdowns take 90 ms, a press shrinks to 0.96 over 100 ms, and all of it is off when the phone's "remove animations" setting is on. Every tap target ignores a second tap within 300 ms. Controls take their size from the layout, never from a picture or font that has not arrived yet. Download progress reaches the screen at most four times a second and only to the parts that show it. Pictures decode on two background threads into a size-limited cache, newest request first, and a request is cancelled when its cell leaves the screen. The live scene runs on a fixed 1/120 s step (same speed at 60 and 120 Hz, no jump after a pause), draws people interpolated between steps, and writes the camera and positions straight to the renderer; the menus see a snapshot at most ten times a second. The camera uses critically damped springs with inertia and soft limits, eases between the room and NPC cameras over 350 ms, and an 8 dp touch slop separates a tap from a drag. A loading cover hides the scene until its first frames are drawn. Skins are composed at most one per frame. Desktop measurements only (Mesa llvmpipe): the first frame after dressing six NPCs went from one 427 ms frame to a worst frame of 94 ms; steady walking runs at p95 20.6 ms. Not run on a phone.
